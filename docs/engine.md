# Engine notes (M2a)

Design record for `mtg-engine` and `mtg-testkit`. Rules of the house are in `CLAUDE.md`; the overall design is in `PLAN.md`.

## Shape
- `Engine::new/resume/apply/concede/decision/state/events/state_hash/add_continuous_effect/checkpoint/restore/preview_forecast`, plus `replay(setup, events)`.
- `Decision` kinds carry their own legal options: Mulligan, BottomCards, Priority, PayMana, DeclareAttackers, DeclareBlockers, AssignDamage, Discard. `Answer` mirrors them.
- `MulliganOptions` states the mulligan costs from `FormatRules::mulligan_rule` (`MulliganRule { hand_size, free_mulligans }`): `free_mulligans`, `bottom_if_keep` (the count the `BottomCards` decision will ask for) and `bottom_if_mulligan`. Normal is the London mulligan with no free one; a format with a free mulligan (Commander, CR 103.5c; M5) only sets `free_mulligans`. Clients repeat these counts and never work them out.
- One `Event` enum (~36 variants) and one `apply_event` fold. State is the fold of events.
- `FormatRules` trait with `Normal` implemented; Commander is not started.

## Decisions
- Random choices use a ChaCha8 substream keyed by (seed, counter in state). Events carry the results, so replay needs no RNG.
- A half-finished cast (spell chosen, payment pending) lives outside game state; a rebuilt engine asks for priority again.

- Payment (`mana.rs`, `cast.rs`): `check_payment` and `complete_payment` take the permanents that are tapped and treat a permanent as one tap, whichever of its abilities is used (`ObjectTappedTwice`, `AlreadyTapped`), and `can_afford`/`auto_payment` match coloured needs to distinct permanents, so `auto_available` stays true only when a valid payment exists. `PayChoice::Complete(partial)` validates the partial parts, requires that the chosen mana fits inside the cost (`Wasted` otherwise), and pays the rest with `auto_payment` over the unspent pool and untouched permanents (`CannotComplete` if it cannot). `PayOptions.can_cancel` is true while nothing is paid; `Cancel` then drops the pending cast with no event, so the spell never left the hand and the log shows nothing of it (a rebuilt engine from the log asks for the same priority decision); with the flag false `Cancel` is `NotOffered`. `RandomBot::new` keeps its choices (cancel when allowed, automatic, exact; roughly a third of casts are cancelled) because seeded fixtures such as the web transcript replay its games; `with_partial_payments` (`PlayOptions::partial_payments` for `play_game_with`) adds `Complete`, and `payment_fuzz.rs` plays bot games with it, checking that after a cancel the state hash, the log, the next decision and a replay all agree and that a completed payment taps the chosen permanents.

- Answers are validated before any mutation. Internal fold failures roll back by replaying the log; `checkpoint`/`restore` expose that rollback so the server can undo a step it failed to persist.
- `concede` is legal at any time (CR 104.3a): `PlayerLost { Conceded }`, then the normal elimination path (objects leave, the turn ends if it was theirs, the game ends when one player remains). A half-finished cast is dropped. The mulligan stage runs the same game-end and housekeeping check as turns.
- An effect whose duration has ended stops applying at once; the later `EffectExpired` event only prunes it.
- Invariants: per-event checks run on every event; whole-state checks and the replay hash comparison run every 25 answers and at game end.
- Fuzz: 500 bot games each for 2, 3, 4 and 6 players (`MTG_FUZZ_GAMES` overrides the count). The dev profile builds core, engine and testkit at opt-level 3 to keep this near 17 s.

## Mana costs with hybrid and Phyrexian symbols
- `ManaCost` (`mtg-core`) is a canonical list of `ManaSymbol`: `Generic(n)`, `Colored`, `Colorless`, `Hybrid(a, b)` (`{W/U}`), `MonoHybrid` (`{2/W}`), `Phyrexian` (`{W/P}`), `HybridPhyrexian` (`{G/U/P}`), `ColorlessHybrid` (`{C/W}`, MH3) and `ColorlessPhyrexian` (`{C/P}`, the two Compleated cards). The list is sorted (generic, colourless, colours, then the symbols with a choice), generic symbols are merged, `{0}` is dropped and a colour pair is stored the way cards print it (`{G/W}`, never `{W/G}`), so equal costs are equal values and `Display` then `parse` is the identity; the type is no longer `Copy`. It is the list on the wire too (TypeScript `Array<ManaSymbol>`), and deserializing normalises. `mana_value` counts `{2/W}` as 2 and every other choice as 1 (CR 202.3), `colors` gives a hybrid symbol both colours, a Phyrexian symbol its colour and `{C/W}` white (CR 105.2). `generic()` and `required(mana)` count the plain symbols only. `parse_faces` reads the card-level `A // B` text of split and adventure cards. X, snow and the silver-border symbols (`{HW}`, `{D}`, `{L}`, `{Y}`, `{Z}`) are `ParseManaCostError::Unsupported`, named, never skipped; X waits for the M3 item "X costs".
- Payment goes through resolutions (CR 601.2b): `ManaCost::resolutions()` lists every way to pay each hybrid and Phyrexian symbol as a plain cost plus the life paid (equal symbols are split among the ways, not permuted: Reaper King has 32, Boros Reckoner 4, a plain cost one, itself). `can_afford`, `auto_payment`, `check_payment` and `complete_payment` (all take the payer's life total now) try the resolutions and hand the mana to the plain-cost code that was there before, so a plain cost takes the same path and the same payments as it always did (the seeded transcript games are unchanged). A payment is valid when some resolution takes exactly its life and its mana. `auto_payment` prefers mana to life, then the least life, then the fewest taps, then taps of the colours the player has most of (a hybrid symbol keeps the scarce colour untapped); it pays life only when no mana can, and never down to 0 or less. A payment chosen by hand may take all of one's life (CR 119.4), so `can_afford` and `castable` count it and `PayOptions.auto_available` is then false. `complete_payment` keeps the life already chosen and adds life only where no mana can pay the rest and some life stays.
- `Payment.life` is the life paid, 2 for each Phyrexian symbol (`#[serde(default)]`, so an answer without it pays none); it must be even, at most 2 per symbol that life can pay (`BadLife`) and at most the life total (`NotEnoughLife`). `PayOptions` adds `life` (the payer's life total) and `max_life` (2 per such symbol, within the life total, rounded down to pairs), so a client never works out the limit. The cast's events are `LifePaid { player, amount }` first (one event for the whole payment, so it reads as one cost payment, before the spell goes on the stack), then `SpellCast`, the taps and `ManaSpent` as before. `LifePaid` folds as life lost, not damage, and is refused (`InvalidEvent`) for more than the life total; a life total of 0 or less ends the game through the usual state-based action. Everything is validated before any event.
- Tests: `mana.rs` holds tables over every symbol form and proptests that compare `can_afford`, `auto_payment`, `check_payment` and `complete_payment` with an independent symbol-by-symbol check (`pays`) over costs of every kind, permanents with several abilities and varying life totals, plus the guarantee that a plain cost is paid exactly as before. `mtg-import/tests/mana_costs.rs` reads every distinct `mana_cost` string of Scryfall's bulk data (`fixtures/scryfall_mana_costs.tsv`: 864 single-faced, 211 face and 297 `//` strings of the 2026-10-17 dump) and checks the mana value and colours against Scryfall's own; `real_data.rs` repeats it on a fresh download. In `mtg-testkit`: symbol creatures (`hybrid_bear`, `phyrexian_bear`, ...), `symbol_deck`/`symbol_fuzz_setup`, `Driver::cast_paying`/`pay_tapping`, `tests/hybrid_payment.rs` (cast with the colour, with life, hybrid either way, refused for too little life, cancelled mid-payment, completed with life) and `tests/symbol_fuzz.rs`. The bot chooses how to pay such symbols only with `RandomBot::with_symbol_choices` (`PlayOptions::symbol_choices`), so the default bot draws exactly as before; without it a cost with such a symbol is paid automatically or cancelled. The invariant checker keeps its own life history (`LifePaid` counts as well as damage).

## Priority options
`PriorityOptions` lists `playable_lands`, `castable`, `mana_abilities` and `can_pass`, and says in `can_act` whether the holder can do anything useful: a land to play, a spell to cast or an ability that is not a mana ability. `castable` already means affordable, at the timing the card allows (sorcery timing needs the active player, a main step and an empty stack; an instant waits for nothing): the floating mana plus the untapped sources the player controls, each permanent counted once (`can_afford`). Mana abilities alone never count, because tapping a land with nothing to spend the mana on is not an action, so after the last land drop with nothing affordable the holder has nothing to do, and tapping that land does not change that. One function holds the rule (`priority::can_act`); `other_abilities` beside it is the seam for abilities that are not mana abilities, empty until a card has one. The server uses the flag only to pass for a seat that asked for it (`server.md`); the response clock and the yields do not look at it, so the pace of a table never tells what a hand holds; `PlayOptions::auto_pass` in `mtg-testkit` applies the same rule in a bot game and is the reference the server tests compare with. `tests/priority_can_act.rs` is the table for the rule (empty hand, lands, land drop used, affordable and one-short creatures, two spells, colour, floating mana, summoning sickness, the opponent's turn, a busy stack) and plays bot games in which every idle holder is passed for.

## Combat forecast
`forecast::combat_forecast(&GameState) -> CombatForecast` says what combat damage would do if dealt now; it is derived from the state on every call and never stored. Per attacker it lists the damage it assigns (one packet per blocker in declared order, or one to the defender when unblocked) and the damage its blockers deal back; per creature in combat the damage it takes and whether it dies (damage marked plus expected damage against derived toughness, as in the state-based action); per attacked player the damage, the life after and whether that is lethal. Where several blockers share an attacker and the controller has not divided yet, the default is lethal damage to each blocker in turn and the rest on the last; once `DamageAssigned` is recorded the forecast uses the recorded division. `pending_combat_forecast` is the guarded form views use: it returns `None` before attackers are declared, with no attacker left, and once damage has been dealt (the combat state lingers until the end of combat, and a damage step that has granted priority is past its damage). Keywords join in `damage::strike_power` (first/double strike, power), `lethal_damage` (deathtouch) and `divide` (trample); `damage::damage_events` is the real counterpart, so change both together. A proptest in `mtg-testkit` checks forecast against reality in bot games.

### Preview of a pending answer
`Engine::preview_forecast(seat, &answer)` (`preview.rs`) is the forecast of a pending DeclareAttackers or DeclareBlockers answer without giving it: what combat damage would do if those blocks, or those attackers (unblocked), were declared. The answer goes through `answers::handle`, the same validation and the same events as `apply`, the events are folded with `apply_event` onto a clone of the state, and `pending_combat_forecast` reads the clone, so nothing is re-implemented and a preview cannot disagree with the forecast after the real answer. `PreviewError` says why there is none: `Illegal` carries the very `IllegalAnswer` `apply` would return (an illegal block is an error, never a forecast), `NotYourDecision` (the seat does not face the decision), `NotPreviewable` (any other decision kind, damage assignment included), `NoDecision` (game over), `NoCombat` (an attack with nobody) and `Engine` (the scratch copy refused the events: a bug). The method takes `&self`, so no event is logged, no random number drawn and the state hash cannot change; the cost is one state clone (5 to 8 µs at 6 seats). `crates/mtg-testkit/tests/preview_matches_games.rs` plays bot games and checks that every preview equals the forecast read after the real answer (also for answers nobody gave, tried with `checkpoint`/`restore`), that spoiled answers are refused exactly as `apply` refuses them, and that a game played with previews is the same game, log and hash, as one without; `combat_preview.rs` holds the tables (single, double and chump blocks, no blocks, lethal attacks, refusals).

## Rules checked against the Comprehensive Rules (2026-10-18)
- **Active player leaves mid-turn: engine is wrong.** The rules (multiplayer rule 800.4, the subrule about leaving during one's own turn) let the turn continue to completion without an active player: priority goes to the next player in turn order, or the top object resolves, or the step ends. The engine ends the turn at once. Fix before multiplayer Commander (see ROADMAP M5).
- Attacker divides damage freely among several blockers: correct. The Foundations update removed damage assignment order (509.2, 510.1c); lethal damage only matters for trample, which is not implemented yet.
- Damage to a planeswalker is only marked: a gap. Rule 120.3c turns it into removed loyalty counters, which need loyalty counters first.
- A mana ability resets the passes in a priority round: consistent with a literal reading of 117.4 (all players pass "without taking any actions in between"). No official ruling found; low impact, keep.

## Gaps (roadmap M3 onwards)
- No keywords, triggers, targeting or Commander, and no spell or ability effects beyond permanents entering: scry, search, tokens and the other keyword actions need the choice decisions listed in the M3 roadmap first.
- Layer dependencies (CR 613.8) are not modelled; only type, colour and power/toughness modifications work.
- No tokens or control change.
- The first-strike step exists but never occurs.
- Scenarios cannot start in the blockers or damage steps.
- The first-draw skip applies only to two-player games.
