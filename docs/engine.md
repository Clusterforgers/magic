# Engine

Design notes for `mtg-engine` (the rules engine) and `mtg-testkit` (scenarios, bots and invariants). The overall design is in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md); the wire form of its types is in [protocol.md](protocol.md).

## Shape

- `Engine` offers `new`, `resume`, `apply`, `concede`, `decision`, `state`, `events`, `state_hash`, `add_continuous_effect`, `add_continuous_effect_from`, `add_rule`, `add_rule_from`, `checkpoint`, `restore` and `preview_forecast`; `replay(setup, events)` rebuilds a state from a log.
- One `Event` enum and one `apply_event` fold: the state is the fold of the events.
- Each `Decision` kind carries its own legal options: Mulligan, BottomCards, Priority, PayMana, DeclareAttackers, DeclareBlockers, AssignDamage, Discard, and the questions of spells and abilities: ChooseTargets, ChooseMode, ChooseNumber, DivideAmount, ChooseCards, OrderCards, YesNo, ChooseOption, ChooseCosts, OrderTriggers, ChooseReplacement. `Answer` mirrors them. Each kind has its options, its answer and its check in `choose/<kind>.rs`; the announcement asks ChooseMode, ChooseCosts, ChooseNumber, ChooseTargets and DivideAmount, and ChooseNumber is also the answer to an effect that asks for a number.
- `FormatRules` is a trait with two implementations, `Normal` and `Commander` ([Commander](#commander)); both skip the starting player's first draw only in two-player games, and their maximum hand size is 7. The engine reads the values a table can override through `RuleSet` ([House rules](#house-rules)), never from the trait.
- `caps()` names the primitives the engine runs, in the vocabulary of the card support records (`cards-dsl.md`); `RUNS` in `caps.rs` is the one list. Today: the plain tap-for-mana ability and the other mana abilities that cost `{T}` and/or their source (several, counted or chosen mana, with effects that ask nothing), `Ability.Spell`, `Ability.Loyalty`, `Ability.Activated` with `Cost.Tap`, `Cost.Mana`, `Cost.SacrificeSelf`, `Cost.ExileSelf` and `Cost.DiscardSelf`, `Ability.Triggered` with the `Trigger.*` patterns (state triggers included; `GroupAttacks` and the like in `detect.rs`), `Ability.Limit` (once each turn, for triggered and activated abilities), `Ability.From` (abilities that work from a hand, a graveyard, exile, the command zone, a library or the stack), the effects Draw, Discard, Scry, Surveil, LookAtTop, Reveal, Search, Choose, Shuffle, Move (onto the battlefield too), Destroy, Exile, Sacrifice, Counter, DealDamage, GainLife, LoseLife, AddMana, PutCounters, RemoveCounters, MoveCounters, CreateToken, Continuous and `Ability.Static`, each with every modification (`Mod.*`), `Effect.Rule` and `Ability.Rule` with the rules of the game (`Rule.*`), DelayedTrigger, May, Pay and Modal, Attach and Unattach (`Keyword.Enchant`, `Keyword.Equip` and `Keyword.Reconfigure` with `Ability.Sorcery` and `Ability.Condition`), and the twelve keywords of combat and hexproof ([Combat and keywords](#combat-and-keywords)). A spell with `{X}` in its printed cost (`CardDefinition::x_in_cost`) is never castable until X costs land.
- `MulliganOptions` states the mulligan costs from the resolved mulligan rule (`MulliganRule { hand_size, free_mulligans }`): `free_mulligans`, `can_mulligan`, `bottom_if_keep` (the count the `BottomCards` decision asks for) and `bottom_if_mulligan`. A format with a free mulligan (Commander, CR 103.5c) only sets `free_mulligans`. Clients repeat these counts and never work them out.

## House rules

- `HouseRules` (`mtg-core`, `house.rs`) is what a game's creator sets in place of the format's values. Every field is an `Option` (`None` keeps the format's value, and a value equal to the format's is kept as given) except `handicaps`, a list that is empty when none is set. `validate` names the first field out of range (`HouseRuleError`; a handicap's field is `handicaps[i].starting_life`, a deck rule's `deck.deck_size`), and `validate_for(seats)` also refuses a handicap for a seat the table lacks.
- `GameSetup::house_rules` (`with_house_rules`) is checked by `GameSetup::validate` against its seat count (`SetupError::HouseRules`), copied into `GameState` and hashed beside the format name: a game under other rules has another state hash, and a replay needs the setup it was played under.
- `RuleSet` (`GameState::ruleset`, `GameSetup::ruleset`) is the format with the house rules over it and the one place the engine reads these values. The values of a seat (`starting_life`, `mulligan_rule`, `max_mulligans`, `mulligan_options`) take the seat and read its handicap first. `FormatRules` supplies what a format may change (`starting_life`, `mulligan_rule`, `skips_first_draw`, `max_hand_size`, `poison_threshold`); `None` from `max_hand_size` is unlimited, and cleanup then asks nobody to discard. `max_mulligans` is the cap, else the seat's opening hand size, and never more than that size plus the free mulligans, so a hand never bottoms more cards than it holds.
- A new rule is one field of `HouseRules` with its range in `validate`, one method of `RuleSet` and the call sites that read it.

| Rule | Range | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `starting_life` | 1 to 1000 | Life of every seat. |
| `opening_hand_size` | 1 to 20 | Cards in an opening hand. |
| `free_mulligans` | 0 to 100 | Mulligans that put no card on the bottom; 100 makes every mulligan free. |
| `mulligan_cap` | 0 to 100 | Most mulligans a seat may take; 0 forbids them. |
| `skip_first_draw` | `always`, `never` | The first player skips their first draw in every game or in none. |
| `max_hand_size` | `{cards: 0 to 50}`, `unlimited` | Cards kept at cleanup. A player with the card rule `Rule.NoMaxHandSize` has no limit whatever this is. |
| `draw_step_cards` | 1 to 10 | Cards each player draws in their draw step; a skipped first draw skips all of them. |
| `land_plays_per_turn` | 1 to 10 | Lands each player may play on their turn. |
| `mana_burn` | `true`, `false` | A player in the game loses 1 life for each mana left in their pool when a step or phase ends, restricted mana included: `ManaPoolEmptied`, `ManaBurned {player, amount}`, then the normal `LifeLost`. |
| `empty_library` | `lose`, `reshuffle_graveyard` | What a failed draw does at the next state-based check: the player loses (`EmptyLibrary`), or `LibraryRefilled` shuffles their graveyard into the library (a seeded shuffle, the order logged whole) and the cards they could not draw are drawn; a graveyard too small to make good every draw leaves the rest failed, so the player then loses. |
| `turn_limit` | 1 to 1000 rounds | The game ends at the end of the last turn of that round: the seat in the game with the highest life wins, a tie for the highest is a draw (`GameResult`), and a seat that has lost does not count. A round is one turn of each seat in play: `TurnState::round` counts from the first player's seat and opens when the turn order wraps round to or past the seat that just played, so a seat that has left takes no turn and holds no round open. The end of the turn is wound down first (damage, effects, mana burn), then `GameEnded`. |
| `winning_life` | 1 to 10000 | A player at or above it when state-based actions are checked wins: the other players in the game lose with `OpponentReachedLife`. Several at it at once are a draw (`RuleSet::game_result`). A player who loses for another reason in the same check keeps that reason. |
| `poison_threshold` | 1 to 100 | Poison counters that make a player lose; the format's 10 when unset. |
| `commander_tax` | 0 to 10 | Generic mana a commander costs more for each earlier cast from the command zone (`RuleSet::commander_tax`, read by the cost modifiers in `announce/modifiers.rs`); 0 is no tax, the format's is 2. Deaths and returns do not reset the tally. |
| `commander_damage` | `{lethal: 1 to 100}`, `off` | Combat damage from one commander that makes a player lose (`RuleSet::commander_damage`, read by `Commander::loss_condition`); the format's is `{lethal: 21}`. `off` loses nobody to it; the damage is still counted and shown. |
| `deck` | `DeckRules {singleton?, colour_identity?, deck_size?, max_copies?}` | The deck check of a format with a command zone, applied by `GameSetup::validate` through `FormatRules::validate_seat(deck, commanders, rules)`: a `RuleSet` reaches the format. `deck_size` (40 to 250) is the exact number of cards with the commanders in place of 100; `singleton: false` lifts the one-copy limit of a non-basic card (`RuleSet::copies_limit`) and `max_copies` (1 to 99) caps it instead and needs `singleton: false` (`HouseRuleError::CopiesWithSingleton`); a card whose own text allows more keeps that limit, and a basic land has none; `colour_identity: false` lets any card in. The commanders themselves are always checked. Normal ignores the deck rules. |
| `commanders_per_seat` | 1 to 2 | The most commanders a seat starts with: 1 refuses a Partner pair, 2 allows two commanders that are not partners (and a pair of partners, and one); the format's allows one or a pair of partners. More than that is `TooManyCommanders {max, got}`. |
| `handicaps` | seats `0..n`, one entry per seat | `Handicap {seat, starting_life?, opening_hand_size?}` sets those values for that seat only, in table order; an entry that sets nothing, a repeated seat or a seat beyond the table is refused. The seat's mulligan options, bottom counts and mulligan limit use its own hand size. |
| `attack_range` | `any`, `neighbours`, `left` | The players an attacker may attack, and the planeswalkers those players control, in `AttackOptions` (`RuleSet::defending_seats`): every opponent, the players next to the attacker in turn order (one with two seats left), or the next one only. They are worked out over the seats still in the game, so a seat that left changes who is next. A creature that must attack with no player to attack is not required to. |
| `open_hands` | `true`, `false` | Every seat sees every hand ([protocol.md](protocol.md), Visibility); a card of a hand is public to the table, a library is not. |
| `revealed_library_top` | `true`, `false` | Every seat sees the top card of every library once the opening shuffle is done: `GameState::revealed_top`, as for the card rule `Rule.RevealTop`. |
| `opening_hand_smoothing` | `true`, `false` | Every deal of an opening hand (the first and after a mulligan) draws two hands from the shuffled library, the top cards and the same number below them, and keeps the one whose land count is closer to the land share of the library times the hand size, the first on a tie. The rest of the library, the hand not kept in it, is shuffled with the next RNG substream and the kept hand put back on top: one `LibraryShuffled`, then `HandDealt`. A library too small for two hands is dealt as it lies. |
| `mulligan_style` | `london`, `paris`, `vancouver` | London is the format's rule: the full hand, then as many cards on the bottom as the mulligans taken beyond the free ones. Paris deals one card fewer for each such mulligan and bottoms none. Vancouver is Paris, and a seat that keeps a hand after a mulligan scries 1 (`MulliganStatus::Scrying`, the `ChooseCards` decision of the scry with the top card, answered by one `Scried`). `MulliganOptions` names `hand_if_mulligan`, `bottom_if_keep`, `bottom_if_mulligan`, `scry_if_keep` and `scry_if_mulligan`, so a client works out nothing. |

- `Rule.NoMaxHandSize` is a card rule (`Rule::NoMaxHandSize` in force through `RulesInForce`); cleanup reads it with `no_max_hand_size` beside the house limit.

## Commander

`Commander` (`format/commander.rs`, CR 903) is a `FormatRules`; the commanders and what is counted for them are in `commanders.rs` and `GameState::commanders`.

- Setup: `PlayerSetup::commanders` (`GameSetup::with_commanders`, `Scenario::commanders`) are the cards a seat starts with in its command zone, one or a Partner pair; the other cards are its deck. A format without a command zone refuses commanders (`SetupError::NoCommandZone`). `FormatRules::validate_seat(deck, commanders, rules)` checks the seat under the game's `RuleSet` and `Commander` refuses, with a `DeckError` for each rule: no commander, more than the commanders a seat may start with (two unless a house rule says one), a card that cannot be a commander (`CardDefinition::can_be_commander`: a legendary creature, a legendary Vehicle or Spacecraft with a power, or a card with `GameRule::CanBeCommander`), two that are not partners (`partners_with`: both Partner, or each names the other with Partner with), a size other than 100 with the commanders (the size of the house rules when set), a card beyond the copies its text allows (`singleton_limit`: one; none for a basic land or "any number of cards named ~"; the number of "up to N"), and a card outside the commanders' colour identity.
- Colour identity is derived from the definition, not stored: `CardDefinition::color_identity` is the union over all faces of the colour indicator, the colours of the mana cost symbols (hybrid and Phyrexian included), the symbols in the cost and mana of every ability the engine runs, the symbols in the text of the clauses it leaves to the table (reminder text excluded) and the colours of the basic land types. A corpus test holds it equal to Scryfall's `color_identity` for every corpus card.
- Rules: 40 life, the London mulligan with the first mulligan free (CR 103.5c, `free_mulligans: 1`), the first draw skipped only in a two-player game (CR 103.8a, 103.8c), `ExtraZone::Command` and the public command zone `Zone::Command(seat)`. A table may change the tax (2), the commander damage (`Some(21)`), the deck check and the commanders per seat ([House rules](#house-rules)); the engine reads them through `RuleSet`.
- A commander is an object id in `GameState::commanders`, fixed at setup; the id stays through every zone change, so what is counted for a commander survives them. A token and a copy are other objects and no commander. A game with commanders looks for replacement effects and cost modifiers from its start (`Possible`), and the commanders, casts, damage and refusals are hashed only once they hold something.
- Casting: `ways::of` offers a commander in its owner's command zone for its mana cost at the timing of its type. The tax is a cost increase added with the other increases in `announce/modifiers.rs` before the reductions: `commander_casts` times `RuleSet::commander_tax` generic mana, counted by the fold of `SpellCast` when the spell lay in a command zone (not for a cast from a hand), so a free cast pays it too.
- Back to the command zone (CR 903.9): a commander in a graveyard or exile is a question of the state-based actions in `flow.rs` after the legend rule: its owner is asked `YesNo` "Put X into the command zone?" (the first owner in turn order from the active player first), yes is a `ZoneChanged` to `Zone::Command` and no is `CommanderStayed`, which holds until the commander moves again. A commander that would be put into a hand or a library is a replacement candidate of `replace.rs` (`Origin::Commander`, ordered with the other candidates), asked `YesNo` in the replacement step; a no leaves the move as it was. The commander that returns is a new object (`counter` plus one). A move sent elsewhere is taken out of the `LibraryBottomed` and `LibraryShuffled` cards of its batch.
- Commander damage (CR 903.10a): the fold of `DamageDealt` to a player counts the amount per (commander, player) when the damage steps are dealing it and nothing is resolving (`replace::in_combat_damage`). `Commander::loss_condition` loses a player at `RuleSet::commander_damage` damage from one commander (`LossReason::CommanderDamage`), so two commanders, and the two commanders of a Partner pair, are counted apart.
- The state is public: `PlayerView.commanders` (the seat's commanders with their casts and tax) and `PlayerView.commander_damage` ([protocol.md](protocol.md)).

## Determinism and recovery

- Random choices use a ChaCha8 substream keyed by (seed, counter in state). Events carry the results, so replay needs no RNG.
- A half-finished cast (spell chosen, payment pending) lives outside game state; a rebuilt engine asks for priority again.
- Answers are validated before any mutation. A fold failure rolls back by replaying the log, and `checkpoint`/`restore` expose that rollback so the server can undo a step it failed to persist.
- `concede` is legal at any time (CR 104.3a): `PlayerLost { Conceded }`, then the normal elimination path (the player's objects leave, the effects that gave them control end, what they still control is exiled, the game ends when one player remains). A half-finished cast is dropped. The mulligan stage runs the same game-end and housekeeping check as turns.
- A turn whose active player has left goes on without one (CR 800.4): `GameState::active_player` is `None` while `TurnState::active` keeps the seat the turn belongs to. Untap, draw, attack declaration and the hand-size discard are skipped; every step that grants priority starts with the next player in turn order (`active_or_next`), where the stack resolves and the steps end as in any turn; the beginning of a step triggers what names every player's step, with no player to refer to, and nothing that names the active player's or an opponent's step; cleanup ends the effects until end of turn, and the next turn goes to the next player in turn order. A game of two ends at once.
- An effect whose duration has ended stops applying at once; the later `EffectExpired` event only prunes it.

## Mana costs

- `ManaCost` (`mtg-core`) is a canonical list of `ManaSymbol`: `Generic`, `Colored`, `Colorless`, `Hybrid` (`{W/U}`), `MonoHybrid` (`{2/W}`), `Phyrexian` (`{W/P}`), `HybridPhyrexian` (`{G/U/P}`), `ColorlessHybrid` (`{C/W}`) and `ColorlessPhyrexian` (`{C/P}`).
  - Canonical means sorted (generic, colourless, colours, then the symbols with a choice), generic symbols merged, `{0}` dropped and a colour pair stored the way cards print it (`{G/W}`, never `{W/G}`). Equal costs are equal values, and `Display` then `parse` is the identity.
  - The list is also the wire form (`Array<ManaSymbol>`); deserializing normalizes.
- `mana_value` counts `{2/W}` as 2 and every other choice as 1 (CR 202.3). `colors` gives a hybrid symbol both colours, a Phyrexian symbol its colour and `{C/W}` white (CR 105.2). `generic()` and `required(mana)` count the plain symbols only.
- `parse_faces` reads the card-level `A // B` text of split and adventure cards.
- Any other symbol (X, snow, the silver-border symbols) is `ParseManaCostError::Unsupported`, named and never skipped.

## Payment

- Payment goes through resolutions (CR 601.2b). `ManaCost::resolutions()` lists every way to pay each hybrid and Phyrexian symbol as a plain cost plus the life paid. Equal symbols are split among the ways, not permuted: Reaper King has 32, Boros Reckoner 4, a plain cost one.
- `can_afford`, `auto_payment`, `check_payment` and `complete_payment` take the payer's life total and try the resolutions, handing each to the plain-cost code. A payment is valid when some resolution takes exactly its life and its mana.
- A permanent is one tap, whichever of its abilities is used. `can_afford` and `auto_payment` match coloured needs to distinct permanents, so `auto_available` is true only when a valid payment exists.
- `auto_payment` prefers mana to life, then the least life, then the fewest taps, then taps of the colours the player has most of, so a hybrid symbol keeps the scarce colour untapped. It pays life only when no mana can, and never down to 0 or less.
- A payment chosen by hand may take all of one's life (CR 119.4), so `can_afford` and `castable` count it and `PayOptions.auto_available` is then false.
- A source adds `count` mana for one tap (`mana_plan.rs`), and mana beyond the cost floats. A payment covers the cost and has nothing to spare: no floating mana, tap or part of one can be left out and still cover it (`spent_of`; one made of single mana is the cost's total exactly). `ManaSpent` lists the cost's mana and the surplus stays in the pool.
- With sources of several mana, sacrificed or doing more than adding mana, `auto_payment` uses floating mana first, then the sources with the fewest sacrifices, then the fewest that do more than add mana, then the fewest taps (`plan`): a Treasure is sacrificed only when nothing else can pay and a painland's colour only when it is needed. `complete_payment` then searches the completions that leave nothing to spare (`complete_with`, within a budget of nodes).
- `ManaOption.count`, `extra` and `sacrifice` are left out of the wire form while one, false and false.

- Restricted mana ("Spend this mana only to cast creature spells", `SpendOn` in `mtg-cards`) stays in `ManaPool` with what it allows (`Restricted`, `spend.rs`) until the pool empties, and a source of it is a `ManaOption` with `only`. `Spend` says what a payment is for: a spell, an ability or neither (the cost of an effect), the types, supertypes, subtypes and number of colors of the spell or of the ability's source, and whether the cost has {X}. `can_afford`, `auto_payment`, `check_payment` and `complete_payment` take it, read the restricted mana and the sources it allows as plain mana and leave the rest out, so the four agree; `spend_tests.rs` compares them with a check of every payment.
- A payment names restricted mana in `Payment.restricted` beside `pool`, which is mana without a restriction; it is refused when the pool does not hold the mana or `Spend` forbids it (`PaymentError::Restricted`). Restricted mana is spent first: among equal payments `auto_payment` and `complete_payment` take the one that spends more of it, taps sources of it before others and, for hybrid and Phyrexian symbols, chooses the way that spends the most. `ManaAdded.only` and `ManaSpent.restricted` carry it, and `ManaSpent.mana` lists all the mana spent.
- A payment for an effect (`Pay`) is for neither a spell nor an ability, so only mana without a restriction pays it. `PayOptions.spend`, `ManaOption.only`, `ManaPool.restricted`, `Payment.restricted`, `ManaAdded.only` and `ManaSpent.restricted` are left out of the wire form while empty.
- Convoke (CR 702.51) and delve (CR 702.66) help pay after the total cost is fixed (`help.rs`). `PayOptions.convoke` lists the untapped creatures the payer controls, summoning sick ones included, with the colours each is, and `PayOptions.delve` the cards of their graveyard (not the spell itself). `Payment.convoke` names the creatures tapped, each paying {1} (`pays` empty) or one mana of a colour it is, and `Payment.delve` the cards exiled, each paying {1}. What they pay is taken out of the plain cost, after the cost modifiers and the choice for each hybrid and Phyrexian symbol, and mana pays the rest, so `Spend` and restricted mana read the rest as before. A colour pays that colour and then generic mana, like any mana; a card pays generic mana only. The check refuses a creature that is not offered, is tapped, is named twice, is also tapped for mana or pays a colour it is not, a card that is not in the graveyard or is named twice, and helpers that pay more than the cost has (`PaymentError`). `can_afford_with`, `auto_payment_with`, `check_payment_with` and `complete_payment_with` take the offer (`Help`); the plain names are the same with none. An automatic payment uses helpers only when mana cannot pay, then the fewest, creatures before cards; a completion keeps the helpers named and adds more only when it must. A spell is offered when the sum of mana and helpers pays its modified cost (`ways.rs`, `announce/total.rs`). The events are the tap of each creature and the exile of each card from the graveyard, between `SpellCast` and the mana taps. `help_tests.rs` compares the four functions with a check of every combination.
- `PayChoice::Complete(partial)` validates the partial parts and requires that the chosen mana fits inside the cost. It pays the rest with `auto_payment` over the unspent pool and untouched permanents (refused if it cannot), keeps the life already chosen and adds life only where no mana can pay the rest and some life stays.
- `PayOptions.can_cancel` is true while nothing is paid. `Cancel` then drops the pending cast with no event: the spell never left the hand and the log shows nothing of it, so a rebuilt engine asks for the same priority decision. With the flag false, `Cancel` is refused.
- `Payment.life` is the life paid, 2 per Phyrexian symbol (`#[serde(default)]`, so an answer without it pays none). It must be even, at most 2 per symbol that life can pay and at most the life total.
- `PayOptions.life` (the payer's life total) and `max_life` (2 per such symbol, within the life total, rounded down to pairs) mean a client never works out the limit.
- The cast's events are `LifePaid { player, amount }` first, one event for the whole payment, before the spell goes on the stack; then `SpellCast`, the taps and `ManaSpent`. `LifePaid` folds as life lost, not damage, and is refused for more than the life total. A life total of 0 or less ends the game through the usual state-based action. Everything is validated before any event.

## Priority options

`PriorityOptions` lists `playable_lands`, `castable`, `mana_abilities`, `abilities` and `can_pass`, and says in `can_act` whether the holder can do anything useful. `abilities` is left out of the wire form while empty.

- `castable` lists cards in a hand and, for a way that names another zone, in a graveyard, exile, the top of a library or the command zone (see Announcement, Ways to cast); it means affordable at the timing the card allows: sorcery timing needs the active player, a main step and an empty stack; an instant, or a card a keyword gives instant timing, waits for nothing. Affordable counts the floating mana plus the untapped sources the player controls, each permanent once (`can_afford`). A spell that needs targets is castable only when every target specification has enough legal targets (CR 601.2c).
- `abilities` lists the activated abilities of the player's permanents, and of the cards they own in their hand, their graveyard, exile and their command zone, that work from the zone the card is in, are not mana abilities and could be activated now (`priority::other_abilities`; the card's zone is the zone of its object in the views): the engine pays `{T}` and mana for one (the mana from the floating pool and the untapped sources the player controls, other than the permanent itself when its `{T}` is part of the cost), a tapped permanent or a summoning-sick creature cannot, `Timing::Sorcery` waits for sorcery timing, and each target specification needs enough legal targets.
- `suspendable` lists the cards in the hand that could be suspended now and `foretellable` those that could be foretold (special actions, see Announcement, Madness and suspend, Foretell). Both are left out of the wire form while empty.
- `can_act` is true with a land to play, a spell to cast, a card to suspend or foretell or such an ability. Mana abilities alone never count: tapping a land with nothing to spend the mana on is not an action, so after the last land drop with nothing affordable the holder has nothing to do.
- One function holds the rule (`priority::can_act`).
- The server uses the flag only to pass for a seat that asked for it ([server.md](server.md)). The response clock and the yields never read it, so the pace of a table never tells what a hand holds.
- `PlayOptions::auto_pass` in `mtg-testkit` applies the same rule in a bot game and is the reference the server tests compare with.

## Manual actions

A table plays any card, also one the engine runs only in part, with its controller doing by hand what the engine does not (`manual.rs`; [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md), Compiler and Manual fallback).

- A card the engine does not run is cast and resolves without the clauses it leaves out: a spell goes to the graveyard, a permanent enters with the abilities the engine does run. Its definition lists the left-out clauses as `CardDefinition.manual` (`ManualClause {text, reason}`, one list for each face), which every seat reads.
- `PriorityAction::Manual(ManualAction)` is the seam: the seat that has priority takes one at any time, so it needs no entry in `PriorityOptions`, and `PriorityAction` is no longer `Copy`. `manual::check(state, seat, &action)` says whether an action is allowed and why not (`ManualRefusal`, carried by `IllegalAnswer::Manual`); a refusal leaves the log, the state and the question as they were.
- Actions: `Move` (a card or token the seat owns or controls, from the battlefield, hand, graveyard or exile, to its owner's hand, graveyard, library top or bottom or command zone, to exile or onto the battlefield; a token that leaves the battlefield ceases to exist; the stack and the cards of a library cannot be named), `MoveTop` (the top 1 to 10 cards of the seat's own library to its hand, graveyard, exile or bottom), `Counters` (on any permanent), `PlayerCounters` and `Life` (the seat's own), `CreateToken` (1 to 10 under the seat's control, from `CommonToken::ALL` or a `CustomToken` with a name, colours, creature, artifact, enchantment or land types, subtypes and, for a creature, power and toughness), `Tap` and `Untap` (any permanent), `Draw`, `Discard` (a card of the seat's hand), `Attach` and `Unattach` (an Aura, Equipment or Fortification of the seat). An opponent's card, life and counters are changed by the opponent's own seat. A rule in force that keeps a seat from drawing or gaining life refuses that action. The command zone is a destination only in a format that has one.
- Each action is `Event::ManualAction {player, action}` followed by the events of the change in one batch (`ZoneChanged` with `ZoneCause::Moved`, `LibraryBottomed`, `CountersChanged`, `LifeGained`, `LifeLost`, `TokenCreated`, `PermanentTapped`, `CardDrawn`, `CardDiscarded`, `Attached`), built from the same events the effects use, so the replacement step, trigger detection and state-based actions treat them as any other: a creature put into a graveyard dies and its triggers fire, an arrival triggers what watches arrivals, a draw and a discard trigger what watches them, zero life or ten poison counters lose the game. A move by hand is no draw and no discard. The action itself changes nothing but the round (`reset_passes`): the seat keeps priority and the others pass again.
- An Aura with a clause left to the table waits unattached on the battlefield (`attach::state_based` skips it) until its controller attaches it by hand.
- The bots never take a manual action. `mtg-testkit` has `ManualScript` (candidates drawn from the state and kept when `manual::check` allows them), `PlayOptions::manual_every` (one priority in n gets a scripted action), `Driver::manual`, the cards of `manual_cards.rs` and `manual_fuzz_setup` (decks of the corpus cards that are not Verified and cards of its own). The invariants hold for every action; the one assumption they change is that an Aura on the battlefield is attached (`check_attachments_settled` lets a card with manual clauses wait).

## Spells and abilities

The rules of the pipeline are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Execution); this section says where the engine keeps them.

- Objects: a `GameObject` has a `kind` (`Card`, `Token`, `Ability`) and a zone-change counter the fold bumps on every move (CR 400.7); `ObjectRef { id, counter }` names one incarnation. An ability on the stack is a `GameObject` that holds an `AbilityObject` (`abilities.rs`): its source, a snapshot of it, its index among the source's rules, an `Arc` to those rules and a trigger's cause. It has no printed side and is dropped when it leaves the stack.
- Reading abilities: `ability_reader` (`ability_reader.rs`) is the one place that reads the abilities of an object. `abilities_of(state, id)` lists them with their addresses in order and `ability(state, address)` reads the one an address names; priority options, trigger detection, casting, resolution, the folds of activations, triggers and abilities put on the stack, and `keywords_of` (through `keyword_abilities`, the keywords among them) all ask there, and a test searches the engine sources for a read of a card's `rules` outside the reader and of its plain mana abilities (`abilities`) outside `characteristics`. An address is `AbilityRef { object, ability, source }`: with `AbilitySource::Printed`, the default that JSON and TypeScript leave out so an address reads and writes as `{ object, ability }`, the index is the ability's index among its card's rules, which is also how the log names an ability (`Triggered`, `PutOnStack`, `AbilityActivated`). A permanent has the abilities printed on its card less those other effects took away (`LoseAbilities`, a keyword taken away), then those they gave it in the order the layers applied them, each under `AbilitySource::Granted(Grant)` with index 0: a `Grant` is a continuous effect (`Effect(EffectId)`) or one modification of a static ability (`Static { source, rule, index }`). A keyword that is built from triggers (ward, prowess, battle cry) adds the abilities of its `adds_abilities` right after itself, or after the grant that gave it: `AbilitySource::PrintedKeyword(rule)` or `GrantedKeyword(Grant)` with the ability's index among those the keyword adds. Each instance of a keyword adds its own (CR 702.21c, 702.91b, 702.108b), and an effect that takes the keyword away, or every ability, takes what it added. Any other object has the printed abilities, the very `Arc`s its card holds, wherever it is: whether one works from a zone is for the caller to ask. `ability` reads an address from the data, so a granted ability stays readable after its effect has ended or the object that gives it has left, which an ability on its way needs (a cost sacrificed its source, a creature died with the trigger it was given); `abilities_of` says whether the object has it now. `Triggered`, `PutOnStack`, `AbilityActivated` and `TriggerRemoved` carry the address's `origin` (left out of the JSON while printed), the keys of `limits_spent` and the identity of a waiting trigger hold it, and two abilities given to one creature by two statics are two triggers its controller orders. A token that has ceased to exist keeps the abilities it was printed with (CR 113.7a, `GameState::source_object`); an ability on the stack and an object that is not in the game have none, and an address that names none reads as `None`. A pending trigger, a delayed trigger and an ability on the stack hold the ability itself, so each runs as it was whatever becomes of the object it came from (CR 603.10a, 113.7a).
- Counters and attachments: a permanent holds its counters by kind and the `ObjectRef` of the permanent it is attached to (`PermanentState`). `GameState::host_of` names that host only while it is still the very object on the battlefield (CR 400.7), `attachments_of` and `attachment_index` are its inverse, and `Snapshot` carries both. A starting permanent gets them from `PresetPermanent` (`attached_to` is a seat and a place in its battlefield; `SetupError::BadAttachment` refuses a place that holds nothing), and a starting player gets counters from `PlayerSetup::counters`.
- Counters: `CountersChanged { on, counter, delta }` (`counters.rs`) puts counters on a permanent or a player and takes them off. The fold refuses a change of nothing, a removal of more counters than there are, an object that is not a permanent on the battlefield or no longer the very object named (CR 400.7) and a player who is out; a kind that falls to none is no longer listed, so the counters of a permanent (`PermanentState::counters_of`) and of a player (`Player::counters_of`) are always the whole story. A permanent's counters go with its `PermanentState` when it leaves, so one that comes back holds none. `GameState::possible.counters` is set when a +1/+1 or -1/-1 counter is first put on a permanent (or starts on one), and only then do the layers look at counters.
- Snapshots: `snapshot(state, id)` (`snapshot.rs`) is last known information. `PutOnStack`, `Cause` and `StepDone` carry them, and the value evaluator reads an object live while its counter matches and from the snapshot otherwise.
- Casting: `announce/` is the one procedure of a cast or activation and `cast.rs` the payment and the events it produces (Announcement, below). An activation that costs nothing or only `{T}` commits when its last question is answered; one with mana asks the payment last.
- Targets: `legal_targets(state, spec, source, controller)` (`choose/targets.rs`) serves choosing, `priority_options` and the recheck on resolution, and asks the keyword hook `protects`. An answer is checked against the options it answers and each target is chosen once.
- Resolution: `stack::resolve_top` begins the top object of the stack. While `GameState.resolving` is set, `flow::next_in_turns` takes the next move from `resolve::step` before state-based actions and priority (CR 704.3), so a resolution is never cut by a priority round. A step is a pure function of the state and its journal; the events it returns end with `StepDone`, which clears the journal.
- Rebuilding: the journal is state, so an engine resumed from a log stands at the same step, and one resumed from the middle of a step asks the same question again.
- Choosing cards: an effect that makes a player take cards (`Discard`, `Sacrifice`) lists a `Chooser` per player in turn order and calls `pick_cards` (`choose/cards.rs`). A player who takes nothing, or has no more cards than they take, takes them all and is not asked, so an empty hand or no matching permanent is no decision and no event. Any other is asked `ChooseCards { source, from, min, max, text }` (min and max both the number taken) addressed to that seat, which need not be the controller; the answer is checked against the options (`NotOffered`, `Duplicate`, `WrongCount`, all named "card"), logged as `ChoiceMade` and read back from the journal when the step runs again. Everyone chooses before anything moves, then the step's events follow together.
- Modes: `announce` asks `ChooseMode` first for a modal spell or ability (`Effect::Modal` is the only effect of its ability, `choose/mode.rs`); a mode with no label (a compiled one) is offered under the oracle wording the compiler's renderer gives its effects, with `~` as the card's name. A mode is offered only when each of its target specifications has enough legal targets; with fewer legal modes than the fewest to choose, a spell is not castable (`announceable`) and a trigger is removed (CR 700.2a, 603.3c). The answer is checked against the modes offered (`NotOffered`, `Duplicate` unless the mode repeats, `WrongCount`; `Cancel` only with `can_cancel`) and kept in printed order in `Choices.modes`. The targets of the modes chosen are then asked mode after mode: `Choices.targets` follows that flat list, and inside a mode `Target(0)` is the mode's own first (`Scope.target_base`). Resolution enters the modes chosen in order; the branches of a `Modal` in `resolve/tree.rs` are the modes chosen, so a mode chosen twice happens twice.
- Yes or no and options: `May` asks `YesNo` of the players it names (the controller by default) in turn order until one says yes (`pick_yes_no`) and then enters its effects or skips them; the question reads as the effects do ("Draw a card?"). `Choose` binds the players (`ChooseOption`, `pick_options`; a player with one option is not asked) or objects (`ChooseCards`, `pick_selections`, between `min` and `max`) it chose to `as` and the rest to `rest_as`. An answer is checked (`NotOffered("option")` for an index past the list), logged as `ChoiceMade` and read back from the journal.
- Paying in an effect: `Pay` asks `YesNo` ("Pay {2}?", "Pay 2 life?", "Discard a card?", "Pay {1}, pay {E}{E}?") of the players it names, in turn order until one pays, then a `ChooseCards` for each part that picks among more objects than it takes (`can_cancel` false), then the `PayMana` decision of that player when the cost has mana (`cast::payment_decision`: the cost, the floating mana, the untapped sources other than the ones picked, `can_cancel` false). The cost may hold mana, life, energy, counters removed from the source and objects picked, paid with the events of an ability's cost (`LifePaid`, `CountersChanged`, `ZoneChanged`, `CardDiscarded`, `PermanentTapped`) before the `paid` effects follow. A player who cannot pay is one for whom `Price::claimed` finds no whole cost. A player who left the game is not asked. A player who cannot pay (`can_afford`: the mana, the life and the sources, whatever the cost) is asked the same question at the same moment with `YesNoOptions::only_no` set, so the table and the response clock do not show whether they could pay: a Yes is refused (`NotOffered("yes")`) before anything is logged, and the clock and the bots answer No. A shock land asks a player with too little life for its two the same way. The answer is checked like the payment of a spell and logged as `ChoiceMade { YesNo(true) }` with `LifePaid`, the taps, `ManaAdded` and `ManaSpent` (`cast::announcement`), so the journal holds a no, or a yes and a yes for the payment; the step does not read the entries of a player who has left. The `paid` effects follow a payment and the `unpaid` ones follow when nobody paid. A cost that loses the source, taps it, has an `{X}` or counts X is refused by name (`Effect.Pay.SacrificeSelf`, `Cost.Mana.X`, `Effect.Pay.X`): no announcement chooses an X in the middle of a resolution. Ward is a `Pay` of its cost, so ward with life, energy, a discard or a sacrifice works as any payment does.
- Paying for an ability: `Price` (`price.rs`) is the `{T}`, the loss of the source, the mana and the other parts (`parts`) of an activated ability's cost, and names the first part the engine does not pay. With mana, `announce` asks `PayMana` after the modes and targets (`can_cancel` true, nothing logged until it is paid). Its events are `LifePaid`, the tap and the sacrifice of the cost, `AbilityActivated`, `AbilityPutOnStack` (the source photographed before any cost), then the taps for mana and `ManaSpent`. The permanent whose `{T}` is paid, or that is sacrificed, is not one of the sources of its own mana.
- Discarding and sacrificing: a discard is `CardDiscarded` per card, a sacrifice `ZoneChanged { cause: Sacrificed }` to the owner's graveyard, so triggers on discards, sacrifices, deaths and leaving the battlefield see them (the sacrifice is judged against the state before it). A sacrifice carries a snapshot of each permanent in `StepDone`. The questions read "Discard a card." and "Sacrifice a creature or planeswalker." (`wording.rs`). A discard at random is the primitive `Effect.Discard.Random`, which the engine does not run.
- Library effects: libraries are listed bottom to top, and every list of cards in an event or an answer reads from the top down. A step that asks one kind of question of several players reads the journal through `pick_selections`; one that mixes questions (a scry asks for cards and then for orders) walks them in one fixed order through `Questions` (`choose/ask.rs`), reading the answers already in the journal and stopping at the first one missing.
  - `LookAtTop` is `LibraryLooked` by the controller for each library named and binds the cards seen, top first; only that seat learns them. `Reveal` is `Revealed`, which every seat sees.
  - `Scry` always asks the player which of the cards looked at go to the bottom (`ChooseCards`, none to all), then the order of the cards that stay and of those that go down (`OrderCards`, only for two or more). It logs one `Scried`, whose fold puts both lists back: the first card of `top` ends up on top, the last of `bottom` at the bottom. A scry of nothing or of an empty library is no event.
  - `Surveil` is the same walk with the graveyard in place of the bottom: `ChooseCards` for the cards that go into the graveyard (none to all), then one `OrderCards` for the cards that stay on top (two or more). It logs one `Surveilled { player, top, graveyard }` for each player, whose fold puts the cards that stay on top and the others just beneath them, and then a `ZoneChanged` with `ZoneCause::Moved` (not `Milled`) for each card put into the graveyard, so replacement effects and triggers see every move. All the players decide before any library changes; a surveil of nothing or of an empty library is no event (CR 701.25).
  - `Move` sends objects to their owner's hand or graveyard (`ZoneChanged`) or into their owner's library: on top by `ZoneChanged` in reverse order, on the bottom by `ZoneChanged` and then `LibraryBottomed` (a card already in the library only needs the second), shuffled in by `ZoneChanged` and a `LibraryShuffled`. Several cards for one library are put in the order the controller chooses (`OrderCards`). A random order on the bottom is drawn from the next substream of the generator (`shuffled`) and logged as the order of `LibraryBottomed { random: true }`, whose fold moves the substream on as a shuffle does; a random order on top is the primitive `Effect.Move.Random.Top`, which the engine does not run.
  - `Move` onto the battlefield is one `ZoneChanged` per card, so what triggers on entering sees it, and an `Entered { object, tapped, controller }` after it when the card enters tapped or under a player other than its owner (`under` names that player; the fold sets both on the new permanent). An instant or a sorcery card and a permanent already there stay (CR 304.4, 307.4). A `Bound` name that named the cards is bound again, in the same `StepDone`, to the permanents that arrived (the fold lets a name be bound a second time only to the same objects after a zone change), so "that creature" in the steps after the move is the one that entered: Sneak Attack gives it haste and sacrifices it. The replacement step changes the entry (tapped, counters, a choice made).
  - `Search` goes through the zone of each searching player in turn order (a library unless the effect names another) and offers the searcher alone the cards that match the filter, listed by id so the list says nothing of the library's order (`ChooseCards`, "Search your library for a basic land card."). In a library or a hand the fewest to find is 0 (CR 701.23b); in a public zone it is `count.min`, and a zone that holds no more than that gives them up without a question. No match is no question. Every search logs a `CardsFound` for the searcher (private to them; other seats see how many), also when nothing was found, and a `Revealed` after it when the effect reveals the cards found; the cards found are bound to `as`.
  - What a seat may identify beyond the zones it sees into: the cards a resolution showed it and the cards it is asked about. `Resolving.shown` records the cards of `LibraryLooked`, `CardsFound`, `Scried` and `Surveilled` for the seat that did them and those of `Revealed` for every seat, as the events fold; an entry holds while the resolution lasts and only for the object it names (a card that changed zones is another object, CR 400.7). `sight::offered_cards(state)` reads the question the resolution is waiting on from its step (`ChooseCards` or `OrderCards`) and names the seat and the cards of that question; the seat may identify them in any zone. Both are functions of the state alone, so a state rebuilt from the log gives the same views.
  - `Shuffle` logs `LibraryShuffled` with the whole new order from `shuffled` (`mulligan.rs`), a Fisher-Yates over the seeded substream `rng_index` plus the position of the shuffle in its batch; folding the event moves `rng_index` on, so a replay reads the order and draws nothing.
  - An `OrderCards` answer lists every card offered once and no other (`NotOffered`, `Duplicate`, `WrongCount`, all named "card"). A choice with more than one possible answer is always asked; an order of fewer than two cards, and a choice that must take everything, are not.
- Destroying, exiling and countering: `resolve/prim/{destroy,exile,counter}.rs` name their objects with `Scope::live_objects` (the objects a reference names that are still the objects it named, each once) and propose one move per object, all in one step. A destroyed permanent goes to its owner's graveyard as `ZoneChanged { cause: Destroyed }` unless `keywords::is_indestructible` says otherwise; an exiled object leaves any zone as `ZoneChanged { cause: Exiled }`; a countered spell or ability ends as `ResolutionEnded { Countered }` for an object that is not the one resolving, which the fold sends to its owner's graveyard (an ability ceases to exist). Detection reads each as a move: a death or leaving the battlefield against the state before the batch, so permanents that go together see each other go, and a counter as a move from the stack with `ZoneCause::Countered`. State-based actions and the triggers they leave come after the resolution, as for any spell. Each step carries a snapshot of what it moved, and `Scope.departed` (the snapshots of the resolution) keeps a target that an earlier step moved named for the steps after it, so Swords to Plowshares reads the creature's power and controller as they were.
- Putting, removing and moving counters: `resolve/prim/{put_counters,remove_counters,move_counters}.rs` name their holders with `put_counters::holders` (the players still in the game and the permanents on the battlefield that are still the objects named, each once, in the order the reference lists them) and propose one `CountersChanged` for each, all in one step. `RemoveCounters` takes at most what a holder has and `MoveCounters` takes from the first permanent `from` names and gives to the first `to` names (nothing when either is missing, when they are one permanent or when the first holds none). `Scope::amount` reads `Amount::Counters` as the sum over what its reference names, a permanent live while it is the object named and from its snapshot after it has left, and `Pred::HasCounter` reads the same way (`Seen::counters`).
- Planeswalkers (`loyalty.rs`): loyalty is the loyalty counters of a permanent (CR 306.5c) and the printed number elsewhere (`Stat::Loyalty` in `Scope`, live or from a snapshot). A planeswalker enters with its printed loyalty as a `CountersChanged` proposed with the events that bring it in, a `SpellResolved` (`stack::resolved`) or a `Move` onto the battlefield, never in the fold of the zone change, so the replacement step can reach it; a starting permanent of a `GameSetup` has the printed number unless it lists loyalty counters, and a token gets none. A loyalty ability is offered in `PriorityOptions.abilities` to the active player in a main step with an empty stack, once for each planeswalker each turn (`GameState::loyalty_used`, set by the fold of `AbilityActivated`, cleared by `TurnBegan`, named by the object and its zone-change counter), and a cost that removes counters only from a planeswalker that holds that many (CR 606.3, 606.6). Activation pays the cost first, as a `CountersChanged` before `AbilityActivated`; the ability keeps a snapshot of its source as it stands once the cost is paid and resolves without it (CR 113.7a). A `-X` cost asks X as the ability is announced, from 0 to the loyalty counters, and removes that many in the cost (`loyalty::paid_change`); it is offered whenever the other conditions hold, since X = 0 is a number. A planeswalker with no loyalty counters goes to its owner's graveyard before anyone receives priority, as `ZoneChanged { cause: StateBased }` (CR 704.5i). The fold of `DamageDealt` takes that many loyalty counters off a planeswalker, no more than it holds, and marks no damage on it unless it is a creature too, which takes both (CR 120.3c); lifelink and triggers see all the damage dealt.
- Attacking planeswalkers: only the opponents of the controller attack a planeswalker. One that leaves the battlefield leaves its attackers in combat attacking nothing (`Attacker::attacks_nothing`, CR 506.4c): they are not redirected, have no defending player to block them (`Attacker::defender`), deal no combat damage to what they attacked and, with trample, assign all their damage to their blockers; an attacker that was blocked still deals damage to its blockers.
- Attachments (`attach.rs`): `Attached { object, to }` sets `attached_to` and gives the attachment the next timestamp (CR 613.7a), `Unattached { object }` clears it; the fold refuses an attachment to itself, to something off the battlefield and to the host it has. An Aura, Equipment or Fortification is told by its subtype (`kind_of`) and `legal_host` says where it may be: an Aura that is not a creature on what all its `Enchant` keywords allow (CR 303.4d, 303.4e, 702.5d), an Equipment that is not a creature on another creature (CR 301.5c), a Fortification on another land (CR 301.6).
  - An Aura spell targets through `Enchant` (CR 303.4a): `spell_target` builds its one `TargetSpec` from the keywords, `announce` asks it in place of any target of the spell, and the spell is offered as castable only with a legal target (`attach::announceable`). `stack::resolve_top` rechecks the target with the rule that serves choosing, hexproof included: an Aura whose target is gone does not enter and ends as `ResolutionEnded { Fizzled }` in its owner's graveyard (CR 608.2b); one that resolves is `SpellResolved` and `Attached` in the same batch (CR 303.4f).
  - An Aura that `Effect::Move` puts onto the battlefield chooses its host among the permanents its keywords allow (`hosts_when_entering`, no targeting: a hexproof creature is a host): `ChooseCards` of its controller, asked only when there are two or more, and the `Attached` follows its `ZoneChanged` in the batch (CR 303.4g). With none it stays where it was.
  - `Effect::Attach` moves each permanent `what` names onto the first permanent `to` names when `legal_host` says so; one that may not be there stays (CR 701.3b) and one already there is not attached again (CR 701.3c). `Ref::Attached` names the host of the source while it is the object it was attached to.
  - State-based actions (`attach::state_based`, part of the pass of `sba::check`, skipped while no card of the game is an attachment): an Aura attached to nothing or to what it may not enchant goes to its owner's graveyard (`ZoneChanged` with `StateBased`, CR 704.5m), an Equipment or Fortification attached to what it may not be becomes unattached (CR 704.5n), and any other permanent attached to something becomes unattached (CR 704.5p). A host that leaves is a new object if it returns (CR 400.7), so `host_of` stops naming it at once and its Auras go to the graveyard and its Equipment comes off in the next pass.
- The legend rule (`legend.rs`, CR 704.5j): a player who controls two or more legendary permanents with one name (the printed supertype, the control the layers give) is asked `ChooseCards` for the one to keep (`min` and `max` are 1, the source is the oldest, the text "Legend rule: choose the Name to keep."), after the other state-based actions of the pass and before triggers. Nothing is resolving and nothing is announced then, so `answers::handle` sends the answer to `legend::answer`, which puts every other permanent of the group into its owner's graveyard in one batch of `ZoneChanged` with `StateBased`: put, not destroyed. Groups are asked in turn order from the active player and then by name, one question each; a rebuilt engine asks the same one. The rule is skipped while no card of the game is legendary.
- Life of players: `Amount::Players` counts the players `Scope::players` names, and `LowestLife` and `HighestLife` take the least and the greatest `life()` among them (`i32::MAX` and `i32::MIN` for none), so a static ability with `LowestLife(Opponents) <= 10` (Bloodghast's haste) follows the opponents still in the game as their totals change; `layers/reads.rs` reads their reference like any other.
- Conditions, tapping, milling and winning: `If` reads its condition with `Scope::holds` in the state the earlier steps left and enters `then` or `otherwise` by position (`resolve/tree.rs`); a divided amount in a condition has no recipient and the support walker names it (`Amount.Divided.Elsewhere`, `Amount.EvenShare.Elsewhere`, see Division), so no card that reads one there is offered. `Tap` and `Untap` take the permanents `Scope::live_objects` names and propose `PermanentTapped` or `PermanentUntapped` for each one not in that state yet, in one step (the fold refuses a permanent already in the state, so none is proposed). `Mill` proposes a `ZoneChanged { cause: Milled }` from a library to its owner's graveyard for each of the top cards, top first; a graveyard is open, so every seat sees them (the protocol's `redact` leaves the event whole). `WinGame` proposes `PlayerLost` (reason `Format("an opponent won the game")`) for every player in the game who is an opponent of a winner, in turn order from the active player; the check after the resolution (`flow::settle`) then ends the game with the one player left, as after a concession.
- Tokens: `Effect::CreateToken` proposes one `TokenCreated { object, controller, name, tapped, attacking, definition }` per token, in one step, with consecutive ids from `GameState::next_object`. `definition` is the `CardDefinition` of the token (`mtg_cards::assemble::token_definition`: the inline description or the file of a predefined token, with the abilities `caps()` says the engine runs), so a log folds with no card data. The fold makes a `GameObject` of kind `Token` on the battlefield, owned and controlled by its creator, summoning sick, tapped when the event says so. `attacking` puts it into `CombatState` as an unblocked attacker that was never declared (CR 508.4): no `Attacks` trigger sees it. Only the active player's tokens enter attacking, after attackers are declared; with more than one player or planeswalker to attack the controller is asked once (`ChooseOption`) for all of them. Detection reads `TokenCreated` as an object entering the battlefield from no zone: a `ZoneChange` pattern with a `to` and no `from` matches it. Copies (`TokenRef::CopyOf`) are `Effect.CreateToken.Copy`, which the engine does not run.
- A token ceases to exist (CR 111.7) in `move_object`, the one place every zone change folds: from any move off the battlefield it goes from `objects` and the zones into `GameState.ceased`, so `object(id)` is `None` and `has_ceased(id)` true. The move is an event first, so deaths and leaving triggers are judged against the state before it. An ability whose source ceased still finds the card it was printed on through `GameState::source_object` (the fold of `AbilityTriggered`, `AbilityActivated` and `AbilityPutOnStack`, and the check that a trigger's source is still in the game), which is how a Clue's own ability resolves after the Clue was sacrificed for it and a Pest sees itself die. The views show a token as `kind: Token`.
- Costs of activated abilities: `{T}`, `SacrificeSelf`, `ExileSelf`, `DiscardSelf` and mana are paid when the ability is activated (`cast::activation_events`: the tap, a `ZoneChanged` with `ZoneCause::Sacrificed` from the battlefield, a `CardDiscarded` from the hand or a `ZoneChanged` with `ZoneCause::Exiled` from the zone the source is in, `AbilityActivated`, `AbilityPutOnStack`; the mana by the `PayMana` decision above, whose events follow). The source is photographed before any cost, so the ability remembers a card that was in a graveyard or a hand as it was, and a source that moved is a new object (CR 400.7): an ability that returns its card does nothing when the card has changed zones before it resolves. The source of a price that moves it is not one of the sources of its own mana. Clue (`{2}`, sacrifice) and Food (`{2}`, `{T}`, sacrifice) are paid so; a Clue's draw and a Food's life resolve after the token ceased. A filter-form `Sacrifice`, life and the others are not in `Caps`, so no card offers them.
- Mana abilities: the plain `{T}: Add {X}` is a printed ability of the card; any other mana ability stays in the rules of its card. `mana_shape` (`mtg-cards`) reads the ones the engine runs: a cost of `{T}` and/or sacrificing the source, one type of mana added some number of times, then effects that ask nothing (damage to you, a draw, life). `mana_ability::options` lists every way to use one as a `ManaOption`: one for each type it can add (any colour is five, a choice of types one each, the colour chosen as it entered one), `count` mana of that type counted when offered (Sol Ring 2, Gaea's Cradle one for each creature, a count of nothing is not offered), `sacrifice` when it sacrifices its source and `extra` when it does more than add mana. A source is numbered past the printed abilities, six to each ability. The options are in `PriorityOptions.mana_abilities` and in the sources of every payment (`priority::mana_options`). `ActivateMana`, and each tap of a payment, is one batch with no stack (CR 605.3b): the tap, the sacrifice, `ManaAdded` for each mana, then the events of the effects that follow, which run as the effects of a resolution do (`resolve::run_inline`) and so go through replacement. A mana ability of another shape (a mana cost, a limit, mixed mana, a commander's colours, an effect that asks) is `Ability.ManaAbility.Other`, which the engine does not run; the others are `Ability.ManaAbility.Complex` unless they add one mana of a fixed type or colour and do nothing else (a Treasure's).
- `Effect::AddMana` (the form that runs on the stack, for spells and triggered abilities): a fixed list of mana is added `times` over; "any color" asks the player for a colour (`ChooseOption`, `pick_options`) each time it is added and adds every mana of that addition in it.

Seams the other tasks fill in; each is complete as a type and refuses or does nothing until its task:

| Seam | Where | Until its task |
|---|---|---|
| Announcement | `announce/{alternative,optional,x,modifiers}.rs`; `Needs::of` reads what a card asks | kicker and multikicker give a spell its optional costs; an `{X}` and a `-X` are read (`XCost::of`); no card offers an alternative cost; `Ability::CostModifier` changes the cost of a spell (`modifiers.rs`) |
| Effects | `resolve/prim/<effect>.rs`, one per `Effect` variant | `ResolveError::Unsupported` and no `Caps` entry, so the card is never offered |
| Keywords | `keywords/<name>.rs`, read through `keywords_of` by combat, damage, state-based actions, priority and targeting | the stub of a keyword without its task answers "no" |
| Replacement | `replace::process` in `Engine::commit`, in front of the fold of every batch | a game with no replacement effect (`Possible.replacements`) passes every batch unchanged |
| Capabilities | `RUNS` in `caps.rs` | what the support records may call Verified |
| Equipment | `keywords/{equip,reconfigure,living_weapon}.rs` | `Equip` returns its ability from `adds_abilities`; `Reconfigure` returns two abilities and a static ability; living weapon returns its trigger; `Attach` and `Unattach` run |

## Announcement

`announce/` is the one procedure that puts a spell, an activated ability or a triggered ability on the stack (CR 601.2, 603.3). What the player has begun (`Pending`) lives outside the state, so nothing is logged until the payment is accepted and backing out is free. `announce(state, pending)` runs the steps of `Phase::ORDER` in turn and returns the first question (`Next::Ask`), the events that put the object on the stack (`Next::Commit`) or `Next::Drop`; a step with no question passes to the next.

| Step | Rule | Module | Asks | Fills |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modes | 601.2b | `modes.rs` | `ChooseMode` | |
| Alternative cost | 601.2b | `alternative.rs` | `ChooseOption`, when more than one way to cast can be used | `Choices.way` |
| Optional costs | 601.2b | `optional.rs` | `ChooseCosts`, then `ChooseNumber` for each cost paid any number of times; asked before the modes for a spell with entwine | |
| Parts of a cost | 601.2b, 601.2h | `parts.rs` | `ChooseCards`, for each part that picks permanents or cards and has a choice: of an ability's cost, of the way a spell is cast and of the optional costs paid | `Choices.cost_picks` |
| X | 601.2b | `x.rs` | `ChooseNumber`, from 0 to the most the player can pay | (g2) |
| Targets | 601.2c | `targets.rs` | `ChooseTargets`, one specification at a time | |
| Division | 601.2d | `division.rs` | `DivideAmount` | |
| Legality | 601.2e | `legality.rs` | | |
| Total cost | 601.2f | `total.rs`, `modifiers.rs` | | |
| Payment | 601.2g, 601.2h | `payment.rs` | `PayMana` | |

- What a card asks beyond modes and targets (an alternative cost, optional costs, an `{X}`) is `Needs`, read from its rules when the announcement begins (`Needs::of`). A spell's optional costs come from its keywords (`KeywordRules::additional_cost`), its X from `x_in_cost`, the `{X}` of an activated ability's mana and the `-X` of a loyalty ability (`XCost::of`); its ways to cast come from `ways::of`, and the unit tests of the procedure give a cast the needs a card does not.
- Answers: `Outcome` carries an answer to the cast in progress and `Pending::accept` keeps it in `Choices` for the step that asked (`modes`, `optional_costs`, `x`, `targets`, `division`) and refuses an answer for any other step. Hybrid and Phyrexian symbols are chosen with the payment, not in 601.2b.
- Total cost: the mana cost of the spell or the cost of the ability, replaced by an alternative cost, plus the mana of the optional costs chosen and X generic mana for each `{X}`, then the cost modifiers. The payment asks `PayMana` for that total only when the player can pay it.

- Cost modifiers: `modifiers.rs` gathers the `Ability::CostModifier`s of the spell itself (only those whose `applies_to` names `This`: a card in a hand has no other static ability) and of the permanents of every player still in the game, through `abilities_of`, so a permanent that lost its abilities has none; `Possible.costs` skips the search while no card has one. A modifier applies when its `applies_to` selects the spell, read as a spell on the stack that its caster controls (`Scope.casting`: `Spell` is true and `ControlledBy(You)` means the caster), and its condition holds; it changes the cost by its `mana` as many times as `per` counts (never below 0, at most 100). The increases are added first and the reductions taken away after (CR 601.2f): a generic reduction stops at no generic mana and leaves colored, hybrid and Phyrexian symbols, a colored or colorless symbol of a reduction takes one such symbol from the cost when it has one, and a symbol of an increase joins the rest. A spell's own modifier reads what it announced (`TargetCount`, `X`, `Kicks`), any other reads the game, so X is offered up to the largest the modified cost allows and a total with the targets chosen that the player cannot pay rewinds the announcement. The priority decision offers a spell by its cost with the modifiers applied before anything is announced (`announce::cheapest`: no X, no kicker, no target). Activated and triggered abilities cost what they cost.

- X: `ChooseNumber` offers 0 up to the largest X whose total cost the player can pay with the floating mana and the permanents they control (found by bisection, since more X is only more generic mana), or for a `-X` up to the loyalty counters of the planeswalker. Only 0 possible is no question. X adds X generic mana for each `{X}` to the total cost, is kept in `Choices.x` of the spell or ability on the stack and is read as `Amount::X` while it resolves (CR 107.3), and as 0 where none was chosen. `Price::announced` counts the `{X}` of an ability's mana, `Price::of` refuses it (`Cost.Mana.X`) for a payment in an effect. A spell with `{X}` is castable when X = 0 can be paid. When a target specification reads X (a comparison with X in its filter), only the values of X that leave every specification a legal target are offered (CR 601.2b, 601.2c): `min` and `max` are the least and the greatest of them, `except` the numbers between them without a target, the one value left is no question and none rewinds the cast. `ways::castable_now` and `grantable` price a spell like that with the least X that has a target (`announce::payable`), so a spell whose every payable X lacks one is not offered; an activated or loyalty ability is offered at priority only when some X it can pay for (up to the counters of a `-X`) has a target (`announce::some_x_has_targets`), and a modal spell or ability is offered by the check that leaves X out (`options_before_x`) and rewound by the announcement when no X has a target.
- X after the resolution: a spell on the stack keeps X in its choices, the permanent it becomes keeps it in `PermanentState.x` until it leaves (`GameObject::announced_x` reads either, `Snapshot.x` records it), and a card that arrives any other way (from a graveyard or exile, flickered, put in by an effect) has none, so X = 0 (CR 107.3). The replacement "enters with X counters" reads the X of the spell still on the stack as the card enters (CR 107.3m); the trigger "when this enters" copies the X of its source's snapshot into its own choices as it goes on the stack, so it still reads it after the permanent has left, and any other trigger of the permanent reads 0. A trigger on a spell being cast ("when you cast this spell", "whenever you cast a spell") reads the X of that spell, taken from the snapshot of the spell in the cause. `triggers::x_read` is the one rule: detection reads it for an intervening "if" (CR 603.4) and the announcement of the trigger copies it, so the "if" is judged with the same X when the trigger would trigger and when it resolves. A delayed trigger records the X of the spell or ability that set it up in the snapshot it keeps (CR 107.3k). A target filter reads the X announced (`legal_for` takes it), before X is chosen a spell is offered when its targets exist with the comparisons against X left out (`options_before_x`), and the mana value of a spell on the stack counts X for each `{X}` of its cost (CR 202.3e). The support walker names an X that none of these places runs `Amount.X.Elsewhere`.
- Optional costs: the costs a card prints in the order it prints them (`optional::listed`, read from its rules, so an index names the same cost wherever it is read): `Kicker` and `Buyback` and `Entwine` are paid once, `Multikicker` any number of times, and `Ability::AdditionalCost` ("As an additional cost to cast this spell, you may ...") once. A cost is mana and parts (life and the objects the player picks: a sacrifice, a discard, an exile from a hand or a graveyard, a tap, a return to hand); a cost that taps or loses its source, removes counters, counts X, or is a multikicker with a part, is listed and never offered. `ChooseCosts` lists the costs paid once, by index and label ("Kicker {4}", "Buyback Pay 4 life", "Sacrifice an artifact"), that the player can pay for with the rest of the total cost, parts included (the picks are claimed as `Price::claimed` does for an ability, leaving the permanents that make mana for the mana); the answer is any of them, each once, and may be none. Each cost paid any number of times is then asked with `ChooseNumber`, from 0 to the most times the player can pay it, and not asked when that is 0. The picks of a cost paid are asked after the way is chosen (`Choices.cost_picks`: the parts of the way first, then those of each cost paid by index) and paid with the cast: the events of the parts (`parts::pay`) come before `SpellCast`, and the card being cast is never offered for its own cost.
- Buyback (CR 702.27): the replacement step returns an instant or sorcery that resolved with its buyback cost paid to its owner's hand instead of its graveyard (`Origin::Buyback`, only for `SpellResolved`), so a spell that is countered or whose targets are all illegal goes to the graveyard; with flashback the controller chooses which replacement applies.
- Entwine (CR 702.42): a modal spell with entwine asks its optional costs before its modes (`modes.rs` runs `optional::run`), offers the cost only when every mode has the targets it needs, and when it is paid chooses every mode without a question.
- What was paid: `Choices.optional_costs` holds the index of each cost paid, a repeated cost once for each payment, so its length is the number of kicks; their mana joins the total cost. The spell keeps them while it is on the stack, the permanent it becomes keeps them in `PermanentState.paid` until it leaves (`GameObject::paid_costs` reads either), and an activated or triggered ability copies those of its source into its own choices as it goes on the stack, so it still reads them after its source has gone. `Scope` reads them against the costs the card prints (`announce::payments`): `Condition::Kicked` and `Amount::Kicks` count the payments of a kicker or multikicker, `Condition::AdditionalCostPaid` those of an "as an additional cost" ability, from the choices of what resolves, else from its source while that is the object named; "when ~ enters, if it was kicked" is checked as the event happens and again as the trigger resolves (CR 603.4). `Snapshot.paid` records them as the trigger triggers, and a triggered ability copies them from its snapshot into its own choices as it goes on the stack, so a permanent that left (a state-based action, a flicker) before its trigger went on the stack is still read as kicked.

- Cost parts (`parts/`, one small file for each): the cost of an activated ability may pay life (`Cost.PayLife`, only as much as the player has, CR 119.4), remove counters from its source (`Cost.RemoveCounters`) and take objects the player picks: a permanent sacrificed (`Cost.Sacrifice`) or returned to its owner's hand (`Cost.ReturnToHand`), an untapped permanent tapped (`Cost.TapPermanents`, where summoning sickness does not matter), a card discarded from the hand (`Cost.Discard`) or exiled from the graveyard (`Cost.ExileFromGraveyard`). Energy (`Cost.PayEnergy`, CR 107.14) removes energy counters from the player; "you get {E}{E}" puts them there (`PutCounters` on the player, the counter `Other("energy")`, shown in `PlayerView.counters` as poison is). The amount is a fixed number or X, the value announced with the ability (`Part::X`, `Price::at`); any other amount (`Cost.PayLife.Counted`) is named by the support walker and not offered. `Price::announced` reads the parts, `Price::of` (a keyword cost paid in an effect) refuses them by name; the parts of the cost of a spell (kicker, buyback, entwine, flashback, evoke, madness and "as an additional cost") are read with `Price::announced` and paid by the cast, and the walker names `Keyword.Cost.<part>` only for the keywords that pay none (multikicker, suspend, overload, foretell, morph, disguise, mutate, encore, squad), for counters removed from the source and for an amount that is X (`Keyword.Cost.<part>.X`); an exile from a hand (`Cost.ExileFromHand`, "Exile a red card from your hand") picks like an exile from a graveyard; a payment inside an effect pays them (`resolve/prim/pay.rs`).
- Counted parts: a part that counts X asks `ChooseNumber` ("Choose a value for X.") when the parts of the announcement come to it (`announce/parts.rs` runs `x::run`), from 0 to the most the player can pay for every counted part: all their life while it is above 0 (CR 119.4), the counters on the source, the energy they hold, the objects they may pick; together with the mana of an `{X}` it is the smaller of the two. A counted part with nothing to pay makes X 0 and nothing is asked; the picks that follow are asked for X objects, none for 0. X is kept in `Choices.x` and read by the effects as `Amount::X`.
- Picks: each part that picks asks `ChooseCards` of the payer (`announce/parts.rs`) from the objects that match its filter and are theirs to take, in the order the cost prints them, after the optional costs and before X. The source is not offered when the price taps it for a part that taps, or loses it, and no object is offered twice. A part with exactly as many candidates as it takes is not asked, one with fewer is not payable and the activation is not offered (`Price::claimed`, which leaves permanents that make mana for the mana as far as it can) or, at the announcement, rewound. The question can be backed out of by answering with no cards (`CardsOptions.can_cancel`, set for an announcement, whose questions take at least one card). The picks are kept in `Choices.cost_picks`, one list for each part that picks.
- Paying the parts: `activation_events` (`cast.rs`) logs the `{T}` of the source, then each part in the order of the cost (`LifePaid`; `CountersChanged`; `ZoneChanged` with `Sacrificed`, `Exiled` or `Moved`; `CardDiscarded`; `PermanentTapped`), then the loss of the source, `AbilityActivated` and `AbilityPutOnStack`, with the mana taps after them. What a part sacrifices, taps or returns is not a source of the mana of the same cost, and the life it pays is not there for Phyrexian mana. The source is photographed after its counters are removed, so the ability remembers it as it then stands. A discard and an exile from a graveyard are public as every discard and exile is.
- Cycling (CR 702.29): the keyword adds an activated ability that works from a hand, at instant speed, for its cost and `DiscardSelf`, and draws a card. `activation_events` logs `Cycled` after the `CardDiscarded` of a cost the keyword added, so "when you cycle this card" (an ability that works from the graveyard) and "whenever you cycle a card" read it, and a discard trigger reads the discard. `working_here` reads only printed abilities, so the abilities a keyword adds that work from another zone are read with `provided_here`, and a card with such a keyword is among those whose abilities work elsewhere (`definition_works_elsewhere`).

- Ways to cast: `ways::of(state, player, card)` lists how a card can be cast from where it is, each as a `Way` (the alternative cost or `None` for the mana cost, the label, the mana paid in its place, whether it may be cast at instant timing). From a hand: the mana cost, evoke and overload; from a graveyard: flashback; from exile: the foretell cost of a card its owner foretold on an earlier turn; a cast permission (`GameRule::MayCast`, read through `rules::cast_permissions`) adds the mana cost from the zone it names (a graveyard, exile, the top card of a library), the free way (`CastWay::Free`, the zero cost) and instant timing for the cards it covers, in every zone when it names none. A flashback, evoke or madness cost has mana and parts (`Way.parts`, life and picks, never the source itself, a counted amount or counters): the way is offered only when the player can give what the parts take (`ways::parts_payable`), the picks are asked with the optional costs' and a cost of parts alone pays no mana (`PayMana` with a total of nothing). Overload and foretell are paid with mana alone. `priority_options` offers a card when some way can be used now (timing, targets announced, mana paid: `ways::castable_now`), looking in a hand, the graveyard, exile, the top of the library and the command zone; `PriorityAction::CastSpell` names the card and the announcement asks the way.
- The way: with one way that can be used (its targets announced, its total cost paid) the announcement takes it; with more it asks `ChooseOption` over their labels, an answer outside them is refused and the question cannot be backed out of (a later question, or the payment, can). `Choices.way` holds an alternative cost, `SpellCast` logs it, and the spell on the stack and the permanent it becomes remember it (`GameObject::cast_way`; an activated or triggered ability copies its source's way into its own choices), read as `Condition::CastWith`. An alternative cost has no `{X}`: X is 0. The `SpellCast` event takes the card from the zone it is in.
- Flashback: the replacement step exiles a spell cast for its flashback cost whenever it would leave the stack for any zone but exile (resolved, countered, fizzled), before the other replacement effects (`Origin::Flashback`). Evoke adds the trigger "when this enters, if it was cast for its evoke cost, sacrifice it" (`adds_abilities`). An overloaded spell announces no target, and `Scope::refs` reads `Target(i)` as every object, or player, the specification could target, whatever protects it (an overload of `AnyTarget` or `Either` is refused by name).
- Division: a target specification with `divide` asks `DivideAmount` once its targets are chosen. One target takes the whole amount without a question, and the targets offered never outnumber the amount, since each gets at least one (CR 601.2d). The answer shares the whole amount among every target, at least one each; `Choices.division` keeps the shares in the order of the targets. Resolution reads it per recipient (`resolve/share.rs`): `DealDamage` and `PutCounters` work out the amount for each target they name with `Scope::amounts_for`. `Amount::Divided` is the share `Choices.division` holds for that target, none when the division left it out; `Amount::EvenShare` is the whole rounded down among the targets the effect names, with no division chosen by a player (CR 601.2d, "divided evenly, rounded down"). The targets named are the legal ones (`Scope::chosen`), so a target that became illegal loses its share and no other gets more, and a spell whose targets are all illegal does nothing (CR 608.2b). A divided amount outside the amount of damage or counters (a sum of one included) has no recipient: the evaluator refuses it by name and the support walker names it `Amount.Divided.Elsewhere` or `Amount.EvenShare.Elsewhere`.
- Rewind: a step that cannot be met rewinds the announcement as a whole (CR 601.2). A mode or target specification with nothing to offer, an X or a total cost the player cannot pay, a division that cannot give each target one and a chosen target that is no longer legal at the check (601.2e) drop a cast or activation with no event, and remove a triggered ability (CR 603.3d). A player backs out with `Cancel` at any question of a cast or activation (`can_cancel`); a triggered ability has no way out.
- A cast during a resolution (CR 608.2g, `Effect::CastFromZone`): the step asks the player "Cast it without paying its mana cost?" unless nothing lets them (the card is not where it was named, a land, a spell with no target to announce, a cost they cannot pay; a free cast still pays a cost increase), then returns `Run::Cast`, and the engine begins the announcement as a `Pending` with `Granted` set (`Pending::during_resolution`). Its questions (modes, targets, X, optional costs, the payment) are asked as for any cast with no way back, a free cast with nothing to pay needs no payment decision, and the spell goes on the stack above the resolving object, which ends after it. The way is the card's mana cost or the free way alone (`ways::granted`). Nothing of the announcement is logged until it ends, so a game rebuilt from its log in the middle of one asks it again; its events end with `ChoiceMade { YesNo(true) }`, and an announcement that cannot go on logs `YesNo(false)`, which the journal of the step reads back to go on: past the effect when the spell was cast, into `otherwise` when not.
- Cascade and discover: `ExileUntil` exiles each card from the top of the library face up, so every seat sees it (`ZoneChanged` to exile is public), until one matches; the library running out ends it with nothing found. Cascade is the trigger of the keyword, "when you cast this spell", working from the stack (`provided_here` abilities are detected there as in a hand); a spell cast by a cascade that cascades has its own trigger on the stack above the resolved one. An X spell cast free has X 0;- Madness and suspend: `CastFromZone` takes a `way`. `Madness` casts the card for the madness keyword's cost (`ways::madness_cost`; the question reads "Cast X for its madness cost {R}?", a cost of nothing needs no payment, a cost with life or picks pays them with the cast, a cost that cannot be paid or a card with no legal target is not asked and the `otherwise` effects run). `Suspend` casts it without paying; a suspend cost is mana alone (`Keyword.Cost.<part>` names the rest). The spell remembers the way (`Condition::CastWith`).
- Madness (CR 702.35): the keyword adds a replacement of the discard that works from a hand (`ReplaceWhen::Discarded`, `MoveTo(Exile)`; `replace.rs` reads the abilities a keyword provides, and a discard is a `Unit::Move` with `discard` set, whether it is `CardDiscarded` or the `ZoneChanged` with `ZoneCause::Discarded` another replacement made). The card is discarded into the open (`ZoneChanged` to exile, `Discarded` triggers see it) and the keyword's trigger, which works from exile and matches that discard, casts it or puts it into the graveyard.
- Counters on a card in exile: a card in exile holds counters (`GameObject::card_counters`, cleared by every zone change); `CountersChanged` folds on it, `Amount::Counters` and the snapshot read it, and `RemoveCounters` and `PutCounters` name it.
- Suspend (CR 702.62): the special action is `PriorityAction::Suspend`, offered in `PriorityOptions::suspendable` when the card is in the hand, its owner could cast it at this timing (instant, flash, or sorcery timing; targets are not needed) and can pay the suspend cost (`ways::can_suspend`; cost modifiers do not apply). The announcement (`announce/suspend.rs`, `Pending::special`) asks `PayMana` with a way back, or nothing for a cost of nothing, and its events are the payment around a `ZoneChanged` to exile (face up) and `CountersChanged` of the card's time counters (`CounterKind::Other("time")`); the stack is not used. The keyword adds two triggers that work from exile (the owner's upkeep removes a counter while the card holds one; the last counter removed casts the card for nothing with `way: Suspend`; a card that cannot be cast stays in exile with no counter) and a static ability that gives a permanent cast that way haste while its owner controls it. A keyword's static ability that grants a keyword is found by the layers (`definition_has_static`) and by the address of the grant (`ability_reader::static_mod`).
- Foretell (CR 702.143): the special action is `PriorityAction::Foretell`, offered in `PriorityOptions::foretellable` to the holder of priority during their own turn, at any step and with a spell on the stack, for a card with the keyword in their hand when they can pay {2} (`ways::can_foretell`; the cost is `keywords::foretell::action_cost`, whatever the card, and cost modifiers do not apply). It shares the announcement of suspend (`announce/foretell.rs`, `Pending::special`, `Special::Foretell`): `PayMana` with a way back, then one `ZoneChanged` from the hand to exile with `ZoneCause::Foretold`; the stack is not used. The keyword adds no ability. The card lies face down in exile until it changes zones, and its owner may cast it from exile for the keyword's cost (`CastWay::Foretold`, offered by `ways::of` when `FaceDown::Foretold { turn }` names a turn before the current one; cost modifiers and the timing of the card apply, a card exiled again is face up and not foretold). The spell is face up on the stack and remembers the way (`Condition::CastWith`).
- Face down (CR 708): `GameObject::face_down` says why an object lies face down (`FaceDown`, set by the fold of a `ZoneChanged` whose cause says it arrives that way, `ZoneCause::face_down`, cleared by every zone change) and `GameState::may_look_at(viewer, object)` says who may look at it (`FaceDown::may_look`: the owner of a foretold card). Characteristics stay the card's own for the engine; the views, the log and the snapshots read only that rule. Morph, disguise, manifest and cloak (n2) add a `FaceDown` kind with its own characteristics and who may look; the projection (protocol.md, Visibility) needs no other change.
- Faces (CR 708 to 715): `CardDefinition.layout` (`Split`, `Adventure`, `ModalDfc`) and `faces` hold a card with several faces; the definition itself is the card outside the stack and the battlefield: the front face, or for a split card both halves (the two names joined by ` // `, the symbols of both costs so the mana value is their sum, the combined colours and types). The face an object is in use as is `GameObject::face` (`Choices.face` while it is a spell, `PermanentState.face` as a permanent, none elsewhere), and `card()` is that face's definition, so characteristics, rules and ability addresses read the face with no other change; `whole_card()` is the card. A zone change clears the face (a card that leaves the stack or the battlefield is its front face again) except from the stack to the battlefield. The front face of an Adventure or modal double-faced card is `None`, a split half is `Some(0)` or `Some(1)`, and the Adventure and a back face are `Some(1)`.
- Casting a face: the announcement asks it first (`announce/face.rs`, `Phase::Face`, 601.2a). The faces that can be cast now (`faces::castable_faces`) are tried one at a time against `GameState::with_face`, the state with the card already in use as that face, so every later step (modes, ways, X, targets, cost, payment) reads the card as it will be on the stack. With more than one face usable the player picks with `ChooseOption` over their names and costs, with one nothing is asked; the choice is kept in `Choices.face` and logged in `SpellCast`. `PriorityOptions.faces` lists each castable face and each playable land face with its cost (`FaceOption`); `castable` and `playable_lands` list the card. A back face land is played with `PriorityAction::PlayLandFace(card, face)`, logged as `LandPlayed { face }`.
- Adventure (CR 715): a spell cast as the Adventure that resolves is exiled by a replacement (`Origin::Adventure`, `ZoneCause::Adventure`) and its card remembers who may cast it (`GameObject::on_adventure`, cleared by every zone change); `ways::of` offers the creature card for its mana cost from exile to that player, and only as the card. A spell that is countered or has no legal target goes to the graveyard as any spell. The Adventure exile is no effect of the spell, so it is ordered with the other replacements of the move to the graveyard (Rest in Peace) by the affected player (CR 616.1): with the Adventure's first the card is exiled on an adventure, with the other first it is exiled and cannot be cast (`ZoneCause::Adventure` only when `Origin::Adventure` was applied). A land played as a face enters as that face: `replace::process` reads the batch against `GameState::with_face`, so the enters replacements (tapped, tapped unless) are the face's and not the card in the hand's.
- Transform (CR 701.28, 712): a transforming double-faced card (`Layout::Transform`) is its front face everywhere and enters the battlefield front face up, cast or put there; `Effect::Transform` turns each named permanent of that layout over with `Event::Transformed { object, face }`, which sets `PermanentState.face` (`None` front, `Some(1)` back). It is the same object, so counters, attachments, tapped state and summoning sickness stay, and characteristics, abilities and name read the face through `GameObject::card()`. A card of another layout, a token, a card in another zone and a repeated name are passed over, and the source of an ability that has been turned over since the ability went on the stack (`Snapshot.face` against the face now) is not turned again (CR 701.28f). A zone change clears the face, so the card leaves as its front face.
- Day and night (CR 726, 702.145): `GameState::day_night` is `None` until a permanent with daybound or nightbound is on the battlefield, then `Day`; it is hashed and shown in `TurnView`. `end_turn` emits `Event::DayNightChanged` after `TurnBegan` when the ending turn's active player cast no spells (day to night) or two or more (night to day), read from the turn's `Tally::Cast` before the new turn clears it. State-based actions (`day_night::sync`, only when `Possible::day_night`) make the first bound permanent set the designation and turn daybound permanents to their night face at night and nightbound ones to their day face by day, so a daybound permanent that enters at night is turned before anyone has priority.
- Fuse (CR 702.102): `assemble` gives a split card with fuse and a spell on each half one rule on the card as a whole, the spell of both halves (the targets of the left half and then those of the right, the effects of the left half and then the right half with its `Target(n)` and `TargetCount(n)` renumbered after the left half's). A card in a hand lists the face position after its last half as a castable face (`faces::candidates`); `GameObject::card()` of that position is the whole card, so cost, colours, types and rules are both halves' and the announcement, payment and resolution need no other change. `FaceOption.face` and `SpellCast.choices.face` carry that position.
- Resolution can also ask `ChooseNumber`: the answer is logged as `ChoiceMade { Number }` and read back from the journal; no effect asks it yet.

## Replacement

The rules of the data are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Triggers, replacement and layers); this section says where the engine keeps them.

- `Engine::commit` is the one place events are applied: it passes the batch to `replace::process`, folds what comes out and logs it. The batch of a resolution step is whole (its events and `StepDone`), so a step is applied or asked about as a unit.
- A question of the step (`ChooseReplacement`, a yes or no for life paid as a permanent enters, a colour or a creature type) is the engine's decision. `Engine` holds the batch and the answers so far (`replacing()` is true), and an answer reruns `process`, which asks the same questions in the same order. The answers are not logged. A batch that a decision cannot recompute (a land played, an ability's cost) is lost with the engine's memory; one that the state recomputes (a resolution step, state-based actions, combat damage, a draw) is asked again.
- `process` reads the replacement effects once for the batch (`gather`: the permanents in play through `abilities_of`, objects in the zone an ability works from) and looks at each item in turn: the events of the batch, with a run of `TokenCreated` for one controller as one item. The candidates that apply and have not been applied to the item are ordered by address; effects about their own object go first; one candidate is applied, more are asked. Applying changes the item or replaces it with new ones (`WithCounters` adds a `CountersChanged` that the other effects then see, `MoveTo` turns a death into a `ZoneChanged`), which carry the effects already applied.
- The spell that is redirected leaves the stack by a `ZoneChanged` and the engine gives priority with `PriorityGranted`; the end of its resolution (`ResolutionEnded`) follows and finds it gone. Triggers read the move from the state after the batch.
- Shields are `RuleEffect`s (`Rule::PreventDamage`, `Rule::Regenerate`) made by `Effect::Shield` and `Effect::Regenerate`; they end by their duration, when the permanents they name are new objects or gone, and when used up. `process` reads them with the replacement effects of the permanents: a damage shield is a candidate for each `DamageDealt` it covers, with what it has left as the amount it prevents, and the batch tracks what it used. A shield that prevents a given amount takes the damage events of one affected player in the order that player picks: when it is chosen for an event and the events it covers for that player (this one and the later ones of the batch) add up to more than it has left, the player is asked with `ChooseOption`, one label per distinct event ("2 damage from Imp to Bear0", `source` the maker of the shield), and the shield is applied to the event picked; the question comes again for what is left until the shield is used up or one distinct event remains. No question is asked when the shield has enough for all of them or they are one and the same event. The players go in the order of their first event in the batch. The batch ends with `ShieldUsed` for a shield with damage left to prevent and `RuleExpired` for one that is used up. A regeneration shield is the only candidate for a destruction of its permanent (`ZoneChanged` with `Destroyed`, `CreatureDied` by lethal damage or deathtouch): the event becomes `Regenerated`, which taps the permanent, clears its damage and removes it from combat. Lifelink follows the damage that is left. Damage that `rules::damage_cant_be_prevented` covers is offered to no prevention effect.
- The state-based checks of the testkit skip a decision of the step: the events it is about (a creature to die) are not applied yet.

## Triggered abilities

The rules of the data are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Triggers, replacement and layers); this section says where the engine keeps them.

- Detection: `Engine::commit` folds a batch of events, then calls `triggers::detect(pre, events, post)`. It copies the state before the batch only when the batch looks back (`triggers::needs_before`: a death, a discard, a move out of the battlefield, a spell or ability that ends without resolving); any other batch is judged by the state after it. Each `Triggered { source, ability, controller, cause, snapshot, delayed }` it returns is logged as `AbilityTriggered` right after the batch and joins `GameState.pending_triggers`. Replay folds the logged events and never detects. A game with no triggered ability and no effect that sets one up or gives one (`GameState::watches`) skips detection and the copy; the flag follows the game as it folds a token with a triggered ability or an effect that gives one, and a batch that brings in the first of them is detected too (`triggers::introduces`).
- What happened: events become `Happened` values. `ZoneChange` comes from `LandPlayed`, `SpellResolved`, `CreatureDied`, `CardDiscarded`, `CardDrawn`, `ZoneChanged` and the end of a spell that did not resolve; `Attacks` from each attacker of `AttackersDeclared`; `SpellCast` from `SpellCast`; `BecomesTarget` from the targets of `SpellCast` and `AbilityPutOnStack`, with the permanent, what targets it and the controller of that as its subjects; `StepBegins` from `StepBegan`. The patterns about players and permanents come from the events they name (`detect_events.rs` tests each): `DamageDealt` (and a `LifeLost` of the player it hits), `LifeGained`, `LifeLost` and `LifePaid`, `CardDrawn`, a discard, `Cycled`, `PermanentTapped` and `PermanentUntapped`, `ManaAdded` after the tap of its source, `CountersChanged`, `BlockersDeclared`, `AttackersDeclared` as a group and `Attached`. A move out of the battlefield is judged against the state before the batch (the permanents that were there, the objects as they were), every other event against the state after. One `Happened` triggers an ability once, so an `AnyOf` that fits two ways triggers once; two attackers or two deaths trigger it twice.
- Batches and turn counts: after the single events of a `detect` call, the `Happened` values that look back and those that do not are each tried as one `Happened::Batch` against the abilities with a `Batch` pattern, which `causes` splits by the players the matched events name and counts into `Cause.amount`. `Happened::Drew`, `Cast` and `LifeGained` carry their `nth`: `GameState::tallies` (by player and `Tally`, folded from `CardDrawn`, `SpellCast` and `LifeGained`, cleared by `TurnBegan`, hashed when not empty) less the later events of that kind in the batch.
- Turn counts by object: `GameState::object_tallies` (by `ObjectRef`, the player whose spell or ability caused it or none for all, and `Tally::Attacked`, `Targeted` or `Damaged`) is folded from `AttackersDeclared`, `SpellCast` and `AbilityPutOnStack` (once for each object among the targets, for the controller and for all) and `DamageDealt` to an object (not 0), cleared by `TurnBegan` and hashed when not empty. `Happened::Attacked`, `Targeted` (with `nth` for the controller of what targets, `nth_any` for all) and `Damaged` carry their `nth` the same way, from `object_ordinal`, and `Nth` picks `nth_any` for a `BecomesTarget` with no `by`.
- A trigger carries its controller (whoever controlled the source as it triggered), the subjects of the event with snapshots (`Ref::Triggering`) and a snapshot of the source, so the ability can read both once they have left the battlefield.
- Limits: `limits_spent` holds the (object, ability) pairs used this turn and is cleared by `TurnBegan`. A triggered ability with `OncePerTurn` does not trigger again and an activated one is not offered again; a new object (CR 400.7) has its own.
- The sweep: `flow::next_in_turns` runs it after state-based actions and the game-end check and before the turn structure, so it precedes every priority decision (CR 117.5, 603.3) and every repeat of it follows state-based actions. It takes the first player in turn order from the active player who has a trigger waiting. A player with two or more of the current round asks `OrderTriggers` once (`TriggersOrdered` marks them ordered) unless they are all of one source and run the same rules (one ability, or the instances of a keyword), which do the same in any order; a trigger that a keyword added is introduced by the keyword in its words ("Battle cry: when it attacks"); the rest go on in the order they triggered. Each trigger then becomes a pending announcement like a cast (`Pending`, `What::Trigger`): its target specifications are asked one at a time (`TargetOptions.triggered`, no cancel), and `AbilityPutOnStack` with its `cause` takes it out of the queue and puts it on the stack with its choices logged.
- Zones: a triggered ability works from the zone its `from` names (`ability_reader::working_here` lists the printed abilities of a card outside the battlefield that work where it is). `detect` tries the permanents first and then, when a card of the game works from another zone (`GameState::possible`), every other card in id order, against the same events and the same state (a creature that dies is looked back at, and a card that was in the graveyard before it is still there in `pre`). Such a trigger carries the snapshot of its card in that zone, the card that left its zone before the ability resolves is read from that snapshot, and an ability that moves its card (`Move` of `This`) does nothing for a card that changed zones (CR 400.7). A trigger from the stack ("when you cast this spell") goes on the stack above its spell.
- State triggers (`triggers/state.rs`, CR 603.8): `flow::next_in_turns` asks for them after state-based actions and the game-end check, before the sweep, only when a card of the game has one (`GameState::possible`, also set by a token or a granted ability). A state trigger found is logged as `AbilityTriggered` with an empty cause. It is not found while an instance of the same ability of the same object (`ObjectRef`, address) waits in `pending_triggers` or is on the stack (`AbilityObject::origin` tells granted abilities apart), so it triggers once, and again after that instance resolved, was countered or was removed if the state still holds. The state is judged where a player would receive priority, not during a resolution.
- Rounds: a trigger that triggers while others are going on the stack, such as one for becoming a target, belongs to the next round (CR 603.3b). A trigger takes `GameState.trigger_round` when it triggers, and each trigger put on the stack raises the number.
- Removal: a trigger whose target specification has too few legal targets (CR 603.3d), one whose controller or source has left the game, and one whose controller chooses no target (`TargetChoice::Remove`) leave the queue as `TriggerRemoved`.
- Holds: nothing goes on the stack in the untap step; the sweep waits for the upkeep (CR 502.4). A trigger in the cleanup step gets a round of priority and cleanup begins again afterwards (CR 514.3a, `steps::cleanup`).
- Resolution: an ability object keeps its trigger's cause, source snapshot and (for a delayed one) the names it took. An intervening "if" is judged by `Scope::holds` when the event happens and once more when the ability begins to resolve; if it is false then the outcome is `ResolutionOutcome::Lapsed`. Targets are rechecked as for any ability and `Ref::Triggering` is available while choosing them.
- Delayed triggers: the `DelayedTrigger` effect logs `DelayedTriggerCreated` with the pattern, the effects, the names the resolution had bound and who controls it; the fold adds a `Delayed` to `GameState.delayed` whose rules are a triggered ability. `detect` tries delayed triggers after the printed ones. One that triggers once is removed when `AbilityTriggered` folds. One that does not lasts until the end of the turn it was set up in. `Delayed::lapsed` also covers the durations of the DSL (end of combat, the controller's next turn, the source leaving) and a controller who left the game; housekeeping logs `DelayedTriggerExpired` for those.

Events: `AbilityTriggered`, `TriggersOrdered`, `TriggerRemoved`, `DelayedTriggerCreated`, `DelayedTriggerExpired`. The protocol shows a trigger about a card the viewer cannot see (a card drawn) as its hidden form ([protocol.md](protocol.md)).

## Combat and keywords

`keywords::keywords_of(state, id)` is the one reader of the keywords an object has: the printed ones in the order of its rules, then those continuous effects grant (layer 6, [Continuous effects](#continuous-effects)), none for an ability on the stack; `has_keyword`, `damage_rules`, `summoning_sick` and the other helpers of `keywords/mod.rs` are built on it. Each keyword is a `KeywordRules` value in `keywords/<name>.rs` with its entry in `RUNS`.

| Hook | Keyword | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `restricts_blocking`, `answers_evasion` | flying, reach | `stops_block` in `block_options` |
| `min_blockers` | menace | `block_options`, `check_blockers` |
| `forbids_attacking` | defender | `can_attack` |
| `ignores_summoning_sickness` | haste | `can_attack`, mana and `{T}` abilities, the views |
| `attacks_untapped` | vigilance | `attack_events` |
| `modifies_damage` (`DamageRules`) | first strike, double strike, deathtouch, trample, lifelink | `damage.rs`, the fold of `DamageDealt` |
| `is_indestructible` | indestructible | state-based actions; the `Destroy` primitive asks `keywords::is_indestructible` |
| `restricts_targeting`, `restricts_player_targeting` | hexproof, shroud | `legal_targets` through `protects` and `protects_player` |
| `protects_from` (`Holder`, `Source`) | protection | `keywords::protected_from`: damage in `replace.rs`, `legal_host` in `attach.rs`, `stops_block`, `legal_targets` |
| `grants_timing` | flash | `priority_options` through `grants_instant_timing` |

- Keywords that are triggers: prowess, battle cry and ward return DSL abilities from `adds_abilities`; the rest of the engine reads them as printed triggers. Prowess: `SpellCast` by you of a spell that is not a creature, `Continuous` +1/+1 until end of turn on `This`. Battle cry: `Attacks` by `This`, `Continuous` +1/+0 until end of turn on each other attacking creature. Ward: `BecomesTarget` of `This` by a spell or ability an opponent controls, `Pay` the ward cost by the controller of that spell or ability, unpaid `Counter` it; the triggering objects are the permanent, the spell or ability and its controller, and `Counter` passes over what is not on the stack. An ability on the stack is countered like a spell and ceases to exist. A ward cost that is not mana is outside `Caps`.
- Equip (CR 702.6) returns one activated ability from `adds_abilities`: its cost, `timing: Sorcery`, one target (a creature its controller controls) and `Attach` of `This` to it. The ability is offered to the controller of the Equipment, who must be the active player in a main step with an empty stack and able to pay (`Ability.Sorcery`); on resolution the target is checked again (the ability does nothing when it is illegal or the Equipment has left), and an Equipment already attached to the creature is not attached again and keeps its timestamp (CR 701.3c). Only mana costs are paid (`Cost.Mana`).
- Reconfigure (CR 702.151) returns three abilities: the attach ability of Equip for another creature, offered only while the source is attached to no creature, the unattach ability (`Effect::Unattach`, no target), offered only while it is attached to one, and the static ability "while attached to a creature, this permanent isn't a creature" (`RemoveType`, layer 4). Both activated abilities have `timing: Sorcery` and a `condition` ("activate only if", `Ability.Condition`) that `can_activate` judges as the ability is activated; the condition counts the creatures among the host (`Pred::Is(Ref::Attached)`), so an unattached source fails it. `legal_host` lets an Equipment that is a creature be attached when it has reconfigure (CR 301.5c); it is no creature while attached, so the state-based action leaves it there, and when its host stops being a creature the action takes it off and it is a creature again. A static ability a keyword adds is named by the rule of the keyword (`Grant::Static`), and `definition_has_static` keeps the layers working in a game whose only static ability is such a one. An Equipment that loses its abilities while attached stays no creature, since the layer 4 effect applied before layer 6.
- Living weapon (CR 702.92) returns one triggered ability: when `This` enters, `CreateToken` of a 0/0 black Phyrexian Germ creature that the effect names (`as: "germ"`) and `Attach` of `This` to `Bound("germ")`. `CreateToken` binds the tokens it proposes as new objects (zone-change counter 0), so the next effect of the resolution finds them on the battlefield; the Germ is made and the Equipment attached in one resolution, before state-based actions or priority, so the 0/0 never dies first. A Germ that loses its Equipment dies as a 0/0 and a token ceases to exist.
- Blocking: a creature that needs several blockers is offered in `BlockOptions.min_blockers` only while that many of the defender's creatures could block it, and `check_blockers` refuses fewer (`IllegalAnswer::TooFewBlockers`).
- Damage steps: the first-strike step occurs when an attacker or a blocker has first strike or double strike as blockers are declared (`damage::first_strike_step_needed`). A creature with first strike alone deals damage in it, one with double strike in both steps and any other in the regular step (`strike_power`, CR 510.4). State-based actions run between the steps, because damage and the priority grant are one batch and the next move checks them first. Entering a step (`StepBegan`) forgets the attackers' divisions, so a double striker divides twice.
- Dividing (`damage::division`): an unblocked attacker deals all its damage to what it attacks and a blocked one to its blockers, all to the only one or as its controller divides among several. With trample the player or planeswalker attacked also receives the damage beyond lethal to every blocker, and all of it when no blocker is left. A controller is asked (`DamageOptions`) only when more than one division exists; the default is lethal damage to each blocker in order and the rest on the player attacked or else the last blocker (`default_shares`).
- `DamageOptions.lethal` is the lethal damage of each blocker: its toughness less the damage marked, and at most 1 from a deathtouch source (CR 702.2c). `excess` is the player or planeswalker attacked, with trample. The answer lists the share of each blocker only: without trample the shares add up to the power; with it they add up to at most the power, the rest goes to `excess`, and that needs at least `lethal` on every blocker (`IllegalAnswer::ExcessBeforeLethal`, CR 702.19b).
- Deathtouch: the fold marks a creature (`PermanentState::deathtouched`) when the `DamageDealt` event says its source had deathtouch (`deathtouch`), combat or not, and the state-based action destroys it (`DeathCause::Deathtouch` when lethal damage would not have). Indestructible: lethal damage and the mark do not destroy it; zero toughness does (CR 704.5f).
- Lifelink: `damage::dealt_by` follows each `DamageDealt` of a source with lifelink with `LifeGained` for its controller, in the same batch; combat and the `DealDamage` primitive both use it. A source that has left deals its damage as its snapshot holds it (`damage::Dealer`, CR 608.2h): the keywords it had decide `deathtouch` and the life, and the player who controlled it then gains it.
- The views show `summoning_sick` as `keywords::summoning_sick`, so a creature with haste is not shown as sick.
- Shroud: `legal_targets` leaves out a permanent that has it on the battlefield and a player who has it, for every controller, its own included.
- Protection (CR 702.16, `keywords/protection.rs`): `Keyword::Protection(Selector)` holds the quality as a selector over the source (a colour, a card type, a player's objects with `ControlledBy`, everything with the empty selector); `keywords::protected_from(holder, source)` is the one reader, judged for the holder's controller, and a permanent holds it on the battlefield only. Damage: the replacement step prevents all damage a source with the quality would deal to the holder before any other effect applies, so no shield is used, unless `DamageCantBePrevented` covers it; the flag `possible.protection` (a card, token, player or effect with protection) switches the step on, and the combat forecast reads it through the same step. Attaching: `legal_host` refuses an attachment with the quality, so `Attach`, an Aura entering without being cast and the state-based actions of attachments all follow (an Aura goes to the graveyard, an Equipment comes off). Blocking: `stops_block` refuses a blocker with the quality, so the block options, the bots, the declaration, the preview and the forecast agree. Targeting: `legal_from` leaves out a holder protected from the spell or ability, read as the source was when it has gone (the snapshot of a resolving ability, CR 608.2h). `Modification::AddProtectionFromChosenColor` asks its controller for a colour through `ChooseOption` as the effect begins and adds protection from it (Mother of Runes).
- Hexproof: `legal_targets` leaves out a permanent that has it on the battlefield, and a player who has it, for a spell or ability whose controller is another seat; its own controller's spells and abilities still reach it, and a hexproof card in a hand, a graveyard or on the stack (a creature spell) is no exception (CR 113.6). The recheck on resolution is the same function, so a target that became protected is dropped and a spell with only such targets fizzles (CR 608.2b). A player's keywords are `Player::keywords`, set by `PlayerSetup.keywords`, and `keywords::player_keywords` is their one reader, where the effects of layer 6 will join.

## Continuous effects

`Effect::Continuous` makes the effects of a resolved spell or ability (CR 611); the rules of the data are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Triggers, replacement and layers).

- A `ContinuousEffect` (`effects.rs`) lives in `GameState.effects` and holds its id, layer (none for a restriction), selector, modification, duration, timestamp, source (an `ObjectRef`), controller and the turn it began in. `EffectCreated` adds one and `EffectExpired` removes it; the fold refuses a layer that is not the modification's, a repeated id and a `WhileSourceOnBattlefield` effect without a source.
- Creation: the primitive names the permanents its reference names when it runs (`Scope::live_objects`) and fixes them as `Selector::Objects(Vec<ObjectRef>)` (CR 611.2c). It makes one effect for each modification, in the order of the text, with the next ids and timestamps of the state (CR 613.7), and its amounts are worked out then. Naming no permanent makes none. Every modification of the DSL becomes the engine's `Modification` (`resolve::lower` works out its amounts and the player a `SetController` names), whether a spell or ability begins it or a static ability does.
- Derivation: `layers.rs` works through the layers once for a state, for every permanent at once, and keeps the result until the next event (`GameState::layers`; an event that cannot change what the layers read, such as passing priority or adding mana, keeps it). A layer's effects are the active effects of resolved spells and abilities and the modifications of the static abilities the permanents have at that point of the pass; each is applied to every permanent it applies to, so an effect reads the other objects as the earlier layers and the earlier effects of its own layer left them. The result holds for each permanent its characteristics (types, the subtypes effects added, colours, power, toughness, plain mana abilities), its controller (layer 2), the abilities and the keywords it has, in the order they were given, and the effects that changed it, in order (`Applied`). `characteristics` and `seen_as` read the first, `controller_of` the second, `ability_reader` the third and `keywords_of` the fourth (layer 6: `AddKeyword` appends, never twice, `RemoveKeyword` removes, `LoseAbilities` clears, in the order applied); `can_attack` and `block_options` read the restrictions (`CantAttack`, `CantBlock`) from the fifth. Combat, damage, state-based actions (toughness 0 or less kills, even an indestructible creature), targeting, the value evaluator and the views read power, toughness, controller and keywords this way; snapshots keep the derived characteristics, controller and keywords. A game with no effect, no static ability and no +1/+1 or -1/-1 counter on the battlefield works out nothing and every permanent is as its card prints it.
- Counters in layer 7c: the +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters on a permanent change its power and toughness by one each (CR 613.4c) as an effect the pass gathers from the permanent itself, not a stored `ContinuousEffect`. It applies at the start of layer 7c, after everything that sets power and toughness (layers 7a and 7b) and before the other effects of 7c and the switch of 7d, so an effect that reads power or toughness sees the counters (CR 613.8), and the pumps of 7c add up with them whatever their timestamps. `GameState::layers` does the pass when a +1/+1 or -1/-1 counter is on a permanent. Power and toughness of a permanent that has none are left as they are. Snapshots keep the result.
- State-based actions for counters (`sba.rs`): a permanent with both a +1/+1 and a -1/-1 counter loses as many of each as it has of the kind it has fewer of (CR 704.5q), as two `CountersChanged` in the pass that finds it, unless it dies in that pass; a player with ten or more poison counters loses with `LossReason::Poison` (CR 704.5c, after life and the library).
- Static abilities: the `Static` abilities a permanent has in the pass (the printed ones less those lost, then those given) apply in the layer of each modification, stamped with the timestamp the permanent entered with (`PermanentState::timestamp`, the counter that effects share; permanents of a starting position have 0), or with the grant's when that is later (CR 613.7a). `GameState::statics_possible` is true when a card of the game, or a token made in play, has a static ability, so a game with neither that nor an effect works out nothing. A static ability of a card that is not on the battlefield applies while the card is in the zone its `from` names (`ability_reader::working_here`, looked at only when a card of the game works from another zone), to the objects its selector names. An ability applies while its source has it: one whose first modification comes after layer 6 is dropped when its source has lost it by then (`LoseAbilities`), and one that has begun to apply goes on applying to the permanents it began with, whatever happens to the ability (CR 613.6). A static ability that an effect or another static gives (`GrantAbility`) applies in the layers after layer 6; its modifications of earlier layers do not apply. A granted ability is read from the data of the effect or the static that grants it (`ability_reader`, `Grant`), and a granted static ability cannot give abilities in turn.
- Dependencies (CR 613.8): within a layer the effects that wait are chosen again after each one applies (CR 613.8c). The first, by timestamp, that depends on no other waiting effect applies next; effect A depends on B when applying B to the permanents it applies to would change whether A exists (its source has the ability and its condition holds), which permanents it applies to or what it does to them (the amounts it works out). The pass finds that out by trying B on copies of those permanents and asking A again, and only for a pair where one reads what the other writes (`layers/reads.rs`: each modification names the characteristics it writes, a selector, condition and amount those they read). Effects that depend on each other in a loop go by timestamp (CR 613.8b), and an effect that depends on a loop waits for it. The modifications of one static ability never depend on each other, since they apply to the same permanents (CR 613.6).
- Characteristic-defining abilities (CR 604.3): a `Static` with `defining` set defines power and toughness (layer 7a), colours, types or subtypes of its own object. On the battlefield it applies first in its layer, before every other effect of the layer, and neither kind depends on the other (CR 613.3, 613.8a). In every other zone `characteristics` applies the defining abilities of the card to its printed characteristics (`layers::outside`), counting the permanents as the layers leave them and reading the controller as the owner; abilities that count each other's objects end, the second read as printed.
- Control: `controller_of` is the controller after layer 2; `GameObject::controller` is the one the object entered under, which changes only by entering or leaving. Whoever reads who controls a permanent (attacking, blocking, priority options, untapping, triggers, targeting, views) asks `controller_of`. A permanent is held back by summoning sickness unless its controller has controlled it since their turn began (CR 302.6): `PermanentState::observed` is the controller it was last seen under at the start of that player's turn, and `sick_for` also holds when it is not the one now.
- Restrictions: `CantAttack` and `CantBlock` apply to the objects their effect or static names; `characteristics::cant_attack` and `cant_block` are the one reading, asked by `can_attack` and `block_options`.
- Evasion (CR 509.1b): `CantBeBlocked` and `CantBeBlockedBy { by, controller, source }` restrict the attacker, from a static ability or from an effect until end of turn. `characteristics::cant_be_blocked_by(attacker, blocker)` is the one reading, asked by `block_options` beside `stops_block`, so the options, the declaration check, the bots, the preview and the forecast agree. The selector `by` judges the blocker as the layers leave it when blockers are declared (its power at that moment), with `controller` and `source` as "you" and "this". Blocks already declared stay when an attacker gains the restriction; one that loses it before blocks can be blocked. Menace counts only the blockers that could block the attacker.
- Objects: `Selector::Objects` matches the very incarnation named, so a permanent that leaves and comes back is a new object (CR 400.7) that keeps nothing. An effect is spent when none of its objects is the one it named; it stops applying and housekeeping removes it.
- Durations (`ContinuousEffect::is_active`): `UntilEndOfTurn` ends in the cleanup step, in the batch that removes damage (CR 514.2), so a creature that a pump kept alive survives it; `UntilEndOfCombat` after the end of combat step of the turn it began in; `UntilYourNextTurn` when its controller's next turn begins, or when they leave the game; `UntilEndOfYourNextTurn` in the cleanup step of the first turn its controller has after the one it began in (`effects::ends_with_turn`, which the cleanup asks for both durations that end with a turn), or when they leave the game; `WhileSourceOnBattlefield` with the source object it names; `Forever` never. An effect whose duration has ended stops applying at once; housekeeping logs `EffectExpired` at the next stable pass.
- `Engine::add_continuous_effect` and `add_continuous_effect_from` register an effect with no card behind it, the hook the tests use for all five durations, `SetPowerToughness`, `AddType` and `SetColors`.
- Creature types (CR 205.1b, 613.1d): `Modification::SetCreatureTypes` (layer 4, "becomes a red Weird") removes the creature types the effects so far gave and sets `Characteristics.creature_types_set`, which hides the printed creature types from `Characteristics::has_subtype` (the subtype test of selectors, the chosen type and spend restrictions); land, artifact, enchantment and spell types stay (`Subtype::is_creature_type`). Turn // Burn sets the types, the colour, the base power and toughness and takes the abilities of one creature until end of turn.

## Copies

A copy effect (CR 707) gives a permanent the copiable values of another object (`copies.rs`); the rules of the data are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Copies).

- Copiable values (CR 707.2) are a `CardDefinition` of one face: the name, mana cost, colours, types, abilities, power, toughness and loyalty of what the object prints, or of the copy effect it is under, never what counters, Auras, Equipment, `becomes` effects or type changes made of it. `copiable_values(state, object, snapshot)` reads them from a live object or, once it has left, from its last known information (`Snapshot::copy` holds the values of a copy that left). A double-faced permanent is copied as the face it shows (CR 707.8, 712.8c): the copy of a transformed one has the values of its back face, is a card of one face and never turns over.
- A copy effect is the first layer (CR 613.1a). `PermanentState::copy` holds a `CopyEffect` (the object copied, the values, a timestamp), and `GameObject::card`, `whole_card` and `base_types` return its values in place of the card's, so the layers start from them and every other effect applies on top whatever its timestamp. A later copy effect replaces an earlier one (CR 613.7). The effect lasts while the object is on the battlefield, since a zone change makes a new object (CR 400.7). `Characteristics` stay derived.
- `Event::BecameCopy { object, of, definition }` begins a copy effect on a permanent: the fold gives it the next timestamp and notes what the values can bring (`copies::note_definition`: triggers, statics, replacements, a legend).
- The exceptions of a copy effect (CR 707.9b) are applied to the values when the effect is made (`CardDefinition::except`): an extra type or subtype, a keyword, a granted ability, colours, a fixed power and toughness. Any other modification in an `except` list is refused with `Effect.CreateToken.Copy.Except`, and the walker names it `Copy.Except.<Mod>`.
- Token copies (CR 111.10): `TokenRef::CopyOf { of, except }` makes tokens whose definition is the copiable values of each object `of` names (one token per object and per count) as `TokenCreated` with `copy_of` set. The token is a new object with its own timestamp, summoning sick, and has none of the counters or damage of the original.
- Enters as a copy (CR 614.1c, 707.9): `ReplaceBy::AsCopy { of, except }` is a self-replacement of an `Enters`. The controller of the permanent chooses with `ChooseCards` (zero or one of the permanents `of` selects; nobody is asked when none can be copied, since the battlefield is public) and the batch gets a `BecameCopy` right after the move, so the permanent enters as the copy. The enters replacements of the copied values (enters tapped, with counters) apply to its entering in place of the card's own (`Origin::Copied`), and its enters triggers are the copy's (CR 614.12a).
- Populate (CR 701.34) is a `Choose` of a creature token the controller controls followed by a `CreateToken` of a copy of the chosen one; with no token nothing is chosen and nothing is created.
- Copies for a duration: `Effect::BecomeCopy` logs `BecameCopy` with `until_end_of_turn`; the `CopyEffect` is temporary and holds the effect it is over (`under`). `end_turn` logs `CopyEnded` for each permanent under a temporary effect in the batch that expires the other effects of the turn, and the permanent is a copy of what it was a copy of before, or what it prints; a lasting copy made over a temporary one replaces it. Other durations are named `Copy.Duration.<name>` by the walker.
- Copies of spells (CR 707.10): `Effect::CopySpell` logs `SpellCopied { object, of, controller, definition, choices }` for each copy, in order, so the last is on top. The copy is a token spell (`ObjectKind::Token` on the stack, `GameObject::is_spell_copy`, `copied_from`): its `definition` is the copiable values of the face the original is in use as (`CardDefinition::copiable`) with the exceptions of the effect, so a copy of a split half or an Adventure is that half alone and `choices.face` is empty. Its `choices` are the original's (modes, X, optional costs, way, targets, division) less the picks of a cost it did not pay. Nothing is cast: no `SpellCast`, no payment, no cast trigger, no storm count. The copy resolves like any spell, becomes a token permanent when it is a permanent spell and otherwise ceases to exist at the move off the stack (`tokens::cease`), which is also how a countered copy ends. The returns to hand and exiles that belong to a card (buyback, flashback, Adventure) pass over it. Targets of a copy count as targeted for the turn's tallies and trigger "becomes the target" (`Happened::Targeted` reads `SpellCopied` and `AbilityCopied`).
- New targets: the controller of the effect is asked once per copy `YesNo` ("Choose new targets for the copy of Lightning Bolt?") when some specification has another choice than the targets it has, then `ChooseTargets` for each such specification with `min` and `max` the number of targets the original had and no way out (`resolve/prim/retarget.rs`). No keeps every target, legal or not (CR 707.10c). The answer to `ChooseTargets` during a resolution is logged as `ChoiceMade { Choice::Targets }`; a division follows the targets that replace the ones it was made for.
- Copies of abilities: `Effect::CopyAbility` logs `AbilityCopied { object, of, controller, choices }`; the fold clones the `AbilityObject` (source, rules, cause, bindings), so a copy of a triggered ability still reads what triggered it. A copy is neither activated nor triggered: no `AbilityActivated`, no limit used, no "whenever you activate" or "whenever this triggers".
- On the stack: what a resolution puts there (a copy, a spell cast during it) lies above the object that resolves, which leaves from beneath it; `SpellResolved` needs the spell on the stack, not on top.
- Storm (CR 702.40): the keyword adds the trigger "when you cast this spell" that works from the stack, with `CopySpell { what: This, count: Triggering }`. The amount of a cast (`Cause.amount`) is the number of spells every player cast before it this turn, counted from the turn's tallies when the spell is cast. A storm trigger that resolves after its spell has left the stack copies nothing (`o2c`).

## Rules of the game

Rules that change how the game is played rather than the objects in it (`Effect::Rule`, `Ability::Rule`; [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md), Triggers, replacement and layers).

- `rules.rs` gathers the rules in force once for a state: the resolved `Effect::Rule`s that still last (`GameState.rule_effects`, logged by `RuleCreated` and ended by `RuleExpired` in the cleanup step or at the next stable pass, with the durations of continuous effects) and the `Ability::Rule`s that permanents have (read through `ability_reader`, so a permanent that lost its abilities lost its rules) and that spells on the stack print for themselves (`from: Stack`). The result is kept until an event that can change what the layers read (`GameState::rules`); a game with no card that prints a rule and no rule effect gathers nothing.
- A rule names players or objects through references. A static ability finds them whenever the state changes (`You` the controller, `Each(selector)` over the objects as the layers left them, `Attached` the host, the `condition` judged each time); an effect fixes them when it resolves (CR 611.2c), and one that names nothing begins nothing. The engine's `Rule` holds `Vec<ObjectRef>` or `Vec<PlayerId>`, and a rule of an effect that names objects is spent when none of them is the object it named.
- Each rule has one reader, asked by the one place that decides it: `must_attack` and `must_block` when the declaration is offered (`AttackOptions.required`, `BlockOptions.required`, the creatures that are able; `check_attackers` and `check_blockers` refuse a declaration that leaves one out, a blocker only while it could still be added), `cant_be_countered` by `Effect::Counter`, `cant_gain_life` by `Effect::GainLife` and lifelink, `cant_draw` by `steps::draw` (the draw step and `Effect::Draw`: no event, so an empty library loses nothing), `cant_search` by `Effect::Search` of a library (no question and no `CardsFound`; the effects after it go on), `GameState::revealed_top` by the views, and `triggers_again` by trigger detection.
- Cast permissions (`GameRule::MayCast`): the engine's `Rule::MayCast` lists the players, the cards it covers, the zone and whether it waives the mana cost or the timing; `rules::cast_permissions` is the one reader, asked by `ways::of`. `only` limits it to the objects the rule named when it began (found by `Scope::objects` in the resolving effect, so a bound name works): a card that leaves its zone is a new object that `Cast::covers` no longer accepts, and `RuleEffect::spent` ends the rule when every object it names is gone. A rule that names no card begins nothing.
- Impulse draw (`Effect::ExileTop` and the `MayCast` rule of `effect::impulse`): `ExileTop` exiles the top cards of the library face up (a short library gives what it has, an empty one nothing, and `ZoneChanged` to exile is public) and binds them as the objects they become in exile. The permission covers a cast from exile for the mana cost at the timing of the card (a sorcery-speed card only on its controller's turn in a main step with an empty stack; flash and instants any time they could be cast), paid like any cast, so cost modifiers apply; a card without a mana cost has no way to be cast. With `play` it also covers a land: `ways::lands_elsewhere` lists the lands in exile it covers, and `priority_options` offers them beside the lands of the hand when the active player is in a main step with an empty stack and has a land play left (`land_plays_per_turn`, CR 305.2). `Event::LandPlayed` takes the land from the zone it is in, a hand or exile, and the land is the player's. The permission ends with its duration (`UntilEndOfTurn` or `UntilEndOfYourNextTurn`, logged as `RuleExpired` in the cleanup step) and covers only its controller.
- Trigger doubling: when a triggered ability of a permanent has triggered, each rule in force that belongs to the permanent's controller and whose `cause` pattern matches the event adds one more `Triggered`, judged against the state the event is judged against (a death against the state before, so a Teysa that dies with the others doubles their triggers). An ability limited to once each turn does not trigger again, and a delayed trigger is not an ability of a permanent.
- Declarations that do only what the rules require: `AttackOptions::required_attack` and `BlockOptions::required_blocks`, which the bots, the server's clock fallback and the testkit's passive answers use; `BlockOptions::could_join` says whether a creature left out could have been added.
- The top card of a library whose owner plays with it revealed (`GameState::revealed_top`) is shown to every seat: `is_shown_to` is true for it, so snapshots and batches list it, and `PlayerView.revealed_top` names it.

## Combat forecast

`forecast::combat_forecast(&GameState) -> CombatForecast` says what the combat damage still ahead would do if dealt now. It is derived from the state on every call and never stored.

- It plays the damage steps on a copy of the state with the code of the real game: `damage_events` deals a step, the events fold, and state-based actions, the game-end check and housekeeping settle before the next step. A keyword is in the forecast because it is in the damage, and the forecast cannot drift from it. An attacker whose controller has not divided yet deals the default division; once `DamageAssigned` is recorded the forecast uses the recorded one.
- Per attacker: the damage it assigns (one packet per blocker in declared order, or one to the defender when unblocked, and with trample one to the defender after the blockers) and the damage its blockers deal back. A creature that deals damage in both steps has both in one packet.
- Per creature in combat: the damage it takes and whether state-based actions take it off the battlefield (lethal damage or deathtouch, unless it is indestructible or a shield regenerates it; an effect that exiles it instead still counts).
- Replacement and prevention: every batch of the copy (the start of a step, the damage, each round of state-based actions) goes through `replace::process` as `Engine::commit` does (`replace_dry.rs`), so the amounts are what is dealt after the effects: doubled by Furnace of Rath, stopped by a shield (what it has left carries into the second step), by protection or by Fog, the life a lifelink creature gains doubled by a life doubler, a regenerated creature alive, an exiled one gone. The effects read are those of the battlefield: one that works from a hand or a library is not, so a forecast every seat shares shows no hidden card.
- A question the step would ask a player (which effect applies first, which damage a shield stops first) is answered with its first option, which is what the server's clock answers. `CombatForecast.depends_on_choice` is set when another option of any one question changes the numbers (two Furnaces and two shields that stop the same total do not; a Furnace and a shield do), and is omitted otherwise. More than 12 other options are not tried and set it.
- Per attacked player: the damage, the life after, with the life a lifelink blocker gains counted, and whether that is lethal. The life an attacker's controller gains from lifelink is not listed.
- Steps ahead (`damage::passes_ahead`): both when a first or double striker is in combat and none is dealt yet, the regular one alone in the first-strike step once it has granted priority and in the regular step.
- `pending_combat_forecast` is the guarded form views use. It returns `None` before attackers are declared, with no attacker left, and once the last damage has been dealt (the combat state lingers until the end of combat, and a regular damage step that has granted priority is past its damage).

### Preview

`Engine::preview_forecast(seat, &answer)` (`preview.rs`) is the forecast of a pending DeclareAttackers or DeclareBlockers answer without giving it.

- The answer goes through `answers::handle`, the same validation and events as `apply`. The events pass through the replacement step, fold onto a clone of the state and `pending_combat_forecast` reads the clone. Nothing is re-implemented, so a preview cannot disagree with the forecast after the real answer.
- `PreviewError` says why there is none:
  - `Illegal` carries the `IllegalAnswer` that `apply` would return (an illegal block is an error, never a forecast);
  - `NotYourDecision`: the seat does not face the decision;
  - `NotPreviewable`: any other decision kind, damage assignment included;
  - `NoDecision`: the game is over;
  - `NoCombat`: an attack with nobody;
  - `Engine`: the scratch copy refused the events, which is a bug.
- The method takes `&self`, so no event is logged, no random number is drawn and the state hash cannot change. The cost is one state clone.

## Rules coverage

Deviations from the Comprehensive Rules:

- The deathtouch mark lasts until damage wears off at cleanup. CR 704.5h looks at damage dealt since the last state-based check, so a creature that is indestructible when checked and loses it later in the turn would not die; here it does.
- A creature deals damage in the regular step when it has double strike or no first strike at that step. CR 510.4 reads the keywords as the first step began, which differs only when a keyword is gained or lost between the steps.

Readings of the rules:

- The choice of how to pay a hybrid or Phyrexian symbol is made with the payment, after the total cost is worked out, not among the announcements of CR 601.2b: the total keeps its symbols and `PayMana` offers the ways to pay them.

- The legend rule asks one question for each name and each player, and the permanents of an answer are put away when it is given, so two names are two batches (CR 704.3 makes the puts of one check simultaneous).
- An Aura spell is checked as it resolves with the rule of targeting, so a creature that gained hexproof since it was chosen stops the Aura; an Aura that enters without being cast is not targeted and may enchant a hexproof creature.
- A state trigger (CR 603.8) is looked for where a player would receive priority, after state-based actions, not continuously: a state that is true only for the length of a resolution does not trigger it. Once triggered it goes on the stack whatever the state is by then, as the rules have it.
- The abilities of a card outside the battlefield are its printed ones: only a permanent has granted abilities or loses any. A static ability that works from another zone has the timestamp 0 (only permanents are stamped), and a triggered or activated ability that works from a hand or a library names its source to every seat as it goes on the stack (`protocol.md`, Visibility).
- A trigger that triggers an additional time is not repeated for an ability that triggers only once each turn: the limit counts the ability, not the events.
- A creature that must block and could still be added to the declaration (to an attacker that is blocked already or needs one blocker, or one that needs several with free creatures to join it) must be in it. The maximum number of requirements (CR 509.1c) is judged by adding creatures, never by moving blockers from one attacker to another.
- A static ability that is given applies only in the layers after layer 6: the rules order the layers in sequence and do not say what the earlier layers of a given ability do.
- An attacker divides damage freely among several blockers. The Foundations rules removed damage assignment order (CR 509.2, 510.1c); lethal damage only matters for trample, whose excess needs lethal damage on every blocker.
- Lethal damage for trample counts the damage marked and deathtouch; it does not count damage other creatures assign in the same step, since a blocker blocks one attacker.
- A mana ability resets the passes in a priority round, a literal reading of CR 117.4 (all players pass "without taking any actions in between"). No official ruling exists and the impact is low.
- Triggers that cannot differ (one source, one ability) go on the stack in the order they triggered; the rules leave the order of such triggers to their controller, and no choice is visible.
- An effect that says a player wins the game makes every other player in the game lose at once, so the check after the resolution finds one player left (CR 104.2a, 104.2b). One effect that names two winners leaves no one standing and the game is a draw.

Not implemented: loyalty abilities that cost -X, planeswalker tokens and copies, keywords other than those of combat, flash, hexproof, shroud, protection, enchant, ward, prowess, battle cry, kicker, multikicker and cascade, copies of objects (a token that copies one), Commander, and any effect beyond drawing, discarding, milling, sacrificing, destroying, exiling, countering, tapping and untapping, attaching, the library effects, life, damage, mana, tokens, winning the game, conditions that `If` and an intervening "if" can read (not divided amounts) and the continuous effects and static abilities of the layers ("counter unless its controller pays" included), and the rules of the game beyond those it plays (a requirement to attack a given player or to be blocked); costs of activated abilities other than `{T}`, mana and sacrificing the source; mana abilities that add several mana at once or count something, and a source that sacrifices itself offered while paying a spell; X, modes, divided amounts and other announcements. The other keyword actions are stubs. A card revealed and moved to a hidden zone within one batch (no question between) is never listed in the views of the seats that were not its owner: the batch lists the objects visible after its last event. Mana abilities that work from another zone than the battlefield are not implemented. Replacement: `Prevent` and `Instead` are not run (no shields, regeneration or redirection), a token that is created does not enter through `Enters`, and a batch that waits for an answer is lost if the engine is rebuilt from its log, which then asks the question that preceded it. Scenarios cannot start in the blockers or damage steps.
