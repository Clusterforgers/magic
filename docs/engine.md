# Engine

Design notes for `mtg-engine` (the rules engine) and `mtg-testkit` (scenarios, bots and invariants): the invariants of the rules engine and where it keeps things. Per-keyword and per-effect behaviour lives in the code (`keywords/`, `resolve/prim/`, `announce/`) and its rustdoc; the wire form of the types is in [protocol.md](protocol.md).

## Shape

- `Engine` offers `new`, `resume`, `apply`, `concede`, `decision`, `decisions`, `apply_for`, `state`, `events`, `state_hash`, `add_continuous_effect`, `add_continuous_effect_from`, `add_rule`, `add_rule_from`, `checkpoint`, `restore` and `preview_forecast`; `replay(setup, events)` rebuilds a state from a log.
- One `Event` enum and one `apply_event` fold: the state is the fold of the events. Characteristics are derived from base values plus continuous effects (layers) and never mutated directly.
- A game has two seats or more and no most: a seat is a `PlayerId(u64)` index into the players of the state, turn order and priority run seat by seat in APNAP order, commander damage is a map keyed by (commander, victim), and no set of players is a fixed-width mask, so the only ceiling is memory (`tests/it/many_seats.rs` plays 8 to 24 seats).
- Each `Decision` kind carries its own legal options and has an `Answer` of the same name, with its options, its answer and its check in `choose/<kind>.rs`. The kinds: Mulligan, BottomCards, Priority, PayMana, DeclareAttackers, DeclareBlockers, AssignDamage, Discard, and the questions of spells and abilities (ChooseTargets, ChooseMode, ChooseNumber, DivideAmount, ChooseCards, OrderCards, Scry, YesNo, ChooseOption, ChooseCosts, OrderTriggers, ChooseReplacement).
- `choose::sole_answer` is the one answer the rules leave a question whose options are all in sight of every player (the targets a triggered ability must take, the only mode, number, option or replacement effect); every other kind and any question that can be backed out of has none. The table gives it for a seat out of Full Control ([server.md](server.md), Windows).
- `FormatRules` is a trait with two implementations, `Normal` and `Commander`; the engine reads the values a table can override through `RuleSet` ([House rules](#house-rules)), never from the trait.
- `caps()` names the primitives the engine runs, in the vocabulary of the card support records ([cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md)); `RUNS` in `caps.rs` is the one list. A card is never Verified for a primitive that is not on it.
- `MulliganOptions` states the mulligan costs from the resolved mulligan rule (`free_mulligans`, `can_mulligan`, `bottom_if_keep`, `bottom_if_mulligan`), so clients repeat these counts and never work them out.
- The opening asks every seat at once (`Engine::decisions`, `mulligan::open_decisions`): keep or mulligan, the cards to bottom and the scry concern only the seat's own hand, so `apply_for` takes the answers in any order, the log keeps that order, and a round of mulligans waits for every answer, which gives the state of answering in turn order. A seat whose final hand holds an `Ability::OpeningHand` card is then asked `ChooseCards` for the cards to put onto the battlefield (CR 103.6), answered by `OpeningCardsPlayed`.
- Seams: effects are `resolve/prim/<effect>.rs` (one per `Effect` variant; an effect with no implementation is `ResolveError::Unsupported` and has no `Caps` entry, so the card is never offered); keywords are `keywords/<name>.rs`, read through `keywords_of`; replacement is `replace::process` in `Engine::commit`, in front of the fold of every batch; a game with no replacement effect (`Possible.replacements`) passes every batch unchanged.

## House rules

- `HouseRules` (`mtg-core`, `house.rs`) is what a game's creator sets in place of the format's values. Every field is an `Option` (`None` keeps the format's value) except `handicaps`, a list that is empty when none is set. `validate` names the first field out of range (`HouseRuleError`), and `validate_for(seats)` also refuses a handicap for a seat the table lacks. The rules and their ranges are the fields of `HouseRules` and `validate`.
- `GameSetup::house_rules` is checked by `GameSetup::validate` against its seat count (`SetupError::HouseRules`), copied into `GameState` and hashed beside the format name: a game under other rules has another state hash, and a replay needs the setup it was played under.
- `RuleSet` (`GameState::ruleset`, `GameSetup::ruleset`) is the format with the house rules over it and the one place the engine reads these values. The values of a seat (`starting_life`, `mulligan_rule`, `max_mulligans`, `mulligan_options`) read its handicap first. `FormatRules` supplies what a format may change (`starting_life`, `mulligan_rule`, `skips_first_draw`, `max_hand_size`, `poison_threshold`); `None` from `max_hand_size` is unlimited. `max_mulligans` never exceeds the seat's opening hand size plus the free mulligans, so a hand never bottoms more cards than it holds.
- A new rule is one field of `HouseRules` with its range in `validate`, one method of `RuleSet` and the call sites that read it, a protocol/web entry ([protocol.md](protocol.md)) and a table test.
- Rules that need more than a number: `turn_limit` ends the game at the end of the last turn of a round (`TurnState::round`; highest life wins, a tie is a draw); `winning_life` makes the others lose with `OpponentReachedLife` (several at it at once are a draw); `empty_library` `reshuffle_graveyard` logs `LibraryRefilled` with the whole order; `deck` (`DeckRules`) relaxes the deck check of a format with a command zone and `commanders_per_seat` the partner limit, both through `FormatRules::validate_seat(deck, commanders, rules)`; `attack_range` limits the players and planeswalkers in `AttackOptions` (`RuleSet::defending_seats`) over the seats still in the game; `open_hands`, `revealed_library_top` and `opening_hand_smoothing` change what is revealed or dealt ([protocol.md](protocol.md), Visibility); `mulligan_style` is `london` (the format's), `paris` or `vancouver` (Paris plus a scry 1 after keeping a mulligan hand, `MulliganStatus::Scrying`); `mana_burn` logs `ManaBurned` after `ManaPoolEmptied`; `commander_tax` and `commander_damage` are read by the cost modifiers and `Commander::loss_condition`.
- Card rules that bear on the same limits: `Rule::NoMaxHandSize` and `Rule::MaxHandSize` (`max_hand_size` in `rules.rs`), `Rule::NoLegendRule` (`legend::groups`), `Rule::UntapDuring` (`untap_during`), and `Rule::SkipDrawStep` (read in `next_step`: the draw step never begins, CR 614.10).

## Commander

`Commander` (`format/commander.rs`, CR 903) is a `FormatRules`; the commanders and what is counted for them are in `commanders.rs` and `GameState::commanders`.

- Setup: `PlayerSetup::commanders` are the cards a seat starts with in its command zone, one or a Partner pair; the other cards are its deck. A format without a command zone refuses commanders (`SetupError::NoCommandZone`). `FormatRules::validate_seat` checks the seat under the game's `RuleSet` with a `DeckError` per rule: commander count, eligibility (`CardDefinition::can_be_commander`), partner pairing (`partners_with`), size 100, copies (`singleton_limit`) and colour identity. [deck-check.md](deck-check.md) describes the same rules from the report side.
- The partner keywords and `Rule.CanBeCommander` are deck-building rules read from the definition when a seat is checked; they have no hook in play. Partner with also adds a triggered ability (`keywords/partner_with.rs`). The colour identity of a pair is the two together.
- Colour identity is derived, not stored: `CardDefinition::color_identity` is the union over all faces of the colour indicator, the mana cost colours, the symbols in the cost and mana of every ability the engine runs, the symbols in the clauses left to the table (reminder text excluded) and the basic land types. A corpus test holds it equal to Scryfall's `color_identity` for every corpus card.
- Rules: 40 life, the London mulligan with the first mulligan free (CR 103.5c), the first draw skipped only in a two-player game (CR 103.8), `ExtraZone::Command` and the public command zone `Zone::Command(seat)`.
- A commander is an object id in `GameState::commanders`, fixed at setup; the id stays through every zone change, so what is counted for a commander survives them. A token and a copy are other objects and no commander. The commanders, casts, damage and refusals are hashed only once they hold something.
- Casting: `ways::of` offers a commander in its owner's command zone for its mana cost at the timing of its type. The tax is a cost increase added with the other increases in `announce/modifiers.rs` before the reductions: `commander_casts` times `RuleSet::commander_tax`, counted by the fold of `SpellCast` when the spell lay in a command zone.
- Back to the command zone (CR 903.9): a commander in a graveyard or exile is a question of the state-based actions in `flow.rs` after the legend rule (`YesNo`; yes is a `ZoneChanged` to `Zone::Command`, no is `CommanderStayed`). A commander that would be put into a hand or library, or drawn, is a replacement candidate of `replace.rs` (`Origin::Commander`); a drawn commander moved to the command zone is no `CardDrawn`, so draw triggers and the tally do not see it. The commander that returns is a new object.
- Commander damage (CR 903.10a): the fold of `DamageDealt` to a player counts the amount per (commander, player) while the damage steps deal it; `Commander::loss_condition` loses a player at `RuleSet::commander_damage` from one commander (`LossReason::CommanderDamage`), so the two commanders of a pair are counted apart.

## Companion

A companion (CR 702.139, `companions.rs`) is a card a seat names before the game and keeps outside it; the rules are implemented once here and the cards only carry `Keyword::Companion(DeckCondition)`.

- Setup: `PlayerSetup::companion`. `FormatRules::validate_companion` checks it with the seat: the card has a companion ability the engine reads and the starting deck, the commanders included, meets the condition (`DeckError::NotACompanion`, `CompanionCondition`); Commander also keeps the companion inside the commanders' colour identity (`deck_minimum` is 100 there). A scenario does not validate.
- The condition is `mtg_cards::companion::DeckCondition` (`Each`, `Distinct`, `ExtraCards`) over selectors that read the card alone; `validate` refuses any other predicate. One evaluator (`DeckCondition::check` over `DeckCard`s) serves the engine and the deck check.
- State: `GameState::companions` keeps each seat's card as a definition with the object id it will have in the hand, and a stage (`CompanionStage`: `Hidden`, `Revealed`, `Brought`). The card is no object while it lies outside the game, so no zone, invariant or effect sees it. The hash covers the stages.
- Reveal: the opening reveals every hidden companion right after `FirstPlayerChosen` and before the first shuffle (`Event::CompanionRevealed`).
- The action: `PriorityAction::BringCompanion`, offered as `PriorityOptions::companion` when the card is revealed and still outside, the holder could cast a sorcery and can pay {3} (cost modifiers do not apply). It shares the announcement of suspend and foretell (`announce/companion.rs`, `Special::Companion`): `PayMana` with a way back, then one `Event::CompanionBrought` that creates the object in the hand. The stack is not used and nothing can respond.

## Determinism and recovery

- Random choices use a ChaCha8 substream keyed by (seed, counter in state). Events carry the results (a shuffle logs its whole order, a roll its result), so replay needs no RNG. A fold that draws a substream moves the counter on.
- A half-finished cast (spell chosen, payment pending) lives outside game state; a rebuilt engine asks for priority again.
- Answers are validated before any mutation. A fold failure rolls back by replaying the log, and `checkpoint`/`restore` expose that rollback so the server can undo a step it failed to persist.
- `concede` is legal at any time (CR 104.3a): `PlayerLost { Conceded }`, then the normal elimination path (the player's objects leave at once, even in the middle of a resolution, the effects that gave them control end, what they still control is exiled, the game ends when one player remains). A half-finished cast is dropped.
- A turn whose active player has left goes on without one (CR 800.4): `GameState::active_player` is `None` while `TurnState::active` keeps the seat the turn belongs to. Untap, draw, attack declaration and the hand-size discard are skipped; every step that grants priority starts with the next player in turn order (`active_or_next`); cleanup ends the effects until end of turn, and the next turn goes to the next player. A game of two ends at once. The table view says it with `TurnView.active_left`.
- An effect whose duration has ended stops applying at once; the later `EffectExpired` event only prunes it.

### Fail-soft resolution

- A card in state `Read` runs rules no checker proved ([cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md)), so its errors must not stop a game. When a step of a spell or ability whose source definition has `read` returns a `ResolveError`, the engine keeps the steps already folded, drops the rest, logs `AbilityLeftToTable {source, controller, text}` (a note: no state change) and goes on as after a resolution, so the table resolves the ability by hand. The same error from any other card stays an `EngineError`, which the tests must catch, except `Unsupported`: it is left to the table for every card, so no game stops on a primitive the engine lacks.

## Mana costs

- `ManaCost` (`mtg-core`) is a canonical list of `ManaSymbol`. Canonical means sorted, generic symbols merged, `{0}` dropped and a colour pair stored the way cards print it. Equal costs are equal values, `Display` then `parse` is the identity, and the list is also the wire form (deserializing normalizes).
- `mana_value` counts `{2/W}` as 2 and every other choice as 1 (CR 202.3). `colors` gives a hybrid symbol both colours. Any symbol the type does not model (snow, silver-border) is `ParseManaCostError::Unsupported`, named and never skipped.

## Payment

- Payment goes through resolutions (CR 601.2b). `ManaCost::resolutions()` lists every way to pay each hybrid and Phyrexian symbol as a plain cost plus the life paid. `can_afford`, `auto_payment`, `check_payment` and `complete_payment` take the payer's life total and try the resolutions; a payment is valid when some resolution takes exactly its life and its mana. Hybrid and Phyrexian symbols are chosen with the payment, after the total cost is worked out, not among the announcements of CR 601.2b.
- A permanent is one tap, whichever of its abilities is used. `can_afford` and `auto_payment` match coloured needs to distinct permanents. `auto_payment` prefers mana to life, then the least life, then the fewest taps, then taps of the colours the player has most of, so a hybrid symbol keeps the scarce colour untapped. It pays life only when no mana can, and never down to 0 or less; a payment chosen by hand may take all of one's life (CR 119.4).
- A payment covers the cost and has nothing to spare: no floating mana, tap or part of one can be left out and still cover it (`spent_of`). A source adds `count` mana for one tap (`mana_plan.rs`), and mana beyond the cost floats.
- Sources that cost mana, sacrifice, take other permanents or cards (`ManaOption.cost`, `takes`, `repeat`, `tapless`) are sources of a payment only (`PriorityOptions.mana_abilities` leaves them out). `check_payment` finds an order in which every cost is paid from floating mana or from mana earlier sources added (`schedule`, `Spent.steps`), and the events follow it. Planners search backwards (`mana_cost::auto`, at most `CHAIN` sources that cost mana) and `mana_takes::solving` re-plans without sources that cannot take; `mana_repeat::expand` gives each further use of a repeatable source a copy so every function of `mana.rs` plans over distinct permanents. The independent check in `mana.rs` tries every order, assignment and way to pay and compares them with the four payment functions; keep it equal when adding a source shape.
- Restricted mana (`SpendOn`, `Restricted`, `spend.rs`) stays in `ManaPool` with what it allows until the pool empties; `Spend` says what a payment is for; the four payment functions read restricted mana as plain mana only where `Spend` allows it, and `spend_tests.rs` compares them with a check of every payment. A payment for an effect (`Pay`) takes only unrestricted mana. Restricted mana is spent first. Riders on mana (`SpendOn::When`, `Snow`, `Uncounterable`) restrict nothing and fire when the cast spends the mana (`spend_rider.rs`, `mana_snow.rs`). A use that names an ability source may require that it is on the battlefield (`SpendFact::OnBattlefield`).
- Kept mana (`AddMana.lasts`: `Step`, `Combat`, `Turn`) is counted in `ManaPool.counts` and `ManaPool.kept`; a spend takes the mana that goes soonest first; `ManaPoolEmptied.through` names the shortest lifetime that empties what went; mana burn counts only the mana that went.
- Convoke, improvise and delve (`help.rs`) help pay after the total cost is fixed; their offer (`Help`) excludes every object the additional costs of the cast take; the `*_with` payment functions take the offer; `help_tests.rs` compares them with a check of every combination.
- `PayChoice::Complete(partial)` validates the partial parts and pays the rest with `auto_payment` over the unspent pool and untouched permanents (refused if it cannot). `PayOptions.can_cancel` is true while nothing is paid; `Cancel` then drops the pending cast with no event, so a rebuilt engine asks for the same priority decision.
- `Payment.life` is the life paid, 2 per Phyrexian symbol; it must be even, at most 2 per symbol that life can pay and at most the life total. `PayOptions.life` and `max_life` mean a client never works out the limit. The cast's events are `LifePaid` first, one event for the whole payment, then `SpellCast`, the taps and `ManaSpent`. Everything is validated before any event.
- The spell keeps which sources paid (`Choices.spent_from`, then the permanent and its snapshot): the traits of each tapped source and how many of its mana were spent, read by `Amount::ManaSpent { from }` ("mana from a Treasure", snow, Desert, Cave). The pool keeps the kind of the source of each unrestricted mana it holds (`ManaPool.sources`), so mana tapped before the cast counts; spending takes mana with a known source first.
- A keyword a static ability gives with `CostItem::OwnManaCost` (Underworld Breach: escape, scavenge, encore) reads the printed mana cost of each card in `layers.rs` when it is added; replicate and emerge read the printed card at the cast, so they do not take it.

## Priority options

`PriorityOptions` lists `playable_lands`, `castable`, `mana_abilities`, `abilities` and `can_pass`, and says in `can_act` whether the holder can do anything useful.

- `castable` lists cards in a hand and, for a way that names another zone, in a graveyard, exile, the top of a library or the command zone; it means affordable at the timing the card allows. A spell that needs targets is castable only when every target specification has enough legal targets (CR 601.2c). Split second (CR 702.61) empties `castable` and `abilities` while the spell is on the stack; mana abilities, special actions and passing stay.
- `abilities` lists the activated abilities that work from the zone the card is in, are not mana abilities and could be activated now (`priority::other_abilities`). `Activated.by` names who may activate a permanent's ability: its controller (default), any player or its opponents; the activator controls the ability on the stack, and a cost that taps or gives up the permanent is not offered to a player who does not control it. `Limit::TimesPerTurn(n)` counts the uses of an ability in the turn (`Tallies::limit_uses`).
- `unplayable` names each card of the holder's hand and command zone that no list offers, with its first reason (`Unplayable`); the hand and command cards are judged once (`unplayable::judge`) and the judgement also fills `castable`. `suspendable` and `foretellable` list the special actions.
- `can_act` is true with a land to play, a spell to cast, a card to suspend or foretell or such an ability. Mana abilities alone never count. One function holds the rule (`priority::can_act`); the server uses the flag only to pass for a seat that asked for it ([server.md](server.md)). The response clock and the yields never read it, so the pace of a table never tells what a hand holds. `PlayOptions::auto_pass` in `mtg-testkit` applies the same rule in a bot game and is the reference the server tests compare with.

## Manual actions

A table plays any card, also one the engine runs only in part, with its controller doing by hand what the engine does not (`manual.rs`; [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md), Compiler and Manual fallback).

- A card the engine does not run is cast and resolves without the clauses it leaves out: a spell goes to the graveyard, a permanent enters with the abilities the engine does run. Its definition lists the left-out clauses as `CardDefinition.manual` (`ManualClause {text, reason}`, per face), which every seat reads. An Aura with a clause left to the table waits unattached on the battlefield until its controller attaches it by hand.
- `PriorityAction::Manual(ManualAction)` is the seam for the seat with priority; `Engine::manual(seat, &action)` takes the same action out of band, from any seat in the game at any decision of any seat. The action needs no entry in `PriorityOptions`.
- Application point: `Engine::manual` runs only where the engine rests at a decision, never inside a resolution step; it is refused before the first turn (`NotStarted`), once the game is over, for a seat out of the game and while a replacement question holds a batch (`Settling`). The events are committed like an answer's and the pending decision is recomputed. A cast or activation in progress gives its answers so far again against the new state and the first that no longer fits is asked again (`announce/again.rs`); one whose card, source or attacker the action took away is rewound without a trace (`announce/anchor.rs`). State-based actions follow a manual action at once when no resolution or announcement is under way, otherwise as that ends.
- Replay: a manual action is logged as `Event::ManualAction {player, action}` and its events, at the position it was applied; `replay` and `resume` fold the same log to the same state. Only `Shuffle` and `Roll` draw a random number, logged whole. `manual::check(state, seat, &action)` says whether an action is allowed and why not (`ManualRefusal`, carried by `IllegalAnswer::Manual`); a refusal leaves the log, the state and the question as they were. An opponent's card, life and counters are changed by the opponent's own seat.
- The events of an action are built from the same events the effects use (`ZoneChanged` with `ZoneCause::Moved`, `CountersChanged`, `LifeGained`, `TokenCreated`, `CardDrawn`, ...), so the replacement step, trigger detection and state-based actions treat them as any other. A move by hand is no draw and no discard. The action itself changes nothing but the round (`reset_passes`): the seat keeps priority and the others pass again. The actions are the variants of `ManualAction`; library looks and reveals record what a seat may identify in `GameState.looked_by_hand` and `revealed_by_hand` (read by `is_shown_to`).
- The bots never take a manual action. `mtg-testkit` has `ManualScript`, `PlayOptions::manual_every`, `Driver::manual` and `manual_fuzz_setup`; `ShownByHand` (run by the invariant checker) holds that a card revealed from a hand is identified to every seat while it stays there.

## Spells and abilities

The rules of the pipeline are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Execution); this section says where the engine keeps them.

- Objects: a `GameObject` has a `kind` (`Card`, `Token`, `Ability`) and a zone-change counter the fold bumps on every move (CR 400.7); `ObjectRef { id, counter }` names one incarnation. An ability on the stack is a `GameObject` that holds an `AbilityObject` (`abilities.rs`): its source, a snapshot of it, its index among the source's rules, an `Arc` to those rules and a trigger's cause.
- Reading abilities: `ability_reader` is the one place that reads the abilities of an object (a test searches the engine sources for a read of a card's `rules` outside it). `abilities_of(state, id)` lists them with their addresses and `ability(state, address)` reads the one an address names. An address is `AbilityRef { object, ability, source }`, with `AbilitySource::Printed` by default; a permanent has the printed abilities less those other effects took away, then those they gave it in layer order (`Granted(Grant)`, `PrintedKeyword`, `GrantedKeyword`); a keyword built from triggers adds the abilities of its `adds_abilities`. `ability` reads from the data, so a granted ability stays readable after its effect has ended, which an ability on its way needs. A permanent may be given an activated, triggered, static, rule, replacement or cost-modifying ability (CR 613.1f); a granted rule or replacement works from the battlefield, and the quick checks before rules and cost modifiers are gathered (`may_hold`) count the granted ones. A pending trigger, a delayed trigger and an ability on the stack hold the ability itself, so each runs as it was whatever becomes of the object it came from (CR 603.10a, 113.7a).
- Counters and attachments live in `PermanentState`; `GameState::host_of` names the host only while it is still the very object on the battlefield (CR 400.7). `CountersChanged` is refused for a change of nothing, a removal of more than there are and a non-permanent; a kind that falls to none is no longer listed.
- Snapshots (`snapshot.rs`) are last known information. `PutOnStack`, `Cause` and `StepDone` carry them, and the value evaluator reads an object live while its counter matches and from the snapshot otherwise.
- Targets: `legal_targets(state, spec, source, controller)` (`choose/targets.rs`) serves choosing, `priority_options` and the recheck on resolution, and asks the keyword hook `protects`. The first step of a resolution binds the targets legal as it begins (`~legal:<index>`, CR 608.2b); the later steps act on those that have not changed zones.
- Resolution: `stack::resolve_top` begins the top object of the stack. While `GameState.resolving` is set, `flow::next_in_turns` takes the next move from `resolve::step` before state-based actions and priority (CR 704.3), so a resolution is never cut by a priority round. The objects of a player who left the game come first of all (`sba::departures_first`). A step is a pure function of the state and its journal; the events it returns end with `StepDone`, which clears the journal.
- Rebuilding: the journal is state, so an engine resumed from a log stands at the same step, and one resumed from the middle of a step asks the same question again. Every question a step asks is checked against its options (`NotOffered`, `Duplicate`, `WrongCount`), logged as `ChoiceMade` and read back from the journal when the step runs again. A choice with more than one possible answer is always asked; a choice that must take everything is not.
- Random picks (modes, `ChooseAtRandom`, random discards, bottom orders) draw the next substream and log the result, so a replay draws nothing.
- Library effects: libraries are listed bottom to top, and every list of cards in an event or an answer reads from the top down. A step that asks one kind of question of several players reads the journal through `pick_selections`; one that mixes questions walks them in one fixed order through `Questions` (`choose/ask.rs`). All players decide before any library changes.
- What a seat may identify beyond the zones it sees into: `Resolving.shown` records the cards of `LibraryLooked`, `HandLooked`, `CardsFound`, `Scried` and `Surveilled` for the seat that did them and those of `Revealed` for every seat, for the object it names while the resolution lasts (CR 400.7); `sight::offered_cards(state)` names the cards of the question the resolution waits on. Both are functions of the state alone, so a state rebuilt from the log gives the same views.
- Destroying, exiling and countering name their objects with `Scope::live_objects` (the objects a reference names that are still the objects it named, each once) and propose one move per object in one step. Each step carries a snapshot of what it moved, and `Scope.departed` keeps a target that an earlier step moved named for the steps after it. A token ceases to exist in `move_object`, the one place every zone change folds.
- Source memory (`memory.rs`): `GameState::memory` maps (source object, slot) to what the source remembers, a list of `Held` (an object, a player or a number): `Effect::Remember` holds objects and players, `Effect::RememberValue` a number that `Amount::Remembered` adds up, `Forget` empties the slot. A number lives for the resolution that wrote it (also for an ability whose source has left) and is cleared when that resolution ends. The `Remembered` event carries the objects and the players and numbers (`values`). It is part of the state hash only while non-empty, and a zone change of an object ends its place in any other memory.
- Costs of activated abilities (`Price`, `price.rs`; parts in `parts/`) are paid when the ability is activated (`cast::activation_events`); an activation that costs nothing or only `{T}` commits when its last question is answered; one with mana asks the payment last. The permanent whose `{T}` is paid, or that is sacrificed, is not one of the sources of its own mana. A draw or a discard at random is a part (`parts/draw.rs`, `parts/random.rs`): payable only while the library or hand holds the cards of every payment at once, and a random pick is logged as `ChosenAtRandom` before its discards.
- Paying in an effect (`Pay`) asks `YesNo` of the players it names; a player who cannot pay is asked the same question with `YesNoOptions::only_no`, so the table and the clock do not show whether they could pay; a Yes is refused before anything is logged.
- Mana abilities: the plain `{T}: Add {X}` is a printed ability of the card; any other mana ability stays in the rules of its card, and `mana_shape` (`mtg-cards`) reads the ones the engine runs.
- Cards with several faces (`CardDefinition.layout`, `faces`), face-down objects (`GameObject::face_down`), merged permanents (`merged.rs`), flip, prepare and Rooms (`dk1c5_rooms.rs`) are modelled in the files named; the public/hidden rules of each are in [protocol.md](protocol.md), Visibility. `announce/face.rs` asks which face to cast first (601.2a).
- A new mechanic enters through one seam: an `Effect` variant with its `resolve/prim` file and `Caps` entry, a keyword file, or an `Event` with its fold and a `redact` rule.

## Announcement

`announce/` is the one procedure that puts a spell, an activated ability or a triggered ability on the stack (CR 601.2, 603.3). What the player has begun (`Pending`) lives outside the state, so nothing is logged until the payment is accepted and backing out is free. `announce(state, pending)` runs the steps of `Phase::ORDER` in turn and returns the first question (`Next::Ask`), the events that put the object on the stack (`Next::Commit`) or `Next::Drop`; a step with no question passes to the next. The steps: face (601.2a), modes, alternative cost (`ChooseOption` when more than one way to cast can be used), optional costs (before the modes for entwine), parts of a cost (`ChooseCards`), X, targets, division, legality, total cost, payment.

- What a card asks beyond modes and targets (an alternative cost, optional costs, an `{X}`) is `Needs`, read from its rules when the announcement begins (`Needs::of`). Its ways to cast come from `ways::of` (`Way`: the alternative cost or none, the label, the mana paid in its place, the zone); with one usable way the announcement takes it, with more it asks `ChooseOption`.
- Answers: `Pending::accept` keeps an answer in `Choices` for the step that asked and refuses an answer for any other step.
- Total cost: the mana cost of the spell or the cost of the ability, replaced by an alternative cost, plus the mana of the optional costs chosen and X generic mana for each `{X}`, then the cost modifiers. Increases are added first and reductions taken away after (CR 601.2f); a generic reduction stops at no generic mana. The payment asks `PayMana` for that total only when the player can pay it. The priority decision offers a spell by its cost with the modifiers applied before anything is announced (`announce::cheapest`).
- X: `ChooseNumber` offers 0 up to the largest X whose total cost the player can pay. A spell keeps X in its choices and the permanent it becomes keeps it in `PermanentState.x`.
- Rewind: a step that cannot be met rewinds the announcement as a whole (CR 601.2): a mode or target specification with nothing to offer, an X or a total cost the player cannot pay, a division that cannot be given.
- Cast during a resolution (`Effect::CastFromZone`, CR 608.2g) uses the same procedure with a granted way; special actions (suspend, foretell, companion, unlock) share it as `Special`.
- Alternative and additional costs, casting restrictions (`Ability::CastOnly`), free casts and the per-keyword ways (`CastWay::*`) are read in `ways.rs` and `announce/required.rs`; each keyword's details are in its `keywords/<name>.rs`.
- Assist (`announce/assist.rs`) is a step of the announcement after the total cost: the caster picks a player, that player picks an amount (`ChooseNumber` that cannot be backed out of, default 0), the amount comes off the generic mana and the helper's mana is spent in the same batch as the caster's payment. Prototype is `GameObject::prototype` (cost, colours, size by the cast way); Sneak puts the creature in the attack as the spell resolves (`sneak.rs`); Craft is a cost pick over the battlefield and the graveyard (`Kind::Craft`) and a return of the exiled card (`Ref::Arrived`).

## Replacement

The rules of the data are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Triggers, replacement and layers); this section says where the engine keeps them.

- `Engine::commit` is the one place events are applied: it passes the batch to `replace::process`, folds what comes out and logs it. The batch of a resolution step is whole (its events and `StepDone`), so a step is applied or asked about as a unit.
- A question of the step (`ChooseReplacement`, a yes or no, a colour or a creature type) is the engine's decision. `Engine` holds the batch and the answers so far (`replacing()` is true), and an answer reruns `process`, which asks the same questions in the same order. The answers are not logged. A batch that a decision cannot recompute (a land played, an ability's cost) is lost with the engine's memory; one that the state recomputes (a resolution step, state-based actions, combat damage, a draw) is asked again.
- `process` reads the replacement effects once for the batch (`gather`) and looks at each item in turn. The candidates that apply and have not been applied to the item are ordered by address; effects about their own object go first; one candidate is applied, more are asked. Applying changes the item or replaces it with new ones, which carry the effects already applied. Each `ReplacementCandidate` carries `text`, the words of its own effect.
- Shields are `RuleEffect`s (`Rule::PreventDamage`, `Rule::Regenerate`, `Rule::DiesInstead`) made by `Effect::Shield` and `Effect::Regenerate`.
- The state-based checks of the testkit skip a decision of the step: the events it is about are not applied yet.

## Triggered abilities

The rules of the data are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Triggers, replacement and layers); this section says where the engine keeps them.

- Detection: `Engine::commit` folds a batch of events, then calls `triggers::detect(pre, events, post)`. The steps of an `Effect::Together` (a loop or a damage map chain that gathers its moves and damage) fold one by one and are detected as one batch after the last (CR 603.2c, 603.10a); an engine rebuilt from the log inside the batch finds its start again (`recover_batch`). Events become `Happened` values; those that look back are judged against the state before the batch, so permanents that go together see each other go. A player who loses in the same batch triggers nothing (CR 800.4a).
- A trigger carries its controller (whoever controlled the source as it triggered), the subjects of the event with snapshots (`Ref::Triggering`) and a snapshot of the source. `limits_spent` holds the (object, ability) pairs used this turn and is cleared by `TurnBegan`.
- Turn counts are tallies kept by the fold (`Tally::*`, `TurnTally`), cleared with the turn.
- The sweep: `flow::next_in_turns` runs it after state-based actions and the game-end check and before the turn structure, so it precedes every priority decision (CR 117.5). State triggers (`triggers/state.rs`, CR 603.8) are asked for there, only when a card has one.
- A trigger that triggers while others are going on the stack belongs to the next round (CR 603.3b). A trigger whose target specification has too few legal targets (CR 603.3d), or whose controller or source has left the game, is removed (`TriggerRemoved`). Nothing goes on the stack in the untap step.
- Resolution: an ability object keeps its trigger's cause, source snapshot and (for a delayed one) the names it took. An intervening "if" is judged by `Scope::holds` when it triggers and again as it resolves. Delayed triggers are created by `DelayedTrigger` (`DelayedTriggerCreated`); their pattern may name what the resolution bound.
- Events: `AbilityTriggered`, `TriggersOrdered`, `TriggerRemoved`, `DelayedTriggerCreated`, `DelayedTriggerExpired`; visibility in [protocol.md](protocol.md).

## Combat and keywords

`keywords::keywords_of(state, id)` is the one reader of the keywords an object has: the printed ones in the order of its rules, then those continuous effects grant (layers). A keyword is implemented once, as a hook the engine asks at the place that decides (`keywords/<name>.rs`):

| Hook | Keyword | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `restricts_blocking`, `restricts_blocked`, `answers_evasion` | flying, reach, fear, intimidate, skulk, shadow, horsemanship, landwalk | `stops_block` in `block_options` |
| `min_blockers` | menace | `block_options`, `check_blockers` |
| `forbids_attacking` | defender | `can_attack` |
| `ignores_summoning_sickness` | haste | `can_attack`, mana and `{T}` abilities, the views |
| `attacks_untapped` | vigilance | `attack_events` |
| `modifies_damage` (`DamageRules`) | first strike, double strike, deathtouch, trample, lifelink, wither, infect, toxic | `damage.rs`, the fold of `DamageDealt` |
| `adds_abilities` (a `defining` static) | changeling, devoid | `layers::outside` and the layers pass |
| `is_indestructible` | indestructible | state-based actions; the `Destroy` primitive |
| `restricts_targeting`, `restricts_player_targeting` | hexproof, shroud | `legal_targets` through `protects` and `protects_player` |
| `protects_from` (`Holder`, `Source`) | protection | `keywords::protected_from`: damage in `replace.rs`, `legal_host` in `attach.rs`, `stops_block`, `legal_targets` |
| `grants_timing` | flash | `priority_options` through `grants_instant_timing` |

- Keywords that are triggers or activated abilities (prowess, battle cry, ward, equip, reconfigure, living weapon, crew, cycling, ...) return DSL abilities from `adds_abilities`; the rest of the engine reads them as printed abilities. Each instance adds its own.
- Damage steps: the first-strike step occurs when an attacker or a blocker has first strike or double strike as blockers are declared (`damage::first_strike_step_needed`). An attacker divides damage freely among several blockers (`damage::division`); `DamageOptions.lethal` is toughness less damage marked, at most 1 from a deathtouch source, and `excess` the player or planeswalker attacked under trample. Deathtouch marks the creature in the fold and state-based actions destroy it; lifelink is `LifeGained` in the same batch as the `DamageDealt`, for combat and the `DealDamage` primitive alike.
- Declarations: the engine lists `AttackOptions.required` and `BlockOptions.required` (CR 508.1d, 509.1c), `min_blockers`, attack and block limits and taxes (`rules.rs`, `attack_tax.rs`); the declaration must meet them or the engine refuses it. A creature that must attack with no player to attack is not required to.
- Removal from combat (CR 506.4): a creature whose controller changes, an attacked planeswalker that leaves (the attackers keep attacking nothing, `Attacker::attacks_nothing`) and `Effect::RemoveFromCombat` are folded into the combat state.

## Continuous effects

`Effect::Continuous` makes the effects of a resolved spell or ability (CR 611); the rules of the data are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Triggers, replacement and layers).

- A `ContinuousEffect` (`effects.rs`) lives in `GameState.effects` and holds its id, layer (none for a restriction), selector, modification, duration, timestamp and source. Creation fixes the permanents the reference names as `Selector::Objects(Vec<ObjectRef>)` (CR 611.2c); `Selector::Objects` matches the very incarnation named, so a permanent that leaves and comes back is a new object that keeps nothing.
- Derivation: `layers.rs` works through the layers once for a state, for every permanent at once, and keeps the result until the next event (`LayersCache`, dropped by `apply_event` for every event but those of `layers::leaves_alone`). Within a layer, dependencies (CR 613.8) are chosen again after each effect applies. A given static ability applies only in the layers after layer 6.
- Static abilities apply in the layer of each modification, stamped with the timestamp of their source; a static ability that names a zone other than the battlefield affects the cards there. Characteristic-defining abilities (`defining`) work in every zone. A copy effect is the first layer.
- Control: `controller_of` is the controller after layer 2; `GameObject::controller` is the one the object entered under.
- Restrictions (`CantAttack`, `CantBlock`, `DoesntUntap`, `CantBeBlocked`, limits) have no layer and are read through `characteristics` (`cant_attack`, `cant_block`, ...), asked by `can_attack` and `block_options`.
- Durations (`ContinuousEffect::is_active`): `UntilEndOfTurn` ends in the cleanup step in the batch that removes damage (CR 514.2), so a creature that a pump kept alive survives it. `Engine::add_continuous_effect` and `add_continuous_effect_from` register an effect with no card behind it, the hook the tests use.
- Counters in layer 7c: counters named for their numbers change power and toughness; keyword counters grant the keyword. A permanent with both a +1/+1 and a -1/-1 counter loses as many of each as it has of the kind it has fewer of (CR 704.5q).

## Copies

A copy effect (CR 707) gives a permanent the copiable values of another object (`copies.rs`); the rules of the data are in [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md) (Copies).

- Copiable values are a `CardDefinition` of one face. `PermanentState::copy` holds a `CopyEffect` (the object copied, the values, a timestamp); `Event::BecameCopy` begins one. Exceptions of a copy effect (CR 707.9b) are applied to the values when the effect is made (`CardDefinition::except`). A copy that has left the battlefield is a new object that prints its card again, so its abilities come from last known information (`Snapshot::copy`).
- Token copies are `TokenRef::CopyOf`; copies for a duration end with `CopyEnded` (`until_end_of_turn`, `until_next_turn_of`).
- Copies of spells (CR 707.10) are `SpellCopied` tokens above the object that resolves, which leaves from beneath it; the controller is asked once per copy whether to choose new targets. Copies of abilities clone the `AbilityObject` (`AbilityCopied`).

## Rules of the game

Rules that change how the game is played rather than the objects in it (`Effect::Rule`, `Ability::Rule`; [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md), Triggers, replacement and layers).

- `rules.rs` gathers the rules in force once for a state: the resolved `Effect::Rule`s that still last (`GameState.rule_effects`, logged by `RuleCreated` and ended by `RuleExpired`) and the static ones. A rule names players or objects through references that a static ability resolves whenever the state changes.
- Each rule has one reader, asked by the one place that decides it (`must_attack` and `must_block` when the declaration is offered, attack taxes in `attack_tax.rs`, cast permissions through `Rule::MayCast` and `rules::cast_permissions` (`spend_as` lets mana of any type or color pay: `ways.rs` prices the cast with `ManaCost::paid_by_any_mana`, or `paid_by_any_color`, which keeps `{C}`, for any color), prohibitions through `prohibit::forbidden`, land plays through `Rule::AdditionalLandPlays`, trigger doubling when a trigger has triggered). A new rule is a variant with its reader; a rule with no reader does nothing.
- Creature and permanent words are `CombatQuirk`s of `GameRule::Combat` (`combat_quirks.rs`): assigns no combat damage, assigns damage by toughness, may assign damage as though unblocked (CR 510.1c; `DamageOptions.unblocked`, answered with no damage to any blocker), may stay tapped (CR 502.3).
- The untap step asks the active player one `ChooseCards` question (min 0, `steps::untap_events`) listing every tapped permanent that may stay tapped and would untap, whatever the hand; the answer is the permanents that stay tapped, the clock default untaps all, and no such permanent means no question.
- `Duration::WhileSourceTapped` ends a continuous effect when its source untaps or leaves (CR 611.2b); the housekeeping that follows removes it at once.
- Declarations that do only what the rules require: `AttackOptions::required_attack` and `BlockOptions::required_blocks`, which the bots, the server's clock fallback and the testkit's passive seats use.
- The top card of a library whose owner plays with it revealed (`GameState::revealed_top`) is shown to every seat; `Rule::LookAtTop` makes it known to its owner alone.
- A life total that can't change (`GameRule::LifeTotalCantChange`, CR 119.7) stops gain, loss, lifelink and mana burn.
- `GameRule::ManaProduced` (`mana_replace.rs`) changes the mana of a tap (CR 106.6): `ManaChange` sets the type, sets one mana of a type, multiplies, or converts one type. It rewrites the options `mana_ability::options` offers after the triggered mana abilities fold in, so the payment planner and the activation agree; it never uses the stack.
- `GameRule::MayCastRather` gives the owner of each exiled card a cast for another mana cost (airbend): the way replaces the mana cost (`CastWay::Alternative`), and the card is cast for no other cost.
- `Ref::Granter` is the permanent whose static ability gave the running ability to its source ("this Equipment" in the words an Equipment gives): `AbilitySource::granter` names it, the `Scope` of a resolution and the `Price` of an activation carry it, and a cost pick (`Sacrifice` of `Is(Granter)`) finds it. It reads nobody for an ability the source has of its own or once the granter has left the battlefield.
- `Ref::OrController(r)` is the player `r` names, or the controller of the permanent it names ("target player or planeswalker", then "that player or that planeswalker's controller").
- `Pred::CanEnchantSource` holds for an Aura (or Equipment) card that `attach::legal_host_entering` lets attach to the source.
- "You may cast this as though it had flash if you pay {N} more" is an `AltCast` (`CastWay::Alternative`, `flash`) for the printed cost plus {N}; a printed {X} has no such way.
- A waterbend cost (`CostItem::Waterbend`) is generic mana that untapped artifacts and creatures help pay as improvise does (`help::waterbend`); only the cost of an activated ability offers the helpers, any other use is an unpaid part.

## Phasing

A permanent that phases out (CR 702.26) is treated as though it does not exist until it phases in; `phasing.rs` holds the rules.

- A phased-out permanent is in `Zone::PhasedOut`, a zone of its own beside the battlefield: it is still an object with its `PermanentState`, but no effect, rule or trigger sees it as on the battlefield. `PermanentPhasedOut` and `PermanentPhasedIn` change the zone and `PermanentState::phased`; Auras, Equipment and Fortifications attached phase out with it (`indirect`).
- The untap step begins with `phasing::in_events` for the active player (CR 502.1), then the Phasing keyword phases out its permanents.
- `TriggerPattern::PhasedOut` looks back at the state before the event; `PhasedIn` is judged after it. An effect that names a permanent that has phased out since passes it over. A phased-out permanent is public ([protocol.md](protocol.md)).

## Extra turns and phases

The turn sequence is the fixed run of steps (`turn.rs`, `steps.rs`) with two queues over it (CR 500.7, 500.8).

- `GameState::extra_turns` holds the extra turns still to take (`ExtraTurn { player, skip_untap }`), the latest added last; the turn that ends takes the last. `TurnState::ahead` lists the phases still to begin after the current one, the added ones first. A skipped untap step does not begin (CR 614.10). A player under `GameRule::SkipExtraTurns` skips each extra turn (`ExtraTurnSkipped`). The state hash adds these fields only when a game uses them.

## Combat forecast

`forecast::combat_forecast(&GameState) -> CombatForecast` says what the combat damage still ahead would do if dealt now. It is derived from the state on every call and never stored.

- It plays the damage steps on a copy of the state with the code of the real game: `damage_events` deals a step, the events fold, and state-based actions, the game-end check and housekeeping run between steps; every batch of the copy goes through `replace::process` as `Engine::commit` does. A question the step would ask (which effect applies first, which damage a shield stops first) is answered with its first option, which is what the server's clock answers; `depends_on_choice` is set when another option would change the numbers.
- Per attacker: the damage it assigns; per creature in combat: the damage it takes and whether state-based actions take it off the battlefield; per attacked player: the damage, the life after (with a lifelink blocker's gain counted) and whether that is lethal.
- `pending_combat_forecast` is the guarded form views use: `None` before attackers are declared, with no attacker left, and once the last damage has been dealt.

### Preview

`Engine::preview_forecast(seat, &answer)` (`preview.rs`) is the forecast of a pending DeclareAttackers or DeclareBlockers answer without giving it.

- The answer goes through `answers::handle`, the same validation and events as `apply`; the events pass through the replacement step, fold onto a clone of the state and `pending_combat_forecast` reads the result.
- `PreviewError` says why there is none: `Illegal`, `NotYourDecision`, `NotPreviewable`, `NoDecision`, `NoCombat`, `Engine` (the scratch copy refused the events, a bug).
- The method takes `&self`, so no event is logged, no random number is drawn and the state hash cannot change.

## Verification

- Invariants (`InvariantChecker`, `mtg-testkit`): per-event checks run on every event; whole-state checks and the replay hash comparison run every 25 answers and at game end. Every bot-game campaign runs under every invariant and replays a seeded game twice.
- Scenarios (`mtg-testkit`, `Scenario`, `Driver`) set up a state, script answers and assert; mechanics are tested over tables of cases at two to four seats, not single cards. A new decision, event or view field comes with a table test, a fuzz campaign over a deck that uses it, and a `hidden_info` case in `mtg-protocol` if it can leak.
- Fuzz: seeded bot games for 2, 3, 4 and 6 players. Each campaign plays a small default number of games (the seeds `0..n`, so a failure repeats) and reads `MTG_FUZZ_GAMES` through `fuzz_games` in `tests/it/common/seeds.rs` for the long run (`MTG_FUZZ_GAMES=500 cargo test -p mtg-testkit --test it`; `MTG_FUZZ_FROM=1000` shifts the seeds to `1000..1000+n`). A campaign plays its seeds on one thread when `RUST_TEST_THREADS` is set and on every core otherwise (`MTG_SEED_WORKERS` overrides).
- Runaway games: `Watchdog` in `mtg-testkit` counts the answers of a game and of a turn and the permanents of a bot game and panics with the seed, the turn, the last answers and the decks. The bots stop casting and activating once the battlefield holds more than `CROWDED_TABLE` permanents, and `RandomBot::choose_in` holds back a repeated free activation that changes nothing, so a token engine or a free loop ends by combat or decking. With `PlayOptions::runaway_fails` a stopped game ends as `FailureKind::Runaway(report)` instead of a panic.
- Smoke gate: `smoke.rs` plays one card in bot games at 2 and 4 seats per seed (`smoke_card`, [cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md)) with bots that favour the card, `runaway_fails`, and counts its casts, resolutions, activations, triggers and land plays from the log.
- Crowded tables: an answer costs time in proportion to the permanents on the table; keep the layers (`LayersCache`), `block_options`, attachments and trigger detection (`Watchers`) from scanning per permanent. `crowded_turn.rs` bounds this.
- `RandomBot::new` keeps its choices fixed (cancel when allowed, automatic and exact payments) because seeded fixtures such as the web transcript replay its games; `with_partial_payments` and `with_symbol_choices` opt into more.
- Independent checks: the payment functions are compared with an exhaustive search (`mana.rs`, `spend_tests.rs`, `help_tests.rs`); the forecast with reality in bot games; the deck colour identity with Scryfall's for the corpus; a replay of the log at every point with the live state hash (`pipeline_log.rs`).
- Test files in `mtg-testkit` are named for the mechanic they cover (`tests/it/<mechanic>.rs`, with `<mechanic>_fuzz.rs` for its bot-game campaign); read the file's `//! Goal:` line.

## Rules coverage

Deviations from the Comprehensive Rules:

- The deathtouch mark lasts until damage wears off at cleanup. CR 704.5h looks at damage dealt since the last state-based check, so a creature that is indestructible when checked and loses it later in the turn would not die; here it does.
- A creature deals damage in the regular step when it has double strike or no first strike at that step. CR 510.4 reads the keywords as the first step began, which differs only when a keyword is gained or lost between the steps.

Readings of the rules:

- The legend rule asks one question for each name and each player, and the permanents of an answer are put away when it is given, so two names are two batches (CR 704.3 makes the puts of one check simultaneous).
- An Aura spell is checked as it resolves with the rule of targeting, so a creature that gained hexproof since it was chosen stops the Aura; an Aura that enters without being cast is not targeted and may enchant a hexproof creature.
- A state trigger (CR 603.8) is looked for where a player would receive priority, after state-based actions, not continuously: a state that is true only for the length of a resolution does not trigger it. Once triggered it goes on the stack whatever the state is by then.
- The abilities of a card outside the battlefield are its printed ones: only a permanent has granted abilities or loses any. A static ability that works from another zone has the timestamp 0, and a triggered or activated ability that works from a hand or a library names its source to every seat as it goes on the stack ([protocol.md](protocol.md), Visibility).
- A trigger that triggers an additional time is not repeated for an ability that triggers only once each turn: the limit counts the ability, not the events.
- A creature that must block and could still be added to the declaration must be in it. The maximum number of requirements (CR 509.1c) is judged by adding creatures, never by moving blockers from one attacker to another.
- A static ability that is given applies only in the layers after layer 6: the rules order the layers in sequence and do not say what the earlier layers of a given ability do.
- An attacker divides damage freely among several blockers. The Foundations rules removed damage assignment order (CR 509.2, 510.1c); lethal damage only matters for trample, whose excess needs lethal damage on every blocker. Lethal damage for trample counts the damage marked and deathtouch; it does not count damage other creatures assign in the same step.
- A mana ability resets the passes in a priority round, a literal reading of CR 117.4.
- Triggers that cannot differ (one source, one ability) go on the stack in the order they triggered; no choice is visible.
- An effect that says a player wins the game makes every other player in the game lose at once, so the check after the resolution finds one player left (CR 104.2a, 104.2b). One effect that names two winners leaves no one standing and the game is a draw.
- The choice of how to pay a hybrid or Phyrexian symbol is made with the payment, not among the announcements of CR 601.2b.
