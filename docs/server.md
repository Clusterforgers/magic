# Server notes (M2c)

Design record for the `mtg-server` game actor. Rules of the house are in `CLAUDE.md`; the wire contract is in `protocol.md`.

## Shape
- `spawn_game(id, setup, store)` starts a game in its lobby; `resume_game(store, id, setup)` rebuilds one from its log. Both return a cheap-clone `GameHandle` (`join(seat) -> Receiver<ServerMessage>`, `send(seat, ClientMessage)`, `leave(seat)`, `status()`) and must run inside a tokio runtime.
- One task per game owns the `Engine`, the `EventStore` and a bounded queue per seat. A full or abandoned queue drops that client, which reconnects and receives a snapshot; the actor never waits for a client.
- Lobby: joins, `Ready`, `NotReady` and chat. Readiness is one flag per seat in `Phase::Lobby { ready, names }`, never stored: it outlives a connection (a seat that is ready and away stays ready), `NotReady` takes it back, and a `Ready` or `NotReady` after the start is ignored. The last `Ready` creates the engine and broadcasts the opening events from seq 0. A resumed game goes straight to running; one with an empty log returns to the lobby with nobody ready.
- Lobby roster (`lobby.rs`): `ServerMessage::Lobby { seats: [{seat, name, connected, ready}], needed }` goes to every connected seat after its `Welcome` and again whenever the table changes: a seat joins, a client goes away, `Ready` or `NotReady` flips a flag. `needed` is the seats not ready. A repeat that changes nothing says nothing, a connection that takes a seat over from another is the only one sent the roster, and the `Ready` that starts the game sends none (the opening events are the news; a start that fails does send one, with everybody ready). A hang-up sends nothing to the actor, so queues that closed are looked for after every command (`announce_departures`; `Seats::take_gone` remembers a queue dropped by a sweep, a send to a closed queue or a full one, so a lookup or chat line that meets it first cannot hide it) and, while the lobby is idle, waited for (`Seats::client_gone` in the actor loop). A client dropped for being too slow is told to the others in a second roster. Names are what the game was made with or what a claim gave (`Registry` passes them to `spawn_named`; a game spawned directly calls its seats "Seat N"); an open seat is listed with an empty name, not connected and not ready, so it counts in `needed` and the game cannot start while one is open. A claim or a release sends the roster (see Open games). `GameStatus.ready` and the REST `ready` list the flags (every seat once the game runs).
- One step: check seat, `DecisionId` and engine legality, run `apply` or `concede`, project for the connected seats, append to the store, then send the events, the next `Decision` (only to the seat it names) and `GameOver`. A store or projection failure restores the engine from its `Checkpoint` and answers `Error{Internal}` without details.
- Join and `Resync` send `Welcome`, a `Snapshot` and the seat's pending decision. The game waits for disconnected seats; what it does for a seat by itself is the passes its windows give (at once for a seat outside its windows or that yields, after its dwell for a seat that cannot act at a default window) and the answer at the deadline of a response clock (Pace of play). A decision held for a dwell is not part of what a join or `Resync` sends.

## Pace of play (`windows.rs`, `stops.rs`, `yields.rs`, `pace.rs`)
- **Windows.** `windows::verdict` answers "ask, pass at once or pass after a dwell" at each priority decision from the table (the step, whose turn it is, the stack), the seat's stops, its Full Control mode, its yield and `can_act`. A seat that is passed for is never sent the decision. The rules, in the order they are judged (the module doc lists them):
  1. Full Control (`NextWindow`, `Held`) asks at every window; a yield and the default windows never pass for such a seat.
  2. A running yield passes at once; a stop of the seat at a window ends it, with the usual `Yielding` notice, and the seat is asked. The step of the window the yield was asked at is the exception: the seat has seen that window, so ending the turn from a stop passes it.
  3. A stop asks, whether the seat can act or not. `Own` stops are for the seat's own turns, `Opponent` stops for the turn of any other seat.
  4. The default windows ask a seat that can act. One that cannot is passed for after the dwell (below), except in its own main phase with an empty stack, where it is passed for at once.
  5. Elsewhere the seat is passed for at once, whatever it holds.

  | Default window (asked when the seat can act) | On the seat's own turn | On another seat's turn |
  |---|---|---|
  | Precombat main, postcombat main | yes | no |
  | Declare attackers, declare blockers | yes | yes |
  | Any step with an object of another seat on the stack | yes | yes |
  | Upkeep, draw, beginning of combat, first-strike damage, combat damage, end of combat, end step, with no such object | no | no |

  The unit tests of `windows.rs` check the verdict for every combination of turn side, the 13 steps, stack (empty, the seat's own spell, another seat's), stop, mode, yield and `can_act`, and for each kind of window in a table.
- **Auto-pass is the mode.** `ClientMessage::AutoPass { enabled: false }` puts the seat in `Held`; `enabled: true` takes a seat out of `Held` (to `Off`) and leaves `Off` and `NextWindow` as they are. `Welcome.auto_pass` is true exactly when the mode is `Off` or `NextWindow`, and `MTG_AUTO_PASS=0` starts every seat in `Held`. A change is told as a `FullControl` message; `AutoPass` has no reply of its own.
- **Stops and modes** (`stops.rs`). `ClientMessage::SetStop` and `ClientMessage::FullControl` fill a per-seat set of stops and a per-seat mode kept in the actor (`stops: Stops`, not stored: a resumed game starts every seat in the default mode with no stop, and a reconnect keeps them). `Welcome.stops`, `Welcome.full_control`, `ServerMessage::Stops` and `ServerMessage::FullControl` tell the seat (`protocol.md`). A stop names a step where a player gets priority (`Step::grants_priority`; untap and cleanup are refused, set or cleared, with `Error{BadMessage}` and no resync). A seat holds one stop per side and step: setting a held stop again replaces its `keep` and tells the seat the new list, clearing ignores the request's `keep`, and a request that changes nothing says nothing. Setting takes no engine step: nothing is saved and the pending decision keeps its id. The lobby accepts both (they hold for the game); a seat that is out of the game, or a game that is over, gets `Error{BadMessage}` (except `AutoPass`, which is ignored).
- **Clearing and reverting.** A one-turn stop (`keep` false) goes once the game has left its step on its side; a lasting one stays until cleared. `Stops::left` follows the `TurnBegan` and `StepBegan` events of each engine step, so a step entered and left in one go counts (declare attackers with nobody to attack) and a step that is never entered does not; a stop set while its step runs covers that step. `NextWindow` goes back to `Off` when the seat passes priority, whether it passes itself or the table passes at its clock. Both apply to the step's copy of the stops, so the passes after the step already meet them.
- **Mode changes.** Turning Full Control on (`FullControl`, or `AutoPass { enabled: false }`) ends a running yield (`Yielding` is told first, then `FullControl`), and while it is on a `Yield` is refused with `Error{BadMessage}`. Turning it off while the seat faces a priority decision that the windows now pass for passes at once, through the same unit as a started yield (`put_in_mode`).
- **One unit.** An answer, the passes after it and the save are one unit (`publish_with`): it starts from copies of the yields and the stops (modes included), applies the clearing and the revert of the step, runs `Running::skip_idle` (the passes, appended to the step), projects once, appends once and sends one batch per seat. The copies replace the seats' own only once saved, so a failed save restores the engine and keeps yields, stops and modes as they were; the sender gets `Error{Internal}`. The run stops at a decision the windows ask, at a window that waits for its dwell, at any other decision, at the end of the game and after `MAX_IDLE_PASSES` (500) passes, after which the decision is sent as it is (and the yield of its seat ends with a notice). Seats that left the game, and every seat once it is over, hold nothing; they are told after the events and the yield notices and before the next decision or `GameOver`. A game resumed from its log, and a scenario that starts on a decision the windows pass for, are settled the same way before any client connects (`start_up`).
- **Reference.** `Windows` in `mtg-testkit` is the rule written plainly and apart from the server. `PlayOptions::auto_pass` plays bot games under the default windows (off: every seat in `Held`, a bare engine) and `PlayOptions::bot_windows` lets each bot choose its stops and mode (`RandomBot::windows`, sent to the server as `SetStop` and `FullControl` in the lobby). `tests/windows.rs` compares the actor's log, hash and result with it for 2 to 4 seats.
- **Yields.** `ClientMessage::Yield { until }` (`Resolve`, `EndOfTurn`, `MyTurn`) and `CancelYield` set and end a per-seat yield kept in the actor (`yields: Yields`, not stored: a resumed game starts with none; `Welcome.yielding` and `ServerMessage::Yielding` tell the seat, see `protocol.md`). The rules that decide when a yield stops are all in one function, `Active::ends_at` in `yields.rs` (the module doc lists them), judged at each priority decision of the seat from the table alone: `Resolve` when the stack is empty, `EndOfTurn` when a turn other than the one it was asked in has begun, `MyTurn` when such a turn is the seat's own, any yield when another seat has put an object on the stack that the seat has not passed on, and any yield at a window where the seat holds a stop, except in the step of the window the yield was asked at (`Yields::start_facing`). They never look at `can_act`, so a seat that can respond and one that cannot are passed for and stopped at the same moments. Once a yield has ended, the other rules decide at the same decision.
- A yield is passed for through `Running::skip_idle`, like the other passes: `set_yield` starts the copy and, if the seat faces a priority decision, runs an empty step through the same path, so the decision is passed at once; if the yield ends there (`Resolve` over an empty stack) the decision is issued again with a new id and nothing is passed, so a client that dropped its prompt is never left without one. A yield set while the seat has no priority decision pending is just stored and told. Every seat whose yield changed is sent `Yielding` after the batches and before the decision. When the 500-pass cap cuts a run at a priority decision of a yielding seat, that seat's yield ends (with a notice) and the decision is sent. A `Yield` before the game starts, after it ended, from a seat that is out, or from a seat in Full Control is `Error{BadMessage}`; `CancelYield` with nothing to cancel says nothing.
- **Response clocks.** A decision gets its clock when it is issued (`Running::settle`, `Pace::clock_for`): a `Priority` decision while the seat is not the active player, `MTG_RESPONSE_SECS` (default 10 s), whether the seat can act or not, and `DeclareBlockers` of a defending seat, `MTG_BLOCK_SECS` (45 s); `0` switches a clock off and nothing above a day is accepted. Every other decision (mulligan, bottom, discard, payment, attackers, damage division, whatever the active player is asked) has none and waits as long as it takes. The clock does not depend on the mode, runs whether the seat is connected or not, is stopped by any answer, and starts afresh when a game is resumed from its log.
- The decision message carries the time left in `deadline_ms` (absent without a clock): the whole clock when it is issued, and what is left when the decision is sent again after a join or `Resync`, so a client's own clock never matters.
- At the deadline the actor answers for the seat with the safe default, `Running::fall_back`: a pass, or no blocks. That is `Running::answer` for the seat, so the move is checked, saved, projected and followed by the passes like the seat's own; the decision's id is gone afterwards, so a late answer from the client is the ordinary `StaleDecision`. The actor's loop is a `select!` between its next command and the `due` time of the pending decision, commands first: a command already waiting was sent before the clock ran out. When the move cannot be saved the engine is restored, the seat is told `Error{Internal}` like for any failed save, the decision stays and its clock moves one second ahead, so a failing store is retried at a pace and never spun on.
- **Dwell** (`dwell.rs`). A seat that cannot act at a default window other than its own main phase with an empty stack, and holds no stop, no Full Control and no yield (the verdict `Dwell`, `Window::dwells`), is neither asked nor passed for at once: a pass at once shows that it holds nothing. The waiting windows are the declare attackers and declare blockers steps, any window with an object of another seat on the stack, and the seat's own main phase with any object on the stack; nowhere else does a seat wait. `skip_idle` stops there (`Halt::Dwell`) and `Running::settle` holds the decision back: it is not sent, a join or `Resync` sends the snapshot alone, an answer for it is stale and a preview null, and its `due` is the dwell instead of a response clock. At `due` the actor's loop calls `expire`, which passes for the seat through `fall_back` as a step, an append and a batch of its own, so every other seat is sent the stream of a seat that could act and passed.
- **The wait.** Uniform between 250 and 700 ms, drawn from a generator of the game that the operating system seeds, independent of the game seed and of the state. `MTG_DWELL_MS` sets it: `250-700` (the default), one number for a fixed wait, `0` for none; a value above 5000 or one that is not a number or a range refuses to start. `Pace::dwell` carries it; `Pace::MANUAL` and the test configs use `Dwell::OFF`. Every idle seat waits a dwell of its own, drawn independently when it receives priority: how long a seat waits never depends on the seats before it or on how they passed, so a table of idle seats waits one dwell per seat.
- **Wall clock only.** The log, the event order and the state hash are those of a game without a dwell, and a game resumed from its log starts the wait of its first idle window afresh. The wait is a timer of the actor's loop between steps, never inside a save. A pass that cannot be saved at the end of a dwell restores the engine, tells the seat `Error{Internal}` and is tried again a second later, like a clock.
- **A seat during its dwell.** A command of its own that changes its situation ends the dwell and the rules decide again: a stop at the window, Full Control or auto-pass off sends the decision at once (the `Stops` or `FullControl` notice first), a yield passes at once, and a concession that removes the seat or ends the game ends the dwell with it. Chat, a stop at another step, `Resync`, a stale answer and the commands of other seats leave it as it is.
- **Hides.** Whether a seat could act in a combat window (declare attackers, declare blockers) or while a spell waits on the stack, in the batches and the arrival times that every other seat sees, whether those seats can act themselves or are idle too. `tests/pace_leak.rs` plays two games with one hidden card apart, an instant the holder can cast against one it cannot afford, through an attack that ends the game: the other seat is sent the same messages in the same batches, 250 to 700 ms apart, and the same comparison finds the difference with the dwell off. The leak test grows a stack-window case when instants land.
- **Does not hide.** A seat's own main phase with an empty stack: a land or a spell played there is in plain sight, a seat that can act is asked and one that cannot is passed for at once, so the other seats wait for the first and not for the second (the leak test shows it). How long a seat that can act takes: a pause past 700 ms or a pass before 250 ms is the seat's own. A pass at the deadline of a response clock, and what a seat chooses with stops, Full Control and yields.
- **Cost.** Each idle seat of a combat or response window waits 475 ms on average and 700 at most, so a step with N idle seats costs N dwells and a combat (declare attackers and declare blockers) 2N.
- Tests use tokio's paused time (`start_paused`, `time::advance`, the `test-util` feature in the dev-dependencies): `tests/response_clock.rs`, `tests/idle_dwell.rs`, `tests/pace_leak.rs`.

## Previews
- `ClientMessage::Preview` is answered by the actor itself (`preview.rs`, `Running::preview`) with one `Forecast` to the sender. The decision id must be the pending one for that seat; then `Engine::preview_forecast` judges the answer. Everything else gives `forecast: null` and never an `Error` (a stale id, a foreign one, an answered decision, a non-combat decision, an answer the engine refuses, no game yet), because the stale-decision treatment of `Answer` would raise a notice and a resync in a client whose preview raced with its real answer. Nothing is applied, projected, stored, logged or sent to another seat, so the event log, the store and the state hash stay as they were (tests compare all three).
- Rate limit: a token bucket per seat inside the actor (`PreviewLimits`; `PREVIEW_BURST` 20, then `PREVIEWS_PER_SECOND` 10), kept for the life of the game so a reconnect does not refill it. A preview over the limit is answered with a null forecast without touching the engine, so the client is never left waiting. Real answers have no bucket and queue behind previews like any message; the previews the bucket lets through cost one clone of the state each (5 to 8 µs), at most 20 in a burst, so a flood cannot starve an answer (a test sends 150 previews and an answer straight after). The socket limit (40 messages, then 20 a second) applies to every frame, previews included.
- Who may preview: every socket is bound to one seat by its credential before the upgrade and must open with `Join`, so there are no unseated or spectator sockets to refuse; a `Preview` as the first frame is refused like any other message (`Error{BadMessage}`, close 1002), and a message for a seat the game does not have is ignored.

## Contract for the transport
- The actor trusts the seat it is given. The transport must map a credential to exactly one seat and never let a client name its own.
- A replaced socket drops its receiver instead of calling `leave`, which closes whatever connection the seat has at that moment.
- `status()` exposes the state hash and is for tests and admin views, never for clients.
- `GameId::random()` (uuid v4) names every new game.
- A `GameSetup` holds an `Arc<dyn FormatRules>`, so what is stored is a serializable `GameRecipe` (format, seed, decks); see Persistence.

## Persistence
- `PgEventStore::new(pool)` implements `EventStore` over table `game_events (game_id uuid, seq bigint, event jsonb, created_at)`, primary key `(game_id, seq)`: one row per engine event, serialised with serde (`Event` as externally tagged JSON). `read_from(game, seq)` returns the tail; `load` is `read_from(game, 0)`.
- An append is one transaction holding a per-game advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(class, hashtext(game))`): it checks that the log ends at `from_seq`, then inserts the whole batch with one `unnest(...) WITH ORDINALITY` statement, so a gap, a repeat or a lost race returns `OutOfOrder` with the stored count and a failure midway leaves no rows. The primary key backs the lock up. The actor's rollback needs nothing from the store, because it restores its `Engine` from a `Checkpoint` and only appends after the step succeeded.
- Reads check that sequence numbers run without a gap and that every event decodes, else `StoreError::Corrupt`; connection and SQL failures are `Unavailable`.
- Migrations live in `crates/mtg-server/migrations` and are tracked in `_mtg_server_migrations`, not `_sqlx_migrations` (which `mtg-db` owns), so the two crates migrate one database in any order, repeatedly and concurrently. `mtg_server::migrate(&pool)` runs them; the table name is part of the on-disk contract and must never change. `sqlx::test` suites must use `migrations = false` and call `migrate`, since the default would use `_sqlx_migrations`.
- Refresh `.sqlx/` after changing a query: create a scratch database, apply `crates/mtg-server/migrations/*.sql` to it, then run `SQLX_OFFLINE=false DATABASE_URL=<scratch db> SQLX_OFFLINE_DIR=<repo>/.sqlx cargo check -p mtg-server`.
- Game records: `games (id, status, visibility, code_digest bytea, recipe jsonb, result jsonb, failure, created_at, started_at, finished_at)` and `seats (game_id, seat, name, state, token_digest bytea, joined, user_id)`, written by `GameRecords` (`user_id` is the account the seat is bound to, see Accounts; `visibility`, `code_digest` and `state` are described under Open games). `status` is `lobby`, `running`, `finished`, `expired` (the janitor dropped it) or `failed` (it could not be loaded on boot). Only the SHA-256 of a seat token is stored; tokens exist in clear once, in the create response (or the claim). `game_events` has no foreign key to `games`.
- `GameRecipe {format, seed, decks, fingerprints}` (`DeckRef::Demo{id}` or `DeckRef::Oracle{cards}`, a list of oracle ids) is the only way the server builds a `GameSetup`, for a new game and a resumed one alike; its JSON (`{"format":"normal","seed":..,"decks":[{"kind":"demo","id":..}],"fingerprints":["v1:<64 hex digits>",..]}`) is an on-disk contract, unknown fields and variants are refused, and `fingerprints` is absent in a recipe stored before it existed (serde default, and not written while empty); a server older than the field refuses a recipe that has it, like any unknown field, so a rollback past this change fails the games created since. A game made with `Registry::register(setup, ..)` has no recipe, so it is never recorded.
- `Registry::create` (now async) writes game and seats in one transaction before the actor exists, so a failed write creates nothing (REST answers 503). The actor carries a `Recorder` and notes the first join of each seat, the start (`running`) and the end (`finished`, with the result, before `GameOver` is sent). A failed write is logged and never stops a game, because the log is the truth: boot repairs a status that lags behind it.
- Resume: `Server::open(config, pool)` (or `Registry::with_database` plus `resume()`) loads every `lobby` and `running` game oldest first, rebuilds the setup from its recipe, checks the seat rows against it, calls `resume_game` and keeps the stored digests, so old tokens still open the same seats. A game whose log shows it ended is only corrected to `finished`. A recipe, seat rows or log that do not load (including a panic while replaying) and a deck that changed since the game was created (next bullet) mark the game `failed` with the reason, log it and move on; only an unreachable database stops the boot. A `failed` game is not retried; fix the row and set it back to `running` by hand. A failed game is not loaded, so tokens, `GET /api/games/{id}` and both socket routes answer as for a game that never existed (404), and `/api/me/games` never lists it.
- Lobbies survive a restart as records with an empty log: they come back as lobbies with their claims, visibility and code, everyone must send `Ready` again (readiness is not stored), and they keep their age, so one older than `Retention::lobby_after` (1 h, by the database clock) is `expired` instead. Resumed running games get a fresh 24 h without a connection. The cap `MTG_MAX_GAMES` limits creation only; every stored game resumes. When the janitor drops a game it marks the record `expired` (or `finished`), so a dropped game stays dropped.
- The binary reads `DATABASE_URL`: set, it connects (10 connections), runs `migrate`, then `Server::open`; unset or empty, it logs a warning and keeps every game in memory. `.cargo/config.toml` sets a default URL for `cargo run` (local Postgres: `docker compose up -d db`); `DATABASE_URL= cargo run -p mtg-server` runs without a database. The URL is never logged.
- Deck check (`recipe/fingerprint.rs`): `Engine::resume` applies stored events as facts, so a log would replay under other cards without failing, and a recipe names a demo deck only by id. A new game's recipe therefore also stores one `DeckFingerprint` per seat (`GameRecipe::with_fingerprints`, called by `Registry::create`): the SHA-256 of a canonical, versioned text of the ordered cards the engine is given (`mtg-deck/v1`, the count, then each card's serde JSON with sorted keys), written `v1:<hex>`. `GameRecipe::build` recomputes it and fails with `RecipeError::DeckChanged { seat, .. }` for the first seat whose cards differ in any field, in order or in size; resume then marks the game `failed` with "the deck changed: seat N (..)" before replaying anything and logs it at error level (`game`, `seat`, `reason`). A recipe stored before fingerprints existed resumes unchecked, with a warning at every boot. A new field on `CardDefinition` changes every fingerprint unless it is skipped in the JSON while it holds its default (the pinned values in the tests fail), and a new encoding needs a new version (a version this server does not read fails the recipe's decoding, never the check). A changed seed or format rule is not detected, and a changed engine only as far as it changes the definitions (next item).
- Card decks (`cards.rs`, `docs/cards-dsl.md`): `DeckRef::Oracle { cards }` lists the oracle id of every card, copies repeated. `CardSource` (the pool, the rules embedded in `mtg-cards`, `mtg_engine::caps()`) reads the distinct cards with `CardRepo::get_many`, adapts each row (`printed_card`), assembles it and returns a `Catalog` of `Arc<CardDefinition>`; an id with no row fails with `CatalogError::NotFound` naming every such id once, in deck order. `GameRecipe::build_with` and `with_fingerprints_from` take the catalog (`build` and `with_fingerprints` pass an empty one, so an oracle deck fails with `RecipeError::UnknownCards`). `Registry::create_from_recipe` assembles, fingerprints, records and starts such a game; resume loads the catalog of a recipe first and touches no card table for a recipe without oracle decks. The fingerprint covers the definitions, so a corrected card record, a changed rules file or an engine that carries more of a card fails the game at boot as a changed demo deck does. The card tables are `mtg-db`'s, migrated by its own migrator (the server's `migrate` does not create them).

## Accounts
- Tables (migration `20261018000003_users.sql`, same `_mtg_server_migrations` table): `users (id, username, username_key, password_hash, created_at)` and `sessions (token_digest, user_id, created_at, expires_at)`. `username` is kept as typed and shown as typed; `username_key` is its lower case and unique, which makes names unique ignoring case. `CHECK`s repeat the server's rules (`[A-Za-z0-9_-]{3,32}`, key = `lower(username)`), so the database refuses what the server would. A session is the SHA-256 of its cookie token (primary key, like the seat tokens), deleted with its user. Accounts need Postgres: a server without `DATABASE_URL` answers every account endpoint 503 `accounts_unavailable` and its games are guest-only.
- Rules: a username is 3 to 32 ASCII letters, digits, `_` or `-` (no trimming, no Unicode, so no look-alikes); a password is 10 to 128 characters (Unicode scalar values), compared byte for byte, never trimmed or normalised. The limits are constants in `mtg_protocol::rest`. Passwords are hashed with Argon2id (`argon2` 0.6.0, pinned exactly) and stored as the PHC string, so the cost travels with the hash and a later change of `HashCost` leaves old hashes valid. The default is the OWASP minimum (19 MiB, 2 passes, 1 lane, about 30 ms); `HashCost::cheapest()` (8 KiB, 1 pass) is for tests. Hashing runs on `spawn_blocking` behind a semaphore (`AuthConfig::hash_jobs`, 4), so a burst of requests cannot exhaust memory.
- Endpoints (bodies `Credentials` and `UserView` from `mtg_protocol::rest`, errors `{error, message}` with the codes of `AuthErrorCode`):
  - `POST /api/auth/register` {username, password} gives 201 {id, username} and signs the new account in (Set-Cookie). 400 `invalid_username` / `invalid_password` (the message states the rule, never the input), 409 `username_taken`, 403 `registration_closed` (`MTG_REGISTRATION=closed`, decided before the body is read), 429.
  - `POST /api/auth/login` gives 200 {id, username} and a fresh session. Any failure on the merits is 401 `invalid_credentials` with one fixed body: unknown username, wrong password, and a username or password that breaks the rules (which cannot belong to an account and so costs no hashing) are byte-identical. 429 `too_many_attempts` with `Retry-After`.
  - `POST /api/auth/logout` deletes the session and clears the cookie; 204 whether or not there was one.
  - `GET /api/auth/me` gives 200 {id, username} or 401 `unauthenticated`; a request that presented a cookie the server does not honour (expired, deleted) gets it cleared in the answer.
  - Signing in or registering while the browser presents a session ends that session first, so one browser holds one. Other browsers' sessions are left alone.
- The cookie `mtg_session` holds 256 random bits as hex (from the operating system's CSPRNG through `getrandom`, like seat tokens) and nothing else: `HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Path=/; Max-Age=2592000` (30 days, `AuthConfig::session_lifetime`), no `Domain`, plus `Secure` when `MTG_COOKIE_SECURE=1` (set it behind TLS). The expiry is absolute, not sliding, and is judged by the database's clock (`expires_at > now()`). An upkeep task deletes expired rows every hour (`purge_every`) and makes the stand-in hash at start.
- Login does not reveal whether a username exists: the same 401 body, and one Argon2 check either way (an unknown name is checked against a stand-in hash made at start with the same cost from a random password nobody knows, and the result is discarded). Registration does say "taken"; that cannot be avoided.
- Guessing is throttled in memory per process (`throttle.rs`): fixed windows (`AttemptLimits`, default 15 minutes) count failed logins per client address (30) and per username (10, whether or not the account exists), registrations per address (10) and, in a window of their own (`code_window`, 1 minute), join code misses per address (5; see Open games). A login is counted when it starts, so parallel guesses cannot slip under the limit, and handed back when it succeeds (the username starts afresh) or when the server could not check it (database down). Over the limit the answer is 429 with `Retry-After` even for the right password, and a blocked try is not counted. The try that uses a limit up is logged once at warn (without the name tried) and the tries turned away after it at debug, so a client that keeps knocking cannot fill the log. IPv6 addresses are grouped by /64, IPv4-in-IPv6 as IPv4. A hostile client can lock a username out for one window; the lock expires by itself. The table is capped at 50 000 counters; past that, keys not yet tracked go unlimited until a sweep frees room.
- The client address is the socket's peer. Behind a reverse proxy set `MTG_TRUSTED_PROXIES=n` (0 to 16, default 0): the address is then the `n`-th entry from the right of `X-Forwarded-For`, which the nearest trusted proxy wrote, so entries a client forged to the left are ignored; a short or malformed header falls back to the peer. With 0 the header is ignored, because anybody can send it. The server must be served with connect info (`Server::serve` does).
- Passwords, tokens, digests and hashes never reach logs or `Debug` output: `Credentials`, `SessionToken`, `SeatToken`, `TokenDigest` and the stored hash print `<redacted>` or nothing, failed attempts are logged without the name tried, and a test captures every log line at TRACE during a whole sign-up and sign-in and searches it.
- `AuthConfig` (in `ServerConfig::auth`): `registration`, `cookie_secure`, `session_lifetime`, `hash_cost`, `hash_jobs`, `limits`, `trusted_proxies`, `purge_every`; only the first, second and seventh come from the environment. A database failure is 503 `unavailable` without details (logged) and costs no throttle budget.

### Games and accounts
- Migration `20261018000004_seat_users.sql` adds `seats.user_id` (nullable, `ON DELETE SET NULL`), a unique index on `(game_id, user_id)` where it is set (an account holds one seat per game, so "the user's seat here" is one seat) and an index by user. `SeatRecord::user`, `SeatSlot::user` in the registry (bindings mirrored in memory, loaded on boot) and `NewSeat::user` carry it.
- Binding: a signed-in `POST /api/games` binds **seat 0** to the creator, the seat the web opens as "my seat"; guests bind nothing, and a cookie the server does not honour makes the creator a guest instead of an error. The first signed-in join through an unbound seat's link (`/ws/{game}/{token}`) binds that seat to the visitor's account, before the upgrade, if the game is recorded and the account holds no other seat of it. A bound seat never changes hands and a second account never binds to it: `UPDATE seats SET user_id = $3 WHERE .. AND user_id IS NULL AND NOT EXISTS (the user's other seat)` decides, so racing accounts get one winner (`Registry::bind_seat` returns `Binding::{Bound, Already, Taken, Elsewhere, Missing}`; the unique index backs the `NOT EXISTS` up). A failed binding is logged and never stops the join.
- The token stays a bearer secret. Binding adds a way in and a list; it takes nothing away, so old links keep working, no link is rotated or invalidated and a link that was shared on purpose still works. Whoever holds a bound seat's token opens that seat as before (a different signed-in account too: it plays the seat like any guest and is **not** bound, so it can never displace the owner). A seat link is therefore as sensitive as it ever was; what the account gives is rejoining without it.
- Rejoin by session: `GET /ws/{game}` (no token) with the `mtg_session` cookie opens the seat bound to the session's user in that live game; `Join` and everything after it are unchanged (protocol version stays 1). The credential rides on the upgrade request because scripts cannot read an `HttpOnly` cookie and so could never put it into a `Join`; the socket's `Origin` check applies as to the link route. Answers before the upgrade, in the order they are decided: 403 `forbidden_origin`; 404 `not_found` for an address that is not a game id, whoever asks; 503 `accounts_unavailable`; 401 `unauthenticated` (no valid session; a cookie that is no good is cleared); 404 `not_found` again for an unknown game and for a game the user holds no seat in, which are one answer, so a stranger learns nothing about which games exist. Only games alive in the registry can be rejoined: a lobby lives an hour, a finished game ten minutes after the sweep that saw it end.
- `GET /api/me/games` (`MyGames` in `mtg_protocol::rest`) gives `{games: [{game, seat, name, status, created_at, others: [{seat, name, user}], result}]}`: the games the user holds a seat in, lobby, running and finished (expired and failed ones are never listed), newest first, at most 100 (`MAX_LISTED_GAMES`). `seat` and `name` are the user's own; `others` are all other seats in seat order, each with its name and, when an account sits there, its username; `result` is a `GameResult` for finished games and null otherwise; `created_at` is `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` in UTC. It reads the database, so a finished game stays listed after the registry dropped it (and can then no longer be rejoined: the list is history for it). 401 `unauthenticated` without a session, 503 `accounts_unavailable` without a database.
- Deleting an account by hand frees its seats and ends its sessions in the database; the running registry still holds the binding in memory until the next restart. There is no delete endpoint yet.
- A game that failed to resume is not listed, so its players see no reason; listing it with the reason needs a failed variant of `MyGameStatus`, a reason field and a web row.

## Open games
- **Visibility.** `Visibility` (`public` or `invite` in JSON and in `games.visibility`; `Invite` by default, for old rows too) says who may find a game: a public game in its lobby is listed, an invite game is reached only through its seat links and its join code. `GameOptions { visibility, held }` goes to `Registry::create_with`; `create` and `create_for` make invite games that hold every seat. `Registry::set_visibility(game, by, visibility)` changes it before the start, for the host only.
- **Seats.** A seat is `Issued` (its token was made with the game and is in the create response: the seat link), `Open` (nobody holds it: no token, no account, an empty name) or `Claimed` (a join took it and was given a token). `held` is how many seats from seat 0 are issued at creation (all by default, at least the creator's); the rest are open. `seats.state` stores it, and a `CHECK` ties an open seat to a null `token_digest` and no account.
- **Claim.** `Registry::claim(game, entrance, SeatClaim { name, user })` gives the first open seat to the visitor under a trimmed name of 1 to 32 characters (the caller checks for control characters), makes its token and tells the table. It answers `Seated { game, seat, token }`; the token is in clear only there. An account that holds a seat of the game is handed it again with `token: None` (the account opens it through its session); guests have no identity and may take several seats. `Entrance::Listing` (a game id) reaches public games only and answers `NotFound` for any other, as for an unknown id; `Entrance::Code` (the caller resolved the code with `find_by_code`) reaches any game. Errors: `NotFound`, `Full`, `Started` (judged first), `BadName`, `Record`. A claim sets the name, the token and the account; the seat keeps the deck it was made with.
- **Release.** `Registry::release(game, seat, by)` opens a claimed seat again: the holder (`by == seat`) leaves or the host (`HOST`, seat 0) removes a player. The token stops working, the account is free, the connection closes, `Ready` is taken back and the table is told. An issued seat, an open seat and a seat past the table are `NotClaimed`; others than the holder and the host get `NotAllowed`; after the start `Started`.
- **One change at a time.** Claims, releases, visibility changes and `bind_seat` of one game run one after another (`Entry::turn`). A claim writes the row first (its `UPDATE` is guarded by `state = 'open'` and the lobby status, so racing claims get one winner), then names the seat in the actor (`GameHandle::occupy`, refused once the game runs) and then the registry's slot; a release empties the seat in the actor first (`vacate`), because a lobby with an open seat cannot start, so nothing begins under the change. A failed write leaves table, row and slot as they were.
- **Join code.** Eight symbols of `ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789` (capitals and digits without `I`, `O`, `0` and `1`; 5 bits each, 40 in all) from the operating system, made with the game and returned in `Created::code`, in clear only there. The registry and `games.code_digest` keep its SHA-256 (unique among games that are `lobby` or `running`; none for games stored before codes). `Registry::find_by_code(typed, address, now)` ignores case, spaces and hyphens, compares the digest of every game without stopping at a match and answers the game id or `None`; text that cannot be a code is the same `None`. Misses are counted per client network in `throttle.rs`: `AttemptLimits::code_misses_per_ip` (5) in `code_window` (1 minute). A hit hands its count back; over the limit the answer is `Err(Blocked)` even for a right code.
- **Listing.** `Registry::lobbies()` returns the public games whose actor has not started, newest first, at most `MAX_LISTED_LOBBIES` (50), as `ListedGame { game, host, format, filled, total, created_at, age }`: `host` is seat 0's name, `filled` the seats that are not open, `created_at` a `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` stamp (UTC). It holds no token, code, digest or link; a full lobby stays in it.
- **After a restart.** A lobby comes back with its visibility, code digest, seat states, names, token digests and accounts; old tokens open their seats and open seats stay claimable. A started game with an open seat does not load (marked `failed`).

## Transport
- `Server::new(config).serve(listener, shutdown)` runs axum; the `mtg-server` binary reads `MTG_BIND` (127.0.0.1:8080), `MTG_ALLOWED_ORIGINS` (comma list), `MTG_MAX_GAMES` (100), the pace of play (`MTG_AUTO_PASS` on; `0`, `off` or `false` turn the default off, `1`, `on`, `true` on, anything else refuses to start; `MTG_RESPONSE_SECS` 10 and `MTG_BLOCK_SECS` 45, whole seconds, `0` for no clock; `MTG_DWELL_MS` `250-700`, one number for a fixed wait, `0` for none, at most 5000) and, for accounts, `MTG_REGISTRATION`, `MTG_COOKIE_SECURE` and `MTG_TRUSTED_PROXIES` (see Accounts). `ServerConfig::fixed_seed` is for tests only and is never read from the environment.
- REST: `POST /api/games` {format:"normal", seats:[{name, deck}]} gives 201 {game, seats:[{seat, token}]} (2 to 12 seats, 16 KiB `application/json` body, unknown fields refused, the seed is always server-random); `GET /api/games/{id}` gives {started, seats, connected, ready, result} (`ready`: the seats that pressed Ready and did not take it back, none after a restart of a lobby, all once the game runs); `GET /api/decks`; `GET /healthz`; the account endpoints below. Errors are `{error, message}`.
- Every request that changes something (any method but GET, HEAD and OPTIONS) goes through `api::same_site_only`: an `Origin` that is neither in `MTG_ALLOWED_ORIGINS` nor the request's own `Host` is 403 `forbidden_origin`, no `Origin` (a bot) passes. It is the socket's rule (`origin.rs`), applied to POSTs too. CORS answers listed origins only and now sends `Access-Control-Allow-Credentials: true` to them, so a front end on a listed origin may send the session cookie.
- `Registry` holds the games: tokens are 256 random bits from the OS and only their SHA-256 is kept (never logged); a cap on games; a janitor drops finished games after 10 min, lobbies that never started after 1 h and started games nobody has been connected to for 24 h.
- `GET /ws/{game}/{token}`: the token picks the seat before the upgrade (unknown game, malformed id and wrong token share one 404); `Origin` must be listed or match `Host` (no `Origin` is a bot and passes). `GET /ws/{game}` takes the seat from the session cookie instead (see Accounts). Then `Join` within 10 s (wrong version gets `Error{UnsupportedVersion}`, a mismatching game `Error{UnknownGame}`), `handle.join(seat)`, and one text frame per `ClientMessage`/`ServerMessage`.
- Limits per socket: frames at most 64 KiB, a burst of 40 messages then 20 per second, five malformed messages in a row, ping every 20 s and a close after 60 s without a pong. A second socket for a seat replaces the first.
- Close codes: 1001 server stopping or game removed, 1002 bad handshake, 1007 not UTF-8, 1008 policy (rate, malformed run, no join, no pong), 1009 frame too big, 1013 dropped as too slow (reconnect), 4000 replaced by a newer socket of the same seat (do not reconnect on your own). A closed socket drops the seat's queue and never calls `leave`.
- Shutdown (SIGTERM or ctrl-c): stop accepting, send every socket 1001, wait up to 5 s for all of it; a stalled request cannot hold the process.
- Tests (`tests/{ws_games,ws_refusals,ws_limits,rest,registry,shutdown}.rs`, helpers in `tests/net/`) use a real listener on port 0, raw TCP for hostile HTTP and tokio-tungstenite for sockets.
