| `GET /api/games/{id}` | `{format, started, seats, connected, ready, result, visibility, manual_play}`, `format` being `normal` or `commander`;# Server

The `mtg-server` crate: one actor per game behind an axum REST and WebSocket transport, with Postgres persistence, accounts and the pace of play. The wire contract is in [protocol.md](protocol.md), the client in [web.md](web.md).

## Shape

- `spawn_game(id, setup, store)` starts a game in its lobby and `resume_game(store, id, setup)` rebuilds one from its log; both return a cheap-clone `GameHandle` (`join`, `send`, `leave`, `status`) and need a tokio runtime. `status()` carries the state hash and is for tests and admin views, never for clients.
- One task per game owns the `Engine`, the `EventStore` and a bounded queue per seat. A full or closed queue drops that client, which reconnects and gets a snapshot, so the actor never waits for a client. The actor trusts the seat it is given: the transport maps a credential to one seat.
- A step checks the seat, the `DecisionId` and engine legality, applies the answer, projects for the connected seats, appends to the store, then sends the events, the next `Decision` (only to its seat) and `GameOver`. A store or projection failure restores the engine from its `Checkpoint` and answers `Error{Internal}` without details.
- Snapshots and batches are built for a `PlayerId`; a viewer past the last seat is a spectator's (`StateView::for_spectator`, [protocol.md](protocol.md)). Every socket today is a seat's, so no client gets that view.
- The lobby has no engine until every seat has sent `Ready`; readiness is never stored and outlives a connection. The last `Ready` creates the engine and broadcasts the opening events from seq 0; if that fails, a repeated `Ready` retries. Chat (500 characters at most) goes to the connected seats and is never stored.
- The lobby roster (`ServerMessage::Lobby`) lists every seat and is sent after each change. An open seat is listed with an empty name, not connected and not ready, so it counts in `needed` and the game cannot start while one is open: nobody can join an open seat and nothing sent as one counts, so a `Ready` still on its way from a player removed a moment ago starts nothing.
- Presence (`presence.rs`): once the game runs, the actor watches for clients that hang up as it does in the lobby, and after every command compares the connected seats with what the table was last told (`Presence::changes`); each change goes to the other connected seats as `Presence`, with the time left on the away seat's sent decision (`Running::time_left`; a decision held back for a dwell has none). Event batches and snapshots carry the flag (`presence::stamp`), and a batch counts as telling the table. Presence is never stored or hashed.
- A game made from a recipe keeps one deck per seat in the lobby (`SeatDecks`, [Choosing a deck](#choosing-a-deck)): the roster carries each held seat's `deck`, and a `Ready` from a seat without one is answered `Error{BadMessage}` to that seat alone and changes nothing. A game made from a bare setup (`Registry::register`, `spawn_game`) has no choices to make, lists no decks and needs none.
- `Registry` holds the live games by random v4 `GameId`. A seat token is 256 random bits kept only as its SHA-256, and `authenticate` compares every seat so timing does not say which one matched. `MTG_MAX_GAMES` caps creation.
- The janitor sweeps every 30 s and drops finished games after 10 min, lobbies that never started after 1 h and started games with nobody connected for 24 h (`Retention`). It closes their sockets with 1001 and marks the record `expired` or `finished`. A host who abandons a lobby ends it sooner with `DELETE /api/games/{id}` ([Open games](#open-games)).

## Transport

`Server::new(config)` keeps games in memory, `Server::open(config, pool)` also records them in Postgres, and `serve(listener, shutdown)` runs axum. Errors are `{error, message}`, and every response is `no-store` (a picture sets its own rule) and `nosniff` because it can hold tokens and live state.

| Route | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `POST /api/games` | `{format: "normal" \| "commander", seats: [{name, deck?}], visibility?, held?, house_rules?}` gives 201 `{game, code, seats: [{seat, token}]}`; 2 seats or more with no most in any format, `deck` the seat's starting deck (a demo deck id in a Normal game, `precon:<code>` for any seat, or for seat 0 the id of one of the creator's stored decks; none leaves the seat to choose), an unknown `format` is 400 `unsupported_format`, `application/json` body of at most `GAME_BODY_LIMIT` (256 KiB, 413 `payload_too_large`: the one ceiling on a game's seats, about 2,500 named seats; a seat is a `u64` index, `Vec`s and `seat.index()` lookups address any of them), unknown fields refused, server-random seed. `visibility` is `public` or `invite` (the default); `held` is how many seats from seat 0 get a token (1 to the seat count, every seat by default): the others are open, their `name` is never read and `seats` lists only the held ones. A deck nobody has is 400 `unknown_deck`, a blocked one 422 `deck_blocked`. `house_rules` is the creator's [house rules](#house-rules); a value out of range is 400 `invalid_request` with the rule's name in `field` and at the start of `message` (`handicaps[1].starting_life` for a handicap, `handicaps` for a seat the table lacks, a repeated seat or an entry that sets nothing). |
| `GET /api/games/{id}` | `{format, started, seats, connected, ready, result, visibility, manual_play, house_rules?}`, `format` being `normal` or `commander`, `house_rules` the [creator's rules](#house-rules) and left out when none is set (so an invite-only lobby can name them); a malformed and an unknown id are one 404. |
| `/api/lobbies`, `/api/join`, `/api/games/{id}/join`, `/leave`, `/seats/{n}/release`, `PUT /seats/{n}/deck`, `PATCH` and `DELETE /api/games/{id}` | The game browser: [Open games](#open-games); the deck of a seat: [Choosing a deck](#choosing-a-deck). |
| `GET /api/demo-decks`, `GET /healthz` | The built-in decks (three mono-colour vanilla decks, `green-black` and `white-blue` with the corpus cards the engine fully supports); `ok`. |

| `GET /ws/{game}/{token}` | A seat's link: the token picks the seat before the upgrade. |
| `GET /ws/{game}` | The seat bound to the session cookie's user. |

- The origin guard (`origin.rs`) keeps other sites from acting with a visitor's cookie: no `Origin` (a bot) passes, otherwise it must be in `MTG_ALLOWED_ORIGINS` or name the `Host` authority, else 403 `forbidden_origin`. It covers both socket routes and every request that changes something (any method but GET, HEAD, OPTIONS); CORS answers listed origins only, with credentials, allows GET, POST, PUT, PATCH and DELETE with the headers `Content-Type`, `Authorization` and `If-None-Match`, and exposes `ETag` and `Retry-After`.
- A socket checks the origin, then the credential (an unknown game, a malformed id and a wrong token share one 404), then expects `Join` first ([protocol.md](protocol.md), Sync contract): a wrong version gets `Error{UnsupportedVersion}`, a foreign game id `Error{UnknownGame}`, anything else `Error{BadMessage}`, each closing with 1002.
- A second socket for a seat replaces the first. A closing socket drops its queue and never calls `leave`, which would close the connection that replaced it.
- Shutdown (SIGTERM, ctrl-c) stops accepting, sends every socket 1001 and waits up to 5 s; what is still open then is abandoned, so a stalled request cannot hold the process.

| Limit per socket | Value |
|---|---|
| `Join` | within 10 s of the upgrade |
| Frame and message | 64 KiB |
| Messages | a burst of 40, then 20 per second |
| Malformed messages | 5 in a row, each answered `Error{BadMessage}` |
| Ping | every 20 s; closed after 60 s without a pong |

| Close code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1001 | Server stopping, or the game left the registry |
| 1002 | Bad handshake or protocol error |
| 1007 | Text frame that is not UTF-8 |
| 1008 | Policy: rate limit, malformed run, no `Join` in time, no pong |
| 1009 | Frame too big |
| 1011 | A server message could not be serialised |
| 1013 | Dropped as too slow: reconnect |
| 4000 | Replaced by a newer socket of the same seat: do not reconnect on your own |
| 4001 | The seat was taken away, because its player left or the host removed them: its token no longer opens anything, do not reconnect |
| 4002 | The host closed the game before it started: its links and code no longer open anything, do not reconnect |

## Persistence

- `PgEventStore` implements `EventStore` over `game_events`, primary key `(game_id, seq)`. An append is one transaction under a per-game advisory lock that checks the log ends at `from_seq`, so a gap, a repeat or a lost race returns `OutOfOrder` and a failure midway leaves no rows. Reads check for gaps and undecodable events (`StoreError::Corrupt`).
- Migrations (`crates/mtg-server/migrations`) are tracked in `_mtg_server_migrations`, not `_sqlx_migrations`, which `mtg-db` owns, so both crates migrate one database in any order, repeatedly and concurrently. The table name is an on-disk contract; `mtg_server::migrate(&pool)` runs them.
- `games` holds each game's recipe, result, status (`lobby`, `running`, `finished`, `expired` when the janitor dropped it or the host closed the lobby, `failed` when boot could not load it), what failed (`failure` for the operator, `failure_reason` for the players), visibility and the digest of its join code; `seats` holds each seat's name, state (`issued`, `open`, `claimed`), token digest (null while open) and account, and whether that account removed a failed game from its list (`hidden`). `game_events` has no foreign key to `games`, so a log can be stored first.
- `Registry::create` writes the game and seats in one transaction before the actor exists, so a failed write creates nothing (503 `unavailable`). The actor's `Recorder` notes each seat's first join, the start and the end; a failed write is logged and never stops a game, because the log is the truth and boot repairs a status that lags behind it.
- `GameRecipe {format, house_rules, seed, decks, fingerprints}` is the only way the server builds a `GameSetup`, for a new game and a resumed one, because a `GameSetup` holds an `Arc<dyn FormatRules>` and cannot be stored. Its JSON is an on-disk contract: unknown fields and variants are refused. It is written when the game is made and rewritten once, when the game starts with decks its seats chose ([Choosing a deck](#choosing-a-deck)).
- `FormatKind` is `normal` or `commander` (`FormatKind::parse`, `rules`). A seat's `DeckRef::Oracle {cards, commanders, companion, printings}` names the library, the commanders and the companion by oracle id; `commanders`, `companion` and `printings` are left out of the JSON for a deck without any, so a recipe stored before Commander, the companion or printings reads as it did. `printings` holds the printing each deck line chose, one entry for every card of `cards`, then of `commanders`, then the companion (`null` for a line that named none); `build_with` labels the card at each position with it (`ObjectView.identity.printing_id`, [protocol.md](protocol.md)). The label changes no rule: the fingerprint and the state hash are those of the deck without printings (`CardIdentity` hashes its oracle id alone, `tests/it/printing_recipe.rs`). `build_with` gives every seat's commanders and companion to `GameSetup::with_commanders` and `with_companions`, so the engine's `validate_seat` and `validate_companion` judge the seats, and house rules layer over the format as in a Normal game. A Commander seat that has not chosen a deck holds the stand-in `DeckRef::Demo {id: "commander-stand-in"}` (a legendary creature and 99 Forests); it cannot be ready with it.

### Resume

- `Server::open` loads every `lobby` and `running` game oldest first, rebuilds the setup from the recipe, checks the seat rows against it and calls `resume_game` with the stored digests, so old tokens open the same seats. A game whose log shows it ended is only corrected to `finished`.
- A recipe, seat rows or log that do not load (a replay panic included), a changed deck, a deck or card the server lost and a started game with a seat nobody holds mark the game `failed`, and boot goes on; only an unreachable database stops it. A `failed` game is not retried (fix the row and set it to `running` by hand) and is not loaded, so its tokens, lookup and sockets answer 404.
- A failure has two texts. `failure` is the operator's detail (the error, the seat, the fingerprints), also logged; `failure_reason` is one sentence for the players from `Cause` (`registry/cause.rs`), such as "The deck of Friend has changed since this game was made, so it cannot continue.": what happened to the game, with no id, error, fingerprint or other internal. A game marked `failed` before the reason was kept has none, and My games says "This game could not be restored, so it cannot continue." for it.
- A lobby returns with its visibility, join code and claims (names, tokens, accounts, open seats) and nobody ready, and keeps its age: past `Retention::lobby_after` (1 h, by the database clock) it becomes `expired`. A started game with an open seat does not load. A running game gets a fresh 24 h without a connection. `MTG_MAX_GAMES` limits creation only.

### Deck check

- A new game's recipe stores a `DeckFingerprint` per seat: the SHA-256 of a canonical, versioned text of the ordered cards the engine is given (`v1:<64 hex digits>`), followed by the seat's commanders and its companion when it has any (a deck without them hashes as it always did). `Engine::resume` applies stored events as facts, so without it a log would replay under other cards without failing.
- `GameRecipe::build` recomputes it and fails with `RecipeError::DeckChanged` for the first seat whose cards differ in any field, order or size; resume marks the game `failed` before replaying anything. A recipe without fingerprints resumes unchecked, with a warning at every boot.
- It covers the definitions the engine is given, so an edited card record, a changed rules file, a compiled row written again or removed (`card_rules`) or an engine that carries more of a card fails the game at the next boot; a game that runs keeps its definitions, whatever is stored meanwhile, and a changed seed or format rule is not detected. A new `CardDefinition` field changes every fingerprint unless it is left out of the JSON while it holds its default (the pinned values in the tests fail otherwise).
- A deck of oracle ids (`DeckRef::Oracle`) is assembled by `CardSource` ([cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md), Printed side and rules); an id with no row fails with `CatalogError::NotFound`.

### House rules

- `POST /api/games` takes `house_rules` (`HouseRules`, [engine.md](engine.md), House rules): every rule is optional and an absent rule keeps the format's value (`handicaps` is a list, left out or empty for none). A value out of range or a handicap for a seat beyond the request's `seats`, checked before anything is made, is 400 `invalid_request` with the rule's name in `field`; an unknown rule or a value of the wrong type is 400 `invalid_request` without one.
- The rules for Commander (`commander_tax`, `commander_damage`, `deck`, `commanders_per_seat`) are stored and listed like the others and named by their path in an error (`deck.deck_size`, `deck.max_copies`). A game checks its decks with the rules when it starts (`GameSetup::validate` calls `Commander::validate_seat` with the layered `RuleSet`). The server judges a seat's deck with the same rules when it is chosen ([Choosing a deck](#choosing-a-deck)) and answers the lobby's deck check for the table (`POST /api/decks/check` with `game` or `table`, [Decks](#decks)). A seat without a deck holds a stand-in of the table's deck size (`commander-stand-in` for 100 cards, `commander-stand-in-<n>` for another size in the recipe), so the table passes its own check before anybody has chosen.
- The rules are part of `GameRecipe` and are stored in `games.recipe`, a `jsonb` column, so no migration is needed. `house_rules` is left out of the stored JSON while none is set, and a recipe stored without it reads as no rules. `GameOptions::house_rules` carries them into `Registry::create_with` and `create_decked`; `SeatDecks` keeps them for the recipe it writes when the game starts. A resume and a replay build the setup from the recipe, so they play under the rules the game was made with, and a stored recipe whose rules are out of range fails with `RecipeError::Setup`.
- `ListedGame::house_rules` and the lobby row show them ([Game browser endpoints](#game-browser-endpoints)); the table view shows them to every seat ([protocol.md](protocol.md), State projection).

## Accounts

- `username_key` is the lower case and unique, so names are unique ignoring case. A session is the SHA-256 of its cookie token. Without `DATABASE_URL` every account endpoint answers 503 `accounts_unavailable` and games are guest-only.
- A username is 3 to 32 ASCII letters, digits, `_` or `-` (no Unicode, so no look-alikes); a password is 10 to 128 characters, compared byte for byte and never normalised. Passwords are Argon2id (`argon2` pinned at 0.6.0), stored as the PHC string so the cost travels with the hash; the default is the OWASP minimum (19 MiB, 2 passes, 1 lane). Hashing runs on `spawn_blocking` behind a semaphore (`AuthConfig::hash_jobs`, 4), so a burst cannot exhaust memory.

| Endpoint | Success | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /api/auth/register` | 201 `{id, username, is_admin}` and a session | 400 `invalid_username` or `invalid_password` (the message states the rule, never the input); 409 `username_taken`; 403 `registration_closed` (decided before the body is read); 429 |
| `POST /api/auth/login` | 200 `{id, username, is_admin}` and a fresh session | 401 `invalid_credentials`; 429 `too_many_attempts` with `Retry-After` |
| `POST /api/auth/logout` | 204 with or without a session; the cookie is cleared | |
| `GET /api/auth/me` | 200 `{id, username, is_admin}` | 401 `unauthenticated`; a cookie the server does not honour is cleared |
| `GET /api/auth/options` | 200 `{registration_open}`; anyone may ask | 503 `accounts_unavailable` |
| `POST /api/admin/users` `{username, password}` | 201 `{id, username}`; no session starts, for the admin's browser or for the new account | 503 `accounts_unavailable`, 401 `unauthenticated`, 403 `forbidden` (in this order, all before the body is read); then 400 `invalid_username` or `invalid_password`, 409 `username_taken`, 429 |
| `GET /api/admin/users` | 200 `{users: [{id, username, created_at, is_admin}]}`, oldest first | 503, 401, 403 as above |
| `POST /api/admin/users/{id}/password` `{password}` | 204; every session of the account ends | 503, 401, 403 as above, all before the body is read; then 404 `unknown_account` (an id that is not a UUID or names no account), 409 `admin_account` (the admin's own account); then 400 `invalid_request` or `invalid_password`, 429 |
| `DELETE /api/admin/users/{id}` | 204; the account is deleted as `DELETE /api/auth/account` deletes it | 503, 401, 403, 404, 409 as above; 429 |
| `POST /api/auth/password` `{current_password, new_password}` | 204; the account's other sessions end, this one stays | 401 `unauthenticated`; 400 `invalid_password` (the new one); 401 `invalid_credentials` (the current one); 429 |

### Admin

- `users.is_admin` marks the one account that makes the others. `MTG_ADMIN_USERNAME` names it (trimmed, a valid username or the server refuses to start, compared ignoring case). At start, after the migrations, `Server::open` runs one statement, `UPDATE users SET is_admin = (username_key = lower(name))`, over every row: at most one admin exists, a changed or removed setting demotes the old admin, and without the setting nobody is admin. A name that belongs to no account yet promotes nobody and logs a warning; there is no bootstrap path, so a fresh install registers the owner first (registration open), then sets the variable and closes registration ([deploy.md](deploy.md)).
- Every admin route reads `is_admin` from the database for the session's account on each request, never from the cookie or the client, so a demotion applies at once. The checks run in one order, all before the body is read: 503 `accounts_unavailable`, 401 `unauthenticated` (a cookie the server does not honour is cleared), 403 `forbidden` for a signed-in account that is not the admin.
- `POST /api/admin/users` follows the rules and errors of `register` (400 `invalid_username` and `invalid_password` state the rule and never echo the input, 409 `username_taken`), works whether `MTG_REGISTRATION` is open or closed, and shares the Argon2 semaphore. It hashes behind a budget of its own per admin account (`AttemptLimits::admin_creates_per_account`, 20 per `window`; a try the rules refuse costs nothing, a taken name counts, 429 with `Retry-After`) that never touches the registrations counted per address. `register` itself is unchanged: `MTG_REGISTRATION=closed` answers 403 `registration_closed` before the body is read.
- `POST /api/admin/users/{id}/password` and `DELETE /api/admin/users/{id}` act on a friend's account after the checks above and, before any body is read, 404 `unknown_account` (the id is not a UUID or names no account) and 409 `admin_account` (the admin's own account: its password changes only in Account settings, and nothing makes another admin). The password follows the creation rules and is stored as a fresh Argon2id hash; `Accounts::reset_password` sets it and deletes every session of the account in one transaction. The removal is `Accounts::delete_user` and then `Registry::forget_user`, as for `DELETE /api/auth/account`. Both draw on the admin's budget of `POST /api/admin/users` (`AttemptLimits::admin_creates_per_account`, hashing or deleting only after the rules and the 404 and 409 checks), and the log names the admin and the account by id.
- `Accounts::delete_user` and `reset_password` lock the account's row first (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) and stop with `Outcome::Admin` or `Outcome::Gone`, so a promotion or a second removal cannot slip in between the route's check and the change.
- A body that does not parse on a route that carries a password (`register`, `login`, `POST /api/admin/users`, the password reset, the two settings routes) is 400 `invalid_request` with one fixed message that quotes nothing, since a parse error can repeat what was sent; other routes name the missing or unknown field.
- `GET /api/auth/options` is the one public hint about the instance: whether registering is open, so the sign-up page can say so before anyone types.

- The two settings routes act on the account of the session cookie (checked before the body is read) and are confirmed with its current password, checked and counted as a login is: the same per-address and per-username counters, the same Argon2 cost, 429 even for the right password once the budget is gone, and a right password gives its try back. A password that breaks the rules is refused as a wrong one, unhashed and uncounted. The new password follows the registration rules and is stored as a fresh Argon2id hash. The origin guard and the cookie rules are those of the other account routes.
- Changing the password updates the hash and deletes the account's other sessions in one transaction, so a leaked cookie dies with the password it could have used.
- The admin's account cannot be deleted: `DELETE /api/auth/account` answers 409 `admin_account` for it before the body is read and counts no password try. A mistyped `MTG_ADMIN_USERNAME` leaves nobody admin; [deploy.md](deploy.md) says how to recover.
- Deleting the account is one transaction in `Accounts::delete_user`: the owner's lock, `DeckRepo::delete_all_of` (decks and their lines; none when the deck tables are not migrated, since accounts run without card data), `FixMarkRepo::delete_all_of` (the account's fix marks, same rule), then the `users` row, which takes the sessions with it (`ON DELETE CASCADE`) and unbinds its seats (`ON DELETE SET NULL`). A test scans every uuid, text and json column of the schema for the account id and finds none afterwards. Game records keep the seat names they were played under.
- After the commit `Registry::forget_user` takes the account out of the live games as a leave: a seat a join took in a lobby is released (`release`), the lobby the account hosts is closed (`close`, the host's seat cannot be given back), and any other seat it held (a game that runs, or a seat made with the game's link) stays with its token and no account while its connection is closed, so the game waits for it on its clock like for any player who left. A seat the database refuses to change is logged and counted in `Departure::failed`. The username is free to register again and the new account holds nothing of the old one.

- Login does not reveal whether a username exists: an unknown name, a wrong password and a name or password that breaks the rules get one byte-identical 401, and an unknown name costs one Argon2 check against a stand-in hash. Registration does say "taken"; that cannot be avoided.
- The cookie `mtg_session` holds 256 random bits as hex and nothing else: `HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Path=/; Max-Age=2592000` (30 days), no `Domain`, plus `Secure` when `MTG_COOKIE_SECURE=1`. Expiry is absolute and judged by the database clock; an upkeep task purges expired rows hourly. A sign-in that presents a session ends it, so a browser holds one.
- Guessing is throttled in memory per process (`throttle.rs`) in 15-minute fixed windows: failed logins per client address (30) and per username (10, whether or not the account exists), registrations per address (10); join code misses per address (5) and requests for the lobby list per address (30) have a one-minute window each. A login counts when it starts, so parallel guesses cannot slip under the limit, and is handed back on success or a server failure. Over the limit the answer is 429, even for the right password. IPv6 counts by /64; past 50 000 counters, untracked keys go unlimited until a sweep frees room.
- The client address is the socket's peer. Behind a reverse proxy, `MTG_TRUSTED_PROXIES=n` takes the `n`-th entry from the right of `X-Forwarded-For`, which the nearest trusted proxy wrote, so forged entries to its left are ignored; with 0 the header is ignored, because anybody can send it.
- Passwords, tokens, digests and hashes never reach logs or `Debug` output; failed attempts are logged without the name tried.

### Seats and accounts

- `seats.user_id` is unique per game, so an account holds one seat of a game. A signed-in `POST /api/games` binds seat 0 to the creator, and the first signed-in join through an unbound seat's link binds that seat before the upgrade; both need a recorded game, and guests bind nothing. One `UPDATE .. WHERE user_id IS NULL AND NOT EXISTS (the user's other seat)` decides, so racing accounts get one winner and a bound seat never changes hands (`Registry::bind_seat`, `Binding`). A failed binding is logged and never stops the join.
- Binding adds a way in and takes none away: old links keep working, and another signed-in account that uses a bound seat's token plays like a guest and is not bound.
- `GET /ws/{game}` answers, after the origin check, 404 for an address that is not a game id, 503 `accounts_unavailable`, 401 `unauthenticated` (a bad cookie is cleared), then one 404 for an unknown game and for a game the user holds no seat in, so a stranger learns nothing about which games exist.
- `GET /api/me/games` lists the user's `lobby`, `running`, `finished` and `failed` games, newest first, at most 100. It reads the database, so a finished game stays listed after the registry dropped it. A failed game carries its `failure_reason` for the accounts that hold a seat in it and nobody else: the list is built from the user's own seats, and the lookup, the sockets and the game browser answer 404 for a failed game as for one that never existed.
- `DELETE /api/me/games/{id}` takes a failed game off the signed-in user's list (204) by marking the user's own seat `hidden`: the other players keep the game and the record stays with every seat bound, so a repaired game (`running` again) shows for all of them. A game that is not in the list, did not fail or was removed already is 404, as is an id that is no game; no session is 401 and a foreign origin 403.

## Open games

A game has a visibility and each seat a state, so a visitor can claim an open seat by the game's id or with its join code ([ui-design.md](ui-design.md), Game browser and lobby).

- `Visibility` is `public` or `invite` (in JSON and `games.visibility`; `invite` by default, for old rows too). A public game in its lobby is listed; an invite game is reached only by its seat links and its join code. `GameOptions {visibility, held}` goes to `Registry::create_with`; `create` and `create_for` make invite games that hold every seat. `Registry::set_visibility(game, by, visibility)` is for the host (`HOST`, seat 0) and works before the start.
- A seat is `Issued` (its token was made with the game and is in the create response: the seat link), `Open` (no token, no account, an empty name) or `Claimed` (a join took it and was given a token). `held` counts the seats, from seat 0, issued at creation: the creator's is always one and the rest are open. A `CHECK` ties an open seat to a null `token_digest` and no account.
- `Registry::claim(game, entrance, SeatClaim {name, user})` gives the first open seat under a trimmed name of 1 to 32 characters (the caller checks for control characters), makes its token and returns `Seated {game, seat, token}`, the one time the token exists in clear. An account that holds a seat of the game is handed it again with `token: None` and opens it through its session; guests may take several seats. `Entrance::Listing` (a game id) reaches public games only and answers `NotFound` for others, as for an unknown id; `Entrance::Code` (a code resolved by `find_by_code`) reaches any game. `ClaimError` is `NotFound`, `Full`, `Started` (judged before `Full`), `BadName` or `Record`. A claim sets name, token and account; the seat plays the deck the visitor brings (`Registry::claim_with`), else the deck it was made with.
- `Registry::release(game, seat, by)` opens a claimed seat again: the holder (`by == seat`) leaves, or the host removes a player. The token stops working, the account is free, the connection closes, `Ready` and the stops are reset and the table is told. `TableError` is `NotFound`, `NotAllowed`, `NotClaimed` (an issued or open seat), `Started` or `Record`.
- `Registry::close(game, by)` ends a game that is still in its lobby, for the host alone. In order: the actor promises that no `Ready` starts the game from then on (`GameHandle::close_lobby`; `TableError::Started` when it already runs, so a close and the last `Ready` have one winner, in the actor's order of commands); the record becomes `expired`, the status of a lobby the janitor drops, so a closed game is not loaded at boot, not listed under My games and frees its code (a failed write takes the promise back with `reopen_lobby` and changes nothing: `TableError::Record`); the entry leaves the registry, so the listing, the lookup, the code and every token stop reaching the game at once. Before the entry is dropped its `Removal` channel (`SeatAccess::removed`) says `ClosedByHost`, which is how a socket picks 4002 over the 1001 of a plain drop.
- Claims, releases, visibility changes, closes and `bind_seat` of one game run one at a time (`Entry::turn`). A claim writes the row first (guarded by `state = 'open'` and the lobby status, so racing claims get one winner), then names the seat in the actor (`GameHandle::occupy`, refused once the game runs), then the registry's slot. A release empties the seat in the actor first (`vacate`): a lobby with an open seat cannot start, so nothing begins under the change. A failed write leaves table, row and slot as they were.
- The join code is eight symbols of `ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789` (capitals and digits without `I`, `O`, `0` and `1`; 40 bits) from the OS CSPRNG, returned in `Created::code` and in clear only there. The registry and `games.code_digest` keep its SHA-256 (unique among `lobby` and `running` games; none for games stored before codes). `Registry::find_by_code(typed, address, now)` ignores case, spaces and hyphens, compares every game's digest without stopping at a match and answers the game id or `None`; text that cannot be a code is the same `None`. Misses are counted per client network (`AttemptLimits::code_misses_per_ip`, 5, in `code_window`, 1 minute); a hit hands its count back, and over the limit the answer is `Err(Blocked)`, even for a right code.
- `Registry::lobbies()` returns the public games whose actor has not started, newest first, at most `MAX_LISTED_LOBBIES` (50), as `ListedGame {game, host, format, house_rules, manual_play, filled, total, created_at, age}`: `host` is seat 0's name, `format` is `normal` or `commander`, `filled` counts the seats that are not open, `created_at` is `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` (UTC). It holds no token, code, digest or link, and a full lobby stays in it.

### Game browser endpoints

`lobbies.rs` puts the registry's calls on HTTP. Bodies and codes are in [protocol.md](protocol.md), REST bodies; `POST /api/games` is in the Transport table.

| Route | Caller | Success | Errors |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GET /api/lobbies` | anyone | 200 `LobbyList` with an `ETag`; 304 when `If-None-Match` names it | 429 `rate_limited` with `Retry-After`; 503 `lobbies_unavailable` |
| `POST /api/games/{id}/join` `{name?, deck?}` | anyone; a session binds the seat | 200 `{game, seat, token}`, `token` null when the account already held a seat | 400 `invalid_request`, `invalid_name`; 404 (`unknown_deck` for a deck nobody has); 409 `full`, `started`; 422 `deck_blocked`; 503 |
| `POST /api/join` `{code, name?, deck?}` | anyone; a session binds the seat | as above, any game the code names | as above; 429 `too_many_attempts` with `Retry-After` |
| `PUT /api/games/{id}/seats/{n}/deck` `{deck}` | the holder of seat `n` | 200 `{name, format, manual_cards}` | 400; 401; 403 `forbidden` (another seat); 404, `unknown_deck`; 409 `seat_ready`, `started`; 422 `deck_blocked`; 503 |
| `POST /api/games/{id}/leave` | the holder of a claimed seat | 204 | 401; 404; 409 `not_claimed`, `started`; 503 |
| `POST /api/games/{id}/seats/{n}/release` | the host | 204 | 401; 403 `forbidden`; 404; 409 `not_claimed`, `started`; 503 |
| `PATCH /api/games/{id}` `{visibility}` | the host | 200 `{visibility}` | 400; 401; 403 `forbidden`; 404; 409 `started`; 503 |
| `DELETE /api/games/{id}` | the host | 204 | 401; 403 `forbidden`; 404; 409 `started`; 503 |

- The three routes that act on a seat need a credential and the others are open to anyone. A seat is named by `Authorization: Bearer <token>`, which needs no database, or else by the session cookie. A token wins over the session; another `Authorization` scheme (a proxy's) is not a token and is ignored. With neither, a server without a database answers 503 `accounts_unavailable` and one with accounts 401 `unauthenticated` (a stale cookie is cleared), as the other session routes do. A token or session that opens no seat of the game, an unknown game and a malformed id are one 404.
- `release` is for the host (seat 0) alone, even on the host's own seat; a player gives their seat back with `leave`. Only seats a join took can be given back: the host's seat, an open seat and a seat made with the game answer 409 `not_claimed`. A seat number over 11, or written with a sign or a leading zero, is a 404. Once the game starts, `leave`, `release`, `PATCH`, `DELETE` and a join that may reach the game answer 409 `started`.
- `DELETE` closes the game for the host alone, named by a token or the session as for `PATCH`; a seat that is not the host's answers 403 whatever state the game is in. The 204 comes after the game left the registry, so the listing, the lookup (404) and the code no longer find it, and every socket of the game, an invite game's too, is closed with 4002. A second `DELETE` finds no game (404). Like the other calls that act on a seat it is not throttled.
- A join checks the body and the name before it looks at the game or the code, so the answer to a missing name says nothing about either. A signed-in visitor needs no `name` and is named after the account unless one is sent; a guest needs 1 to 32 characters without control or invisible marks (400 `invalid_name`). Guests may take several seats of a game.
- An unknown id, a malformed id, an invite game reached by its id (started or not) and a wrong or malformed code are one byte-identical 404, so a visitor cannot find an invite game by trying ids. A code reaches any game, so a right code for a full or started game says 409 `full` or `started`, and a started game says `started` before `full`. The game's id comes back only in the `Joined::game` of a join that succeeds: no refusal and no 429 carries one, so a right code is the one way to learn the id of an invite game, and the page that joins by code opens the game from the answer.
- `POST /api/join` counts wrong codes per address (5 a minute, [Accounts](#accounts)); the sixth try is 429 with `Retry-After`, even for the right code. A bad body or name is refused before the code is counted. `GET /api/lobbies` counts every request, a 304 too, 30 a minute per address (`AttemptLimits::lobby_requests_per_ip` in `lobby_window`); the precon endpoints count apart (`precon_requests_per_ip`, 60, in the same window). The address is the one the throttle uses everywhere ([Accounts](#accounts), `MTG_TRUSTED_PROXIES`).
- The `ETag` is a strong tag: the SHA-256 of the rows (id, host, format, house rules, manual play, filled, total and `created_at`, each length-prefixed) cut to 128 bits, so it holds nothing secret and does not change as games age. `If-None-Match` is read as a list, with `W/` prefixes ignored and `*` matching any listing. A client that gets a 304 keeps its rows and counts their age on from the time it received them. `lobbies_unavailable` is for a registry that cannot list, which is a server shutting down.
- A seat that is taken away closes its connection: a removed or departing player's socket gets 4001 ([Transport](#transport)); the actor tells the socket apart from a replaced or dropped one by the seat being open (`GameStatus::open`). A game that leaves the registry closes every socket of every seat at once: 4002 when its host closed it, 1001 otherwise.

### Choosing a deck

The holder of a seat chooses the deck it plays while the game is in its lobby: the host when the game is made, a joiner when joining or after (`seat_deck.rs`, `deck_choice.rs`, `lobbies.rs`). Bodies and codes are in [protocol.md](protocol.md), REST bodies.

- A choice is a snapshot (`ChosenDeck`) taken when it is made: the cards the engine is given, the commanders of its command zone, the companion, the recipe's name for them (`DeckRef::Demo` or `DeckRef::Oracle`, the oracle id of every card in deck order, of the commanders and of the companion, and the printing of each deck line: `ChosenDeck::with_printings`, from a stored deck's lines and a precon's, left out for a line that names none and for a built-in deck, `pg_seat_decks.rs` and `pg_precons.rs`) with their `DeckFingerprint`, and the three facts the table is told (`SeatDeck`: name, format, `manual_cards`). Editing or deleting the stored deck later changes nothing.
- The companion is the one card of the stored deck's companion zone whose rules carry a companion ability (`ChosenDeck::with_companion`, `PlayerSetup::companion`); a card without one is not judged by the deck check and is no companion for the game, and a second companion card is refused by the deck check (`companion_count`). It is a card outside the game, so the table is told nothing of it: not in the roster, the lookup or the listing, and `manual_cards` counts it like a card of the deck. Its name reaches the other seats with the opening's reveal ([protocol.md](protocol.md), State projection). A seat given back, or that chooses another deck, takes its companion with it. No stored precon has a companion board.
- A deck id is a demo deck's id, `precon:<code>` ([Precons](#precons): public, so no account and any seat) or the UUID of a stored deck. A stored deck is found under the signed-in account of the request (the session cookie, which a seat token does not replace), so another account's deck, a deck that does not exist and a caller without a session are one 404 `unknown_deck`; creating a game answers 400 with the same code. Only the creator's seat may start with a stored deck; the other seats of a new game take demo decks or none.
- The check is `deck_report` in the deck's own format, the verdict the deck list shows, plus the rules of the game's format (`format_issues`), judged once the game is known: a Normal game has no command zone and wants 60 cards with at most four copies of a card (`Normal.validate_deck`); a Commander game takes the engine's `Commander.validate_seat` (the commanders, 100 cards with them, one copy of each, the colour identity, under the deck relaxations of the game's house rules) on the deck's command zone and main deck, so a `commander` or `freeform` deck is played as it stands and a `modern` deck with a command zone is still refused by its own report (`no_commander_zone`); each reason becomes an `Issue` of the report's codes (`commander_count`, `commander_eligibility`, `commander_pair`, `deck_size`, `copies`, `color_identity`). A seat's companion is judged after its deck by the format's `validate_companion` (the condition over the starting deck, and in Commander the commanders' colour identity unless the table relaxed it): an issue `companion_condition` in the companion zone, or `color_identity` there. A demo deck has no commander, so a Commander game refuses it with `commander_count`. A table that relaxed a deck rule (`deck.singleton: false` and `max_copies`, `deck.colour_identity: false`, `deck.deck_size`, `commanders_per_seat: 2`) lifts the issues of the deck's own check that the rule replaces (`IssueCode::lifted_by`: `copies`, `color_identity`, `deck_size`, `commander_pair`), so a deck or a precon that the report refuses only for those is judged by `validate_seat` under the table's rules instead; the codes the engine's errors map to stay the same. A card the game cannot play and every other issue (an unknown card, a ban, a commander that cannot lead, a command zone in a constructed deck) refuse the deck at once, wherever it is brought. A chosen deck keeps the lifted-or-not issues of its own check (`ChosenDeck::with_own_issues`) and the table decides (`ChosenDeck::issues_in`), so a join and a deck change judge it in the registry after the game is found, as before; a Normal game lifts nothing. At creation the rules are judged with the deck; a join or `PUT` judges them in the registry after the game is found, so a refusal tells only a visitor who may see the game its format. Any issue, or a `blocked` state, refuses the deck with 422 `deck_blocked` and the issue list (`DeckBlockedBody`); `manual_play` and `verified` are allowed. The issues name cards and go only to the account whose deck it is. The main deck and the commanders are played; `manual_cards` counts the copies in them that are not verified, never the sideboard or the companion.
- `PUT /api/games/{id}/seats/{n}/deck` is for the seat's holder (a token or the session, as `leave` decides; another seat's holder is 403, a stranger the 404 of `leave`). The actor decides in its order of commands: a seat that pressed Ready answers 409 `seat_ready` and keeps its deck until it sends `NotReady`, a game that runs 409 `started`. The roster is sent to the table after each change.
- `create_with` makes every seat's deck a demo deck, as before; `create_decked` takes a `ChosenDeck` or none per seat. A seat made without one has no deck until its holder chooses, and its `Ready` is refused. A seat given back (`release`) returns to the deck the game was made with, or to none, and an open seat is listed without a deck, so the next player never sees the last one's.
- The recipe holds the decks the game was made with. When the game starts with other decks, the actor stores the recipe with them and `games.manual_play` (`GameRecords::set_start`, one statement, only while the record is a `lobby`) before it stores the first event; a start that cannot store it is refused with `Internal` and tried again by the next `Ready`. A started or resumed game rebuilds from the recipe alone, with each deck's fingerprint checked ([Deck check](#deck-check)).
- `GameStatus::manual_play` says whether the table plays with manual cards: some seat that somebody holds plays a deck whose `manual_cards` is above zero. While the game is in its lobby it is `mtg_protocol::manual_play` over the roster, so it follows a choice, a claim with a deck and a release, and an open seat counts for nothing; the start decides it, and it stays as it was once the game runs. `Registry::lobbies` carries it in `ListedGame` and `Registry::describe` in `PublicGame`, which become the lobby row and `GameInfo`.
- `games.manual_play` (`boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false`) holds the value the game started with, so a resumed game keeps it with no roster to ask. A lobby never reads it: it returns with the roster of its recipe and judges the flag by that. A game stored before the column existed reads false.
- Choices made in a lobby are not stored until the start: a lobby that restarts reads its recipe, so a seat comes back on its built-in deck and a seat whose recipe names a stored deck is without one until it chooses again.

## Cards and decks

`Server::open_with(config, pool, cards)` takes the `CardSource` (database, rules, `Caps`) that the games, the card endpoints and the deck check share; `Server::open` makes one. The rules are the embedded files and the rows of `card_rules` that `mtg-cli compile` writes ([data-sources.md](data-sources.md)): the library is built again whenever a row was added, written again or removed (one cheap query per request tells), a file wins over a row for the same card, and a row this build cannot read is left out and logged. Drafts a player approved join them by the precedence of the read tier ([Reads](#reads)). Every card of a request is judged under one snapshot of them. A server without a database answers 503 `cards_unavailable` and 503 `decks_unavailable`.

### Cards

Public and read-only (`card_api.rs`).

| Endpoint | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/cards` | `CardSearchPage {cards, total, page, per_page}` over the deck cards (tokens and emblems are never listed). |
| `GET /api/cards/{oracle_id}` | `CardDetail`: the hit, oracle text, keywords, faces, Scryfall's legality per format, printings (newest first), rulings (oldest first) and `reasons`, the clauses the game cannot run, each with a face and a span of that face's oracle text. A malformed and an unknown id are one 404; capitals in the id make no difference. |

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `q` | Free text: the name contains it (case and accents ignored) or the rules text matches it in web-search syntax (`"phrase"`, `-word`, `or`). With `q` the order is by relevance (name equal to `q`, name starting with it, name containing it, rules text only), then by name; without it by name. Ties break by oracle id, so every order is total and pages never overlap or skip. |
| `colors`, `colors_match` | A card's colors as letters of WUBRG in any case, or `C` for colorless cards; `colors_match` is `includes` (default), `exactly`, `within` or `overlaps`. |
| `identity` | A color identity in the same letters: a card matches when its identity lies within them, so `identity=BG` lists what a black-green commander may play and `identity=C` the colorless identities. |
| `type` | Words that must each appear in the type line (`legendary creature`). |
| `cmc` | A mana value (`3`), a range (`2-4`) or a minimum (`5+`). |
| `format` | A Scryfall format key (`commander`, `modern`): the card is legal or restricted there. |
| `page`, `per_page` | `page` counts from 1 up to 10 000; `per_page` defaults to 40 and a larger value is lowered to 100. The answer repeats both and carries the total. |

Blank values are no filter; an unknown parameter or a malformed value is 400 `invalid_request` whose message names it. A hit (`CardHit`) carries the oracle id, name, mana cost, mana value, type line, colors, color identity, the art of the card's default printing (the newest paper one; `images` holds the card's links or one entry per face) and `support`: the state `Library::support` gives with the server's rules and `Caps`.

### Pictures

`images.rs` and `images/` serve the picture of a card identity from a disk cache that fills from the Scryfall CDN as pictures are asked for. Public and read-only like the card endpoints: no account, 503 `cards_unavailable` without a database. A picture is shown as Scryfall serves it: never cropped, re-encoded, resized or marked (Scryfall's image terms); `art_crop` is Scryfall's own crop.

| Endpoint | Identity |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/images/oracle/{oracle_id}/{face}/{size}` | A card: the newest paper printing that has a picture (digital ones only when no paper one has). |
| `GET /api/images/printing/{printing_id}/{face}/{size}` | One printing, as the deck chose it. |
| `GET /api/images/token/{face}/{size}?name=&type=&colors=&power=&toughness=` | A token by name, fitted to the other facts given (`type` with or without Scryfall's leading `Token`, `colors` as in the card search, `power` and `toughness` of that face). The stored token that answers is, in order: one with every fact given; one with the same type line and colors (an effect moved power and toughness); any token of that name, where the one that shares the most facts (power and toughness first) wins. A tie goes to the smallest oracle id, so a URL always gives the same picture and `ETag`. |

- `face` counts from 0. A printing with a picture per face (transform, modal and reversible cards) answers face `n`'s own; a printing with one picture answers it for each of its faces (a split card shows both halves). Any other face is a missing picture.
- `size` is `small`, `normal`, `large`, `art_crop` or `png`; any other size or a face that is no number is 400 `invalid_request`.
- A picture answers 200 with `Content-Type` (`image/jpeg`, `image/png` for `png`), `Cache-Control: public, max-age=604800` and a strong `ETag`, a digest of the cache key, so it changes when the stored link does. `If-None-Match` (the weak comparison, a list or `*`) answers 304 without reading the cache or asking the CDN. Every other answer keeps `no-store`.
- An unknown identity, a malformed id, a face or size the printing has no picture for, a CDN that has no such picture and a token name no stored token has are one typed 404, `no_image`: the web draws the card's text face (a token's as a card). A CDN that fails or answers something else than a picture is 502 `image_unavailable`; the web draws the text face and may ask again later. Tokens are cards of the database that are no deck cards, with the printings of Default Cards ([data-sources.md](data-sources.md), Tokens).
- The link comes from the database, never from the client, and is checked all the same: `https`, a host that is `scryfall.io` or under it, no port or credentials, a path of plain segments ending in `.jpg` or `.png`. A stored link that fails is a missing picture and nothing is requested.
- **Cache.** One file per picture in `MTG_IMAGE_CACHE`, named by the link's path with `_` for `/` and `@` and the link's query (the time the picture changed) after it, so a changed picture is another file. Files are written to a temporary name and renamed, so a reader or a crash never sees half a picture. The directory is read when first used: older files count as less recently used, leftovers of an interrupted write are deleted. After each write the least recently used files go until the total is at most `MTG_IMAGE_CACHE_MIB`; a read counts as a use.
- **Upstream.** One fetch per link at a time: requests that arrive while the CDN is asked wait for that answer, a failure included. Requests send `User-Agent: mtg-cli/<version>` and an `Accept` header, follow no redirect, give up after 30 s, and start at least 100 ms after the previous one ended, for the whole server. Timeouts, 408, 425, 429 and 5xx are tried three times with waits of 2 s and 4 s, or the `Retry-After` (at most 20 s); 404 and 410 are a missing picture, any other status a failure. An answer is kept only when it starts like a JPEG or PNG and is at most 8 MiB.
- **Warm-up.** When a game starts, a task of its own fetches the `normal` picture of every face of the cards the seats play (the commanders and companions included), in the printing the deck line chose (a precon names them) and else in the card's newest paper printing, once per card and printing, two at a time through the same cache and spacing. The table asks `normal` for its cards and for its preview, so the first look at a card finds its picture cached; a size nobody warmed waits behind every picture of a table that is still filling. The game never waits for it and a failure is only logged.

### Decks

`decks.rs`, `deck_cards.rs` and `deck_import.rs`. Every route needs a signed-in account: 503 `decks_unavailable` without a database, then 401 `unauthenticated`. A deck of another account is a 404, like an unknown id. Bodies are `application/json` of at most 64 KiB (640 KiB for an import), the origin guard covers every verb but GET, and CORS allows PUT and DELETE. The tables `decks` and `deck_cards` belong to `mtg-db` (migration `20261019000007_decks.sql`, `DeckRepo`): `owner` is the account id with no foreign key, since `users` is the server's own table, so deleting an account has to delete its decks; a line's card is a foreign key and its printing is set to null if the printing goes.

| Endpoint | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/decks` | `DeckList {decks}`, the most recently saved first: id, name, format, timestamps, the derived `state`, `verified`, `changed`, `checked_at` and the `summary` of the check of the moment, and the `commanders` (the `CardHit` of each command-zone card, for the list's pictures). |
| `POST /api/decks` | `{name, format, notes, cards: [{zone, oracle_id, printing_id?, count}]}` stores a deck: 201 `DeckView`. |
| `GET /api/decks/{id}` | `DeckView`: the lines (each a card as a search hit, in the art of the chosen printing), the `check` stored at the last save, the `report` of the moment and the derived `state`, `verified` and `changed`. |
| `PUT /api/decks/{id}` | Replaces name, format, notes and every line in one transaction, runs the check again: 200 `DeckView`. |
| `DELETE /api/decks/{id}` | 204. |
| `POST /api/decks/check` | `{deck}` (a stored deck of the account, or `precon:<code>`, which needs no account) or `{format, cards}` (an unsaved list, under the rules of a save), and optionally the table that judges it: `game` (the id of a game) or `table` (`{format, house_rules?}` for one not made yet), not both. The `DeckReport`, nothing saved; with a table, `legality` holds what a seat choosing the deck there would be refused with. A precon is checked as [Precons](#precons) describes. |
| `POST /api/decks/import` | `{text, source?, format?}`: `ImportResult`, nothing saved. |
| `GET /api/decks/{id}/export?format=text\|arena\|mtgo` | The deck as `text/plain` (`text` by default) with a `Content-Disposition` file name. |

- A save is refused with 400 `invalid_deck` for a name outside 1 to 100 characters or with control characters, notes over 10 000 characters, a format outside `SUPPORTED_FORMATS` (`commander`, `freeform` and the constructed formats), more than 250 lines or 250 cards, a count outside 1 to 999, an id that is no id, or a printing that is not the card's; with 400 `unknown_cards` for oracle ids that are no deck cards of the database. An account stores at most 100 decks (409 `deck_limit`); the count and the insert run under an advisory lock of the owner, so racing saves cannot pass it.
- A check for a table (`game` or `table`) is the deck's own report with its legality replaced by the table's verdict (`judge_at_table`, `DeckReport::set_legality`): the issues the table's deck rules lift are gone, and a deck nothing else is held against is judged by the engine's `validate_seat` in the table's format under its house rules (`format_issues`), the same verdict a seat choosing the deck there gets; `summary.issues` and `summary.state` follow. A game is found by its id alone, like `GET /api/games/{id}`; an unknown or malformed id is 404 `not_found`, naming both `game` and `table` or a format other than `normal` and `commander` is 400 `invalid_request`, and a rule out of range is 400 with the rule in `field`. `GET /api/decks/{id}` and the marker do not depend on a table.
- Saving runs the check (`mtg_cards::deck::deck_report` with the server's rules and `Caps`) and stores `{deck_hash, coverage_hash, checked_at, summary}` in `decks.last_check`. The marker is never stored: every read derives it from that check and the report of the moment (`mtg_cards::deck::marker`, [deck-check.md](deck-check.md)). `verified` is true only when the stored check is clean, both hashes equal the ones computed now and the report of the moment is clean. An edit, other rules for a card of the deck or another `Caps` make the deck `unchecked` with `changed`; a ban or a card the engine no longer plays makes it `blocked` or `manual_play`; a check that was not clean never verifies. Reading writes nothing, so a deck whose cards the engine learned to play is marked by saving it again.
- Import reads a pasted list in any format `mtg-import` reads (`source`: `text`, `arena`, `mtgo`, `mtgo_dek`, `moxfield`, `archidekt`; detected when absent; `format: commander` makes Moxfield's first line the commander), at most 100 000 characters and 1 000 lines. The answer holds the `cards` it resolved (a printing when the set and number name one of the resolved card, or when a line with no set matched the flavor or digital name of exactly one printing), the `unresolved` lines (`unknown_card` or `ambiguous_name`, with suggestions that are never applied), the `problems` (lines that are no entries) and the `ignored` parts (the maybeboard, Moxfield's stickers). Lines are tied through the stored card index (`CardRepo::card_index`, [data-sources.md](data-sources.md#name-resolution)): a set and number name a printing before the name is read, the flavor name or digital name of a printing finds its card and that printing, and `A // B` finds the meld piece `A` (`cards.melds_into`). `CardSource` keeps the index and builds it again when `CardRepo::index_version` changes, so an ingest shows in the next import; with no printings stored, plain names resolve as before.
- Export writes split cards as the format does (`A /// B` for Arena, `A/B` for MTGO) and other multi-faced cards by their front face; printings are written where the format has them. A deck the format cannot hold, MTGO text with a commander and nothing else, answers 422 `export_failed`.

### Precons

`precon_api.rs` and `precons.rs`. Public and read-only like the card endpoints: no account, 503 `cards_unavailable` without a database. Every request counts per address (`AttemptLimits::precon_requests_per_ip`, 60 in `lobby_window`), unknown codes too; over it the answer is 429 `rate_limited` with `Retry-After`. Bodies and codes are in [protocol.md](protocol.md), REST bodies.

| Endpoint | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/precons` | `PreconList {precons}`, newest release first, then by name: code, name, set, deck type, release date, commanders, card count, the format of the check, the derived `state`, `verified`, `playable` and the `summary` counts. |
| `GET /api/precons/{code}` | `PreconView`: the list entry, the `issues` that keep a game from taking the deck, and the cards by board (commander, main, sideboard), each with its support, its reasons and the art of the deck's printing. The code is read in lower case; an unknown and a malformed code are one 404 `not_found`. |

- A code is the set code and the slug of the name (`c18-adaptive-enchantment`: lower-case ASCII words joined by hyphens, cut at 80 characters). A set holds several precons, so the set alone names none; a code another deck already has ends in the deck's id.
- One function judges a precon (`Judge::judge`), so the list, the view and a seat that takes the deck agree. A deck with a commander is checked as `commander`, any other as `freeform` (a product, not a tournament format), with `deck_report`; an entry that matches no stored card is an `unknown_card` issue, counts as `unplayable` and blocks the deck. `state` is the verdict of the moment (`verified`, `manual_play` or `blocked`, never `unchecked`: nothing is stored). `playable` is true when the report's legality is clean, no card is `unplayable` and the engine accepts the deck in the format it is for: a Commander precon in a Commander game (`Commander.validate_seat`), any other in a Normal game (60 cards, at most four copies, `Normal.validate_deck`). A card the game plays by hand (`manual`, `partial`) does not keep a deck from being playable; `issues` says what does. Of the 198 real Commander precons, 195 are playable (`pg_commander_real` starts each as a two-seat game); 3 are blocked by the report (two hold a banned card, one a land twice).
- The judgments of all precons are kept in an index (`CardSource::precon_index`): `PreconRepo::all` reads the decks and their cards in three queries, each card is assembled once, and the index is built again when `PreconRepo::version` (a digest of every deck and entry), `CardRepo::index_version` (cards and printings) or `CardRulesRepo::version` (the compiled rules) changes. The rules files and `Caps` are fixed per `CardSource`, and a source given other ones starts with no index. The first request after a change waits for the build; one precon is read and judged again per request.
- A seat plays a precon by `deck: "precon:<code>"` in a create, a join or `PUT .../deck` ([Choosing a deck](#choosing-a-deck)), with the same snapshot, fingerprint and recipe as a stored deck: the library is the main board in deck order, the commanders are the commander board, and the table is told the precon's name, its format key and the manual copies of both boards. The refusals are those of any deck: 422 `deck_blocked` with the issues for a deck that is not `playable`, or that the game's format does not take (a Commander precon in a Normal game: `no_commander_zone`; a Normal precon in a Commander game: `commander_count`), 404 `unknown_deck` (400 at creation) for a code nobody has. One-click play is a create with the precon as seat 0's `deck`: one request, one lobby.
- `POST /api/decks/check` with `deck: "precon:<code>"` needs no session and answers the precon's `DeckReport` in its own format, the one the list summarises. With a `game` or a `table` the report is the verdict at that table: the deck rules the table relaxed lift their issues and the engine's check of the table's format (`judge_cards_at_table`) judges what is left, so the verdict equals what a seat that chooses the precon there is told. An unknown and a malformed code are one 404 `not_found`, a `format` or `cards` beside the precon is 400 `invalid_request`, and a bad `game` or `table` is refused as for any deck. The request counts against the address's precon budget, an account's too (429 `rate_limited`); without a database the answer is 503 `cards_unavailable`. Any other body needs a session and without one is 401, a malformed body included.

### Fix marks

`fix_marks.rs` and `mtg-db`'s `FixMarkRepo` (`card_fix_marks`). A player marks a card the game does not run whole, or any card that misbehaves, as important to fix; the owner reads the marks ranked. Every route needs a signed-in account: 503 `cards_unavailable` without a database, then 401 `unauthenticated`. Bodies are `application/json` of at most 4 KiB.

| Endpoint | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/fix-marks` | `FixMarkList {marks}`: the account's marks, newest first, each `{oracle_id, name, note, created_at}`. |
| `PUT /api/fix-marks/{oracle_id}` | `{note?}` marks the card, or replaces the note of the mark the account has (its time stays): 200 `FixMarkView`. A card the database lacks, or an id that is no uuid, is 404 `not_found`. |
| `DELETE /api/fix-marks/{oracle_id}` | Takes the account's mark back: 204; 404 when it made none. |

- One row per account and card (`PRIMARY KEY (user_id, oracle_id)`), so marking twice counts once. `user_id` has no foreign key, like `decks.owner`: `Accounts::delete_user` calls `FixMarkRepo::delete_all_of` in its transaction. A card deleted from `cards` takes its marks (`ON DELETE CASCADE`).
- A note is trimmed; an empty one is none. It holds at most 280 characters (`MAX_FIX_NOTE_CHARS`; the table checks the same limit) and no control character but a line break, else 400 `invalid_request`.
- No endpoint shows another account's mark. `FixMarkRepo::ranked(limit)` counts accounts per card, the most marked first, ties by the newest mark and then name, with the note of the newest mark that has one; `mtg-cli fix-marks` joins the support state and prints it ([data-sources.md](data-sources.md)), and `check-rules --gaps --marks` weighs gaps by the marks of the cards they free ([cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md)).

### Reads

`reads.rs` and `mtg-db`'s `DraftRepo` (`card_drafts`) and `ReadRepo` (`card_reads`). A player reads a draft beside the engine's reading and judges it ([cards-dsl.md](cards-dsl.md), Read tier). Accounts are made by the admin, so every signed-in account may judge; a guest reads nothing. Every route needs a signed-in account: 503 `cards_unavailable` without a database, then 401 `unauthenticated`. The origin guard covers `PUT`; bodies are `application/json` of at most 4 KiB.

| Endpoint | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/reads?deck=<uuid>` or `?precon=<code>` | `ReadQueue {cards}`: the deck's cards whose current draft is `pending`, in deck order, then those `approved` that this account has not judged. A deck of another account is 404, like an unknown one. |
| `GET /api/reads/{oracle_id}` | `ReadCard`: the printed faces, the `read_hash`, the sheet, the read state, the number of accounts that judged it correct, this account's verdict and whether that verdict was on an older hash. A card with no current draft is 404 `not_found`. |
| `PUT /api/reads/{oracle_id}` | `ReadVerdict {read_hash, verdict, note?}` records the account's verdict: 200 `ReadCard`. A hash that is not the current draft's is 409 `stale_read` with the current `ReadCard`; `wrong` without a note is 400 `invalid_request`. |

- `card_drafts (oracle_id, read_hash, rules jsonb, retired, created_at)`, primary key `(oracle_id, read_hash)`, is written only by `mtg-cli compile` from the drafts the build embeds (`DraftRepo::insert` makes a draft its card's current one and retires the card's other; a stored retired draft with the same hash is current again); a card's current draft is its one row not `retired`, and `DraftRepo::get` reads pinned drafts, retired ones too. `card_reads (id, user_id, oracle_id, read_hash, verdict, note, created_at)` is append-only (the database refuses an update; only `ReadRepo::delete_all_of` removes rows): an account's newest row on a hash is its verdict, `user_id` is null for the server's own verdict, which is always `wrong`, and a `wrong` always has a note. `ReadRepo::state` gives the read state of each current draft in one query, `queue` the reading queue of a deck, and `of_account` an account's verdict on a card and whether it is on the current hash.
- A note follows the fix-mark rules (trimmed, at most 280 characters, no control character but a line break). A `wrong` verdict and the account's fix mark of the card, with the note, are written in one transaction, so the ranked fix marks are the list of wrong reads; `mtg-cli reads` prints the verdicts by card, account and time (`--wrong`, and `--quick` for one account's verdicts less than 4 s apart).
- Verdicts count per account in 15-minute windows (`AttemptLimits::read_verdicts_per_user`, 150); over it the answer is 429 `rate_limited` with `Retry-After`. `Accounts::delete_user` calls `ReadRepo::delete_all_of` in its transaction; a card that account alone approved is `pending` again.
- `CardSource` loads the current drafts with their read state beside `card_rules` (`Library::with_drafts`) and builds the library again when `DraftRepo::version`, a digest of the current drafts and the verdicts, changes, in the same cheap query as `CardRulesRepo::version`. A verdict reaches the card endpoints, the deck check and the precon index at once, and a game when it starts.
- Pinning: when a game starts, its recipe stores `reads`, the `read_hash` of every card it plays in state `Read` (left out of the JSON while empty). Resume and replay build those cards from their `card_drafts` rows, whatever the verdicts are now, so a verdict never changes a game that runs and never fails its resume; a pinned row that is gone fails the game like a lost card.
- An `AbilityLeftToTable` event ([engine.md](engine.md), Fail-soft resolution) appends the server's `wrong` verdict on that card's pinned hash with the error as its note, so the card is played by hand from the next game; `mtg-cli fix-marks` lists those verdicts beside the marks.

## Pace of play

At each priority decision the actor asks the seat, passes for it at once or passes for it after a short wait. Every pass takes the path of the seat's own answer (`Running::answer`), so it is checked, logged, saved and projected like a click.

### Windows

`windows::verdict` returns `Ask`, `Pass` or `Dwell` from the step, whose turn it is, the stack, the seat's stops, mode and yield, and `can_act`; auto-pass off is the `Held` mode. A seat that is passed for is never sent the decision, and a decision that is not a priority decision is always asked. The first matching rule wins:

| # | Condition | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full Control (`NextWindow`, `Held`) | Ask at every window |
| 2 | A running yield (a stop of the seat ends it, except in the step it was asked at) | Pass at once |
| 3 | A stop of the seat for this side and step, or a by-hand window | Ask, whether it can act or not |
| 4 | A default window and the seat can act | Ask |
| 5 | A default window and the seat cannot act | `Dwell`; pass at once in its own main phase with an empty stack |
| 6 | Anything else | Pass at once, whatever the seat holds |

The default windows are both main phases on the seat's own turn, declare attackers and declare blockers on any turn, and any step with an object of another seat on the stack. `Own` stops are for the seat's turns, `Opponent` stops for any other seat's.

### Stops and Full Control

- Stops and modes live in the actor per seat (`Stops`), never stored: a reconnect keeps them, a resumed game starts every seat in the default mode with no stop. Setting one takes no engine step, so nothing is saved and the pending decision keeps its id.
- A one-turn stop goes once the game has left its step: a step entered and left in one go counts, one never entered does not. `NextWindow` returns to `Off` when the seat passes, itself or at its clock or dwell.
- A by-hand window: once a spell or ability of the seat whose card has clauses left to the table that a resolution calls for (`CardDefinition.manual`; a clause that is only activated or loyalty abilities is used later and owes nothing, `ManualClause::owed_at_resolution`) resolves, the seat holds a stop at every window until it passes, itself or at its normal clock (`Stops::resolved`, `Stops::by_hand`), so it can apply them; it is never told and reads only public facts.
- Turning Full Control on ends a running yield and `Yield` is refused while it is on; turning it off at a priority decision that the windows then pass for passes at once, as a started yield does.

### Yields

`Yield` and `CancelYield` set and end a per-seat yield (`Yields`), never stored. Its end rules ([protocol.md](protocol.md), Yields) are all in `Active::ends_at` and read the table alone, never `can_act`, so a seat that can respond and one that cannot are passed for and stopped at the same moments. If a started yield ends at once (`Resolve` over an empty stack) the decision is issued again with a new id, so a client that dropped its prompt is never left without one.

### Passes and saves

- An answer, the passes after it and the save are one unit (`publish_with`): copies of the yields and stops, `Running::skip_idle` (the passes, appended to the step), one projection, one append, one batch per seat. The copies replace the seats' own only once saved, so a failed save restores the engine and keeps yields, stops and modes.
- A run of passes stops at the first decision the windows ask or that waits for a dwell, at any other decision, at the game's end and after `MAX_IDLE_PASSES` (500), where the decision is sent as it is and its seat's yield ends with a notice. `start_up` settles a resumed game before any client connects.

### Manual actions

- `ClientMessage::Manual {action}` is accepted at any time from a seat in the game (`running/manual.rs`), whoever the pending decision belongs to and whether or not the windows ask the seat: the actor handles one message at a time, so an action always lands between two engine decisions and never inside a resolution step. A spectator has no seat to send it from; a seat that lost or a finished game gets `Error{BadMessage}`, a refused action `Error{IllegalAnswer}` with the engine's reason, and nothing changes.
- The step is `Engine::manual` published like a concession (`keep_unchanged`): one projection, one append, one batch for every seat, then the passes the windows make. While a replacement question holds a batch (`Engine::replacing`) the action waits in `Running.deferred` and is applied in order after the next command or clock expiry that leaves the engine settled (`flush_deferred`).
- Pending decision: a decision the change leaves equal keeps its id and clock and is not sent again; one whose options changed (a mana source tapped by hand, a target moved) is sent again under a new id, so an answer to the old id is stale and the seat answers the recomputed question. A decision of the same seat and kind keeps the clock it had (`carried_clock`): a manual action never starts, resets or extends a response clock, and the acting seat learns nothing of the other seat's decision.

### Response clocks

- A decision gets its clock when issued (`Running::settle`, `Pace::clock_for`). The rule reads the decision kind and the seat alone, never the options, the hand, the mode, the stops or Full Control, so a clock shows nothing a client does not already hold: a `Priority` decision gets its clock whether the seat can act or not, because a clock that read `can_act` would show what a hand holds. The match names every kind, so a new kind cannot be added without choosing its clock and its answer.
- Four lengths, each `0` for none: `MTG_RESPONSE_SECS` (10 s, `Pace::response`) for what a seat answers at the table; `MTG_BLOCK_SECS` (45 s, `Pace::blocks`) for declaring blockers; `MTG_THINK_SECS` (60 s, `Pace::think`) for what takes thought; `MTG_ACTIVE_SECS` (none, `Pace::active`) for every decision of the active player's turn, whatever its kind: priority, targets, attackers, discarding, payments, damage and the questions of effects. `Class::of` names the class of every kind (an exhaustive match, so a new kind cannot be added without a rule); a seat that is not the active player is hurried by the class, the active seat by `active`, and the opening (keep or mulligan, bottoming), asked before any turn exists, by `think` for every seat. With `MTG_ACTIVE_SECS=0` the active player's decisions have no clock.
- The countdown shows after `MTG_CLOCK_GRACE_SECS` (3 s, `Pace::grace`, `0` shows at once): the clock runs from when the decision is issued, and `Decision.show_after_ms` tells a client how long to wait before it shows the countdown. It is absent when the countdown shows at once, also for a clock no longer than the grace. `Pace::shown_after` reads the length alone, which follows the kind and the seat, so it shows nothing the clock does not; a reconnect is told what is left of the delay.
- The timer bank (`bank.rs`). A seat that finishes three turns of its own in a row without a clock reaching its deadline earns one extension, at most three (`BANK_TURNS`, `BANK_MAX` in `pace.rs`); a clock that reaches its deadline, on any turn, starts the run again, and the turn it ran out in does not count. At the deadline of a clock that was sent, a seat holding an extension spends one instead of being answered for (`Running::extend`): the deadline moves by `MTG_EXTENSION_SECS` (20 s, `Pace::extension`; `0` turns the bank off), once per extension, the seat is sent the same decision again with the same id, the new `deadline_ms` and no `show_after_ms`, and every seat is sent `Bank { seat, bank }`. Only an empty bank lets `Running::fall_back` answer. Answering in time keeps the bank; a decision held back for a dwell spends nothing. The bank is public: `PlayerView.bank` carries it for every seat in a snapshot and in the table of every event batch (the actor stamps it, because the engine does not know it). It lives in the running game's memory and is not stored: a resumed game starts every bank empty, like its yields and stops.
- The clock ignores the mode, runs while the seat is away, stops with any answer and restarts when a game is resumed. `deadline_ms` carries the time left ([protocol.md](protocol.md), Decisions); a reconnect is told what is left and the deadline does not move.
- At the deadline the actor answers with `Running::fall_back` through `Running::answer`: the answer is checked, saved and sent like the seat's own, a late answer is the ordinary `StaleDecision`, and the table logs at info, once per answer, `clock ran out; the table answered for the seat` (or `clock ran out; an extension was spent` with the bank left, when the clock was extended instead) with the game, the seat and the decision kind (never a card). The loop selects between the next command and the pending `due` time, commands first, because a command already waiting was sent before the clock ran out. A failed save restores the engine and retries a second later. A decision with no answer to give (a choice with no option or mode, a damage with no blocker to take it, a payment only the player can make) stops its clock and waits.
- The answer is the one that decides least and shows nothing: a pass, keeping, backing out of a cast at no cost, the order the engine gave. It reads the kind and its options alone, never the seat:

| Decision | Clock | Answer at the deadline |
|---|---|---|
| `Mulligan` | think, every seat | Keep. |
| `BottomCards` | think, every seat | The first `count` cards of the hand as offered. |
| `Priority` | response | Pass. |
| `PayMana` | think | `Cancel` when `can_cancel` (the cast is taken back); else the engine's own payment (`Auto`); none when neither exists. |
| `DeclareAttackers` | think (active seat: active) | Only the attackers a rule requires (`AttackOptions::required_attack`; none when none must attack). |
| `DeclareBlockers` | blocks | Only the blocks a rule requires (`BlockOptions::required_blocks`; none while no creature must block). |
| `AssignDamage` | think | Lethal damage to each blocker in the order offered, the rest to the last blocker (kept for the player attacked when trampling). |
| `Discard` | think (active seat: active) | The first `count` cards of the hand as offered. |
| `ChooseTargets` | response | No target: `Cancel` for a spell, which the rules take back at no cost; `Remove` for a triggered ability, which they take off the stack when its target cannot be chosen. A seat's own `Remove` is refused with `IllegalAnswer`: a player with a legal target has to choose one. |
| `OrderTriggers` | response | The triggers in the order they triggered. |
| `ChooseReplacement` | response | The first effect on offer. |
| `YesNo` | response | No, also when the payer cannot pay (`only_no`, which the clock never reads). |
| `ChooseOption` | response | The first option (for the damage a shield takes first, the first event on offer). |
| `ChooseMode` | response | `Cancel` when `can_cancel` allows backing out of the cast, else the first mode (the first `min` modes when the choice needs more, the first repeated when the list is shorter and a mode may repeat). A mode is named by its `index`, not its place in the list. |
| `ChooseNumber` | response | `Cancel` when `can_cancel`, else the lowest number. |
| `ChooseCosts` | response | `Cancel` when `can_cancel`, else no optional cost. |
| `DivideAmount` | response | `Cancel` when `can_cancel`, else an even division (one at least to each target, the first getting the rest; none when the amount is smaller than the number of targets). |
| `ChooseCards` | response | The first `min` cards of `from` in the order offered (none for a `min` of 0, all of them when fewer are offered; never more than the most). |
| `OrderCards` | response | The cards in the order given. |

"Response", "blocks" and "think" clocks hurry a seat that is not the active player; the active player's decisions run on "active" (none by default) with the same answers; the opening decisions run on "think" for every seat.

### Dwell

- A seat that cannot act at a default window, other than its own main phase with an empty stack, and holds no stop, Full Control or yield is neither asked nor passed for at once, because a pass at once shows that it holds nothing. The windows are declare attackers and declare blockers, any window with an object of another seat on the stack (a spell, or an ability: a triggered ability is an object of its controller, the seat it was put on the stack for), and its own main phase with any object on the stack.
- The decision is held back (`Running::settle`): it is never sent, a join or `Resync` sends the snapshot alone and an answer for it is stale. At its `due` time `expire` passes through `fall_back` as a step, an append and a batch of its own, so every other seat sees the stream of a seat that could act and passed.
- The wait is uniform between 250 and 700 ms (`MTG_DWELL_MS`), drawn from a generator the operating system seeds, independent of the game seed and the state; every idle seat draws its own, so no wait depends on the seats before it. It is wall clock only: the log, event order and state hash are those of a game without a dwell. With `Dwell::OFF` (also `Pace::MANUAL`) `Dwell` is a pass at once.
- A stop at the window, Full Control or auto-pass off ends the wait and sends the decision at once; a yield passes at once; a concession that removes the seat ends it. Chat, `Resync`, a stale answer, a stop elsewhere and other seats' commands leave it.
- It hides whether a seat could act in a combat window or while a spell waits on the stack, in the batches and arrival times every other seat sees. It does not hide a seat's own main phase with an empty stack (what is played there is in plain sight, so a seat that can act is asked and one that cannot is passed at once), how long a seat that can act takes (a pause past 700 ms or a pass before 250 ms is its own), a pass at a response-clock deadline, or a choice of stops, Full Control or yield. A step with N idle seats costs N dwells, 475 ms on average and 700 at most each.

## Previews

The actor answers `Preview` itself (`preview.rs`) with one `Forecast`; `Engine::preview_forecast` judges the answer for the seat's pending decision. Everything else gives `forecast: null` and never an `Error`, because the stale-decision treatment of `Answer` would raise a notice and a resync in a client whose preview raced its real answer. A token bucket per seat (`PREVIEW_BURST` 20, then `PREVIEWS_PER_SECOND` 10) lives as long as the game, so a reconnect does not refill it; a preview over the limit gets a null forecast without touching the engine. Answers have no bucket, and a preview costs one state clone, so a flood cannot starve an answer.

| `MTG_SEED` | random | A whole number (`u64`) every game shuffles with instead of a random seed, so a game replays exactly; each game logs its seed (`game created`). For tests and replays, not production. |
| `DATABASE_URL` | unset | Postgres URL, never logged. Set, the binary connects (10 connections), migrates and calls `Server::open`; unset or blank, it warns and keeps games in memory without accounts. `.cargo/config.toml` defaults it to the local database (`docker compose up -d db`). |
| `MTG_IMAGE_CACHE` | `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/mtg-server/images`, else `~/.cache/mtg-server/images` | Directory of the picture cache ([Pictures](#pictures)). |
| `MTG_IMAGE_CACHE_MIB` | 2048 | Size limit of the picture cache in MiB, 1 to 1048576. |
| `RUST_LOG` | `info` | Log filter. |

`ServerConfig` holds more values (queues, socket limits, retention, hash cost) that only tests set.

## Testing

- Actor tests drive games through `GameHandle` with bot and manual clients (`tests/it/common`) and compare the log, state hash and result with the same game on a bare engine (`mtg-testkit`); `roomy()` runs under `Pace::MANUAL`, `windowed()` under the default windows alone.
- `Windows` in `mtg-testkit` is the window rule written plainly and apart from the server; `tests/it/windows.rs` compares the actor with it for 2 to 4 seats, with stops and modes the bots choose.
- Clocks, the dwell and the leak test run on tokio's paused time; `tests/it/pace_leak.rs` plays two games that differ by one hidden card, in combat windows and with a spell or a triggered ability on the stack, and requires the other seat to be sent the same messages in the same batches; `tests/it/pay_leak.rs` plays two games that differ in what a spell asks the opponent to pay (one can pay it, one cannot) and requires the same messages at the same moments, the same question and clock for the payer, a refused Yes that no other seat sees, and No from the clock; `tests/it/response_clock_effects.rs` covers the clocks of the target and order decisions through the actor. `tests/it/question_clocks.rs` is a table over the questions of effects and the opening (yes or no, option, mode, number, division, payment, a spell's targets, keeping, bottoming): for each, the clock carries its length from the moment the question is sent, an answer in time is never answered again, the deadline plays the documented fallback once (logged, saved like the seat's own, a late answer stale), a reconnect is told the time left and the deadline stays, and every other seat is sent the same messages as when the player answers with the fallback itself. `tests/it/timer_bank.rs` plays 2, 3 and 4 seats on the paused clock: an extension earned after exactly three own turns, spent at the deadline (the deadline moved, the decision sent again, the bank told to every seat), a bank of three spent three times before the table answers, a failed save earning nothing, a reconnect told the time and the bank left, a resumed game with empty banks, the grace on the wire, the active seat's clock and the same clock fields for a seat that can act and one that cannot. The clocks and fallbacks of every kind, seat, setting and shape of options are tables in `pace.rs`; each kind has an actor case or a table row.
